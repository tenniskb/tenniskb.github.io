67 - Fundamental -- 1-2 Rhythm: The Backhand Slice - Nick Wheatley

By Henry Phạm Đức · Tennis For Everyone (2025-03-31) · Bản gốc Blogspot ↗

 

1-2 nhịp điệu: The cú trái tay (backhand) cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice)

Nick Wheatley


What are the keys in developing 1-2 nhịp điệu on the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice)?

Like all the groundstrokes, a well struck cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú trái tay (backhand) cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) has 1-2 nhịp điệu. However, there are real differences in the feeling of executing 1-2 nhịp điệu on the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) compared to the drive groundstrokes.

These differences are especially important to understand for players who hit the one-handed cú trái tay (backhand) drive. (Click Here for 1-2 nhịp điệu on the cú thuận tay (forehand). Click Here for the two-hander. And Click Here for the one handed cú trái tay (backhand) drive.)

The differences in 1-2 nhịp điệu on the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) have to do in part with the shape of the swing. But the most important differences are in the relative role of the body and legs.

There is some body rotation on the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice), but it is minimal compared to the cú thuận tay (forehand) or the two hander, and also appreciably less than the one-handed drive. The same is true of the uncoiling of the legs. There is noticeably less knee bend on most slices compared to the other groundstrokes.

For these reasons, establishing 1-2 nhịp điệu on the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) means focusing on the hand, arm and racket--specifically the movement of the arm in the shoulder in the pha vung vợt về phía trước. This is especially critical to understand for one handers who go back and forth from the drive to the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice).



Note the differences between the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) and the drive in the knee bend and forward body rotation.

Despite technical differences the same two distinct nhịp điệu phases are essential in the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice). As with the other strokes, Phase 1 is smooth and deliberate, and Phase 2 has explosive energy.

So let's look at the physical positions of the body and the racket in the two phases of the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice). Then let's see how to activate 1-2 nhịp điệu. To do this, we'll analyze the greatest cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú trái tay (backhand) in the modern game--the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú trái tay (backhand) of Roger Federer.

Like all high-level groundstrokes, the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) begins with a body turn. When the shoulders have turned about 45 degrees to the net, the racket hand is still positioned in line with the center of the torso.

The racket position for Federer is at this point virtually identical with the drive, which facilitates disguise. But look what happens next.

Watch the continued path of the racket in the preparation and then the path of the pha vung vợt về phía trước.



Phase1: slow and deliberate with the racket wrapped behind the head.

As the racket continues to go back it actually wraps behind the head. The face of the racket is slightly open and the tip points behind Roger to the opposite sideline. The left hand is positioned on the racket's throat.

It's important to note that the extent of this wrap can vary with the player. It can also be less extreme when the player is returning giao bóng. As we will see, players at lower levels may also be more successful with this less extreme preparation and also a flatter pha vung vợt về phía trước plane.

In any case, the execution of Phase 1 has the same feeling as Phase 1 on all strokes. The technical position is different but the nhịp điệu to get there is smooth and deliberate.

Phase 2 in the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) starts with the pha vung vợt về phía trước from the wrap position around the head. The tay cầm vợt đánh bóng and racket accelerate along a high to low swing path towards the ball, with the tay cầm vợt đánh bóng straightening out as it approaches contact.

The explosiveness in Phase 2 is primarily in the movement of the arm and racket. This is different from the other strokes where Phase 2 includes significant energy from the uncoiling of the legs and the rotation of torso. The driving force here is the shoulder, specifically, using the shoulder muscles to drive the arm and racket down and through.



Phase 2: Explosive acceleration forward and down.

Watch how the opposite hand releases and stays behind the body to help maintain balance, and also the sideways orientation of the body.

It's important to emphasize the energy explosion from the shoulder because for too many players the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) is viewed as a passive shot. Many players lose potential quality of shot because they poke, jab, or push at the ball instead of hitting it with the force it warrants.

Studies have shown that the spin levels on a cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú trái tay (backhand) at the pro level can surpass that of the độ xoáy cuộn (topspin) groundstrokes, reaching over 5000rpm. (Click Here.) By thinking of the pha vung vợt về phía trước as very aggressive and full of energy, players can get a lot more out of their cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú trái tay (backhand).

A signature relationship in the pro cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) is between the height of the hand and the racket head. With this explosive downward swing path, the racket head can stay below the hand for the entire course of the pha vung vợt theo đà (follow-through) with the racket tip actually pointing straight down at the court at some point in the swing.



The emphasis is on the shoulder driving the hand, racket and arm.

But not all players--or even most--are dealing with pro level heavy incoming balls much less replying with slices with 5000rpm of spin. This means there can be a disconnect between the pro cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) and the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) for lower-level players. (Click Here.)

Players who blindly copy the extreme swing shapes of Federer's cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) may find they are leaving velocity on the table and hitting floating balls that are not as effective or penetrating as they could be. In this case the swing shape can be moderated.

The height and the wrap behind the head can be reduced. The downward swing plane can be flatter and less sharp. One indication of a flatter cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) is that the racket head stays above the wrist in the pha vung vợt theo đà (follow-through).



Depending on the level, the positions associated with 1-2 nhịp điệu can be less extreme.

The bottom line is that every player needs to find the level of underspin and the corresponding swing plane that works for his game and the incoming balls he faces. But learning the 1-2 nhịp điệu pattern will maximize its efficiency and effectiveness. What are the keys for 1-2 nhịp điệu on the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice)? Again, it is some combination of mental images and words.

It can be a count of "1" then "2" corresponding with the speed of the phases. The "2" should be associated with the acceleration of the arm from the shoulder.

Another combination would be "Smooth" or "Slow" followed by "Extend!" The second word again correlating with the downward and outward movement of the tay cầm vợt đánh bóng. If you develop a feeling for how it works, you will likely come up with individual words or pictures of your own.

So that's it for the concept of developing great nhịp điệu on your groundstroke--in all varieties. What about cú giao bóng? Does 1-2 nhịp điệu apply there as well? Yes! Stay tuned.
