Xây Dựng Cú Volley Bất Bại Phần 2: Hai Phong Cách Cốt Lõi - The Prodigy Maker

By Chris Lewit · The Prodigy Maker (2020-05-03) · Original Article ↗

When I look at elite cú bắt lưới (volley) technique, I see a fundamental bifurcation – đánh bóng bạt (flat) style and cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) style. Sometimes players use a combination of both depending on the tactical situation.

I call the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) style the “Israeli style” because the nuances were first taught to me by my coach Gilad Bloom, the former Israeli Davis Cup player. In this article let me outline the technical parameters I use when building both invincible đánh bóng bạt (flat) and cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) volleys.

The cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) style and the đánh bóng bạt (flat) style on both sides.

đánh bóng bạt (flat) Style and Israeli Style Compared

Both styles of cú bắt lưới (volley) are good and valid and both can be used effectively at the high levels of the game. In fact, most players may benefit from learning both styles and I teach both to my students.

The đánh bóng bạt (flat) style cú bắt lưới (volley) is simple and practical. The Israeli style is elegant and more complex.

Many times, I will advise my students to cú bắt lưới (volley) the ball more Israeli style on sharp angle, transitional, low, or slower balls and to use the đánh bóng bạt (flat) style on reflex and fast balls or power put away volleys.

Max Mirni: a great example of đánh bóng bạt (flat) style.

For this reason, you often see the đánh bóng bạt (flat) style in great doubles players who have to play a lot of reflex and power volleys. Think Max Mirnyi or the Bryan brothers.

Singles players who venture to net have more time and opportunity to play cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) and xoáy ngang (sidespin) volleys, although you will also see some spin volleys in doubles on slow balls and angle/drop shots and transition first volleys.

In general, you typically see more cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) on cú trái tay (backhand) volleys, but the Israeli style taught to me emphasizes cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) on the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley) as well. That’s a primary difference.

I explain to my students that there are two different ways to prepare the racquet and two different effects on the ball – đánh bóng bạt (flat) or cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice).

But I also introduce the concept of a third effect – xoáy ngang (sidespin). Bottom line is that I want my players to choose the desired effect and technique to use based on the tactical situation and their intention.

The cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) volleys are prepared with the racket face open.

Preparation

The cú thuận tay (forehand) and cú trái tay (backhand) cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú bắt lưới (volley) is prepared with the racquet face very open – it almost looks too open – and entails a more extreme downward flight path of the racquet to the incoming ball, to compensate for the extremely open racquet face.

The flatter the cú bắt lưới (volley), the more the racquet face is prepared closer to vertical or slightly open. The primary focus is to get the hand and racquet face behind the incoming ball – “square the racquet face,” as coaches say – and drive through the ball with good extension through the impact.

Toni Nadal calls this extension through the ball “the accompaniment.” It’s critical to extend through impact – to accompany the ball well – if you want to get a good response and good control of the cú bắt lưới (volley). This is true of any cú bắt lưới (volley), except perhaps extreme angle and cut volleys, which are sharply grazed.

As the name implies, the đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú bắt lưới (volley) typically has little if rotation (spin) on the ball. It is a simple, straight shot.

The đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú bắt lưới (volley) are shape is more of a U. The cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú bắt lưới (volley) more of a V.

The cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú bắt lưới (volley) is differentiated by higher levels of RPM, typically backspin and sometimes xoáy ngang (sidespin) – which can make the ball skid, die out, and curve.

In general, the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) has been shown to have higher levels of backspin than the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley), but with the Israeli technique, the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley) is a lot slicier and can sometimes reach RPM levels similar to the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) spin rates.

The đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú bắt lưới (volley) style shows the ubiquitous U-shape preparation, which John Yandell has analyzed in detail. (See his cú bắt lưới (volley) articles in the Classic Lessons this month.) The cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley) style generally has more of a wider angled V shape preparation of the racquet, wrist, and arm. On the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) the U shape preparations is similar in both styles.

Grip

The đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú bắt lưới (volley) uses a stronger cú thuận tay (forehand) grip, in between Continental and Eastern or weaker Continental. Some đánh bóng bạt (flat) style volleyers have a small grip change between the cú thuận tay (forehand) and cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley).

The less Continental the grip, the flatter the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley). The more continental the grip, the flatter the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley).

The Israeli cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) grip is an extreme continental.

To say it another way: moving more towards the cú trái tay (backhand) grip equals higher spin rates on the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley). More towards the cú thuận tay (forehand) grip equals higher spin rates on the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley).

The Israeli cú bắt lưới (volley) typically uses an extreme continental, closer to a cú trái tay (backhand) grip, which helps impart more spin on the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley). The grips used will affect RPM rates on the cú bắt lưới (volley) but ultimately the swing path and racquet face angle will be more determinant of whether the ball is struck đánh bóng bạt (flat) or with cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice).

Swing Path

The swing path for a đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú bắt lưới (volley) is slightly high to low or linear, depending on the height of the ball. Sometimes players will even stab at the ball with subtle độ xoáy cuộn (topspin) on stretch volleys the way the great Patrick Rafter used to do.

Sometimes flatter volleys will use slight độ xoáy cuộn (topspin) on stretch volleys.

Israeli technique on the cú bắt lưới (volley) dictates an extreme open racquet face and sharp downward slope of the swing path to generate higher cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) RPM rates. On low balls the downward swing path is closer to linear.

In general, a sharper downward swing path is required because of the more open racquet face. Without enough downward swing path, the open racquet face, like a sand wedge in golf, will pop the ball up.

On stretch, this style of cú bắt lưới (volley) will “cut” the ball with backspin and xoáy ngang (sidespin) rather than the độ xoáy cuộn (topspin) stretch cú bắt lưới (volley) style of Rafter. On drop volleys with heavy backspin – the most extreme version of cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) volleys – the swing path is near đánh bóng bạt (flat) linear with a very open racquet face.

Shoulder Turn

The shoulder turn for the đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley) is slightly less extreme than with the Israeli cú bắt lưới (volley). The grip on the Israeli cú bắt lưới (volley) dictates a more extreme shoulder turn to comfortably prepare the racquet face on the cú thuận tay (forehand) side.

The Israeli cú bắt lưới (volley) have a sharper downward swing path.

This is one reason that I favor a flatter cú bắt lưới (volley) style on fast incoming balls. Not only is it simpler to execute under duress, but the skill requires less shoulder turn and less time.

The cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú bắt lưới (volley) can be hit tư thế đứng mở (open stance) and open shoulders, but it’s more difficult to execute and you have to lay the wrist back in an extreme way. And you hit the ball with a slightly weaker hand position.

bộ chân di chuyển

On almost all volleys, it is important to step forward aggressively and generally try to make contact while the front foot is still in the air. While some situations will dictate that the front foot strikes the ground before or at the same time as the impact with the ball, I always want my students to attempt a power lunge step and impact the ball with the front foot midflight.

I tell my students to “synchronize” their power step with their arm and hand movement forward.

Two Hands Or One?

There is much current debate about whether players should start with two hands or one hand on the cú bắt lưới (volley) and whether a two handed cú bắt lưới (volley) is viable at the pro level.

On the đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley) the shoulder turn is less extreme.

I usually encourage single handed volleys, but I always have an open mind and let some players experiment with two if they feel more comfortable.

I always encourage two hands for cú trái tay (backhand) half cú bắt lưới (volley) (for two handed cú trái tay (backhand) players) and reflex volleys, when the ball is coming at high speed. You see this a lot in modern world class doubles. I would not discourage two hands in these situations.

If I have a kid with two hands on regular volleys, that’s fine with me, but I insist he or she learn one handed options when stretched wide for better reach.

The Finish

The đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley) typically has a more vertical racquet face through impact and finish, although John Yandell has demonstrated the that the wrist and racquet face can sometimes open up to the sky after impact, a movement probably dependent on the speed, spin, and angle of impact of the incoming ball.

Impact the ball with the front foot in mid flight.

However, the cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú bắt lưới (volley) – already more open at the start – is almost always finished with a rotation of the wrist and a subsequent opening of the racquet face to the sky.

The Israeli style emphasizes slightly more sideways chest position at impact and at the completion of the stroke. The đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú bắt lưới (volley) tends to be more open with the shoulders at finish.

The cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) cú bắt lưới (volley) is almost always finished with wrist rotation, opening the racket face to the sky.

Final Thoughts

I encourage readers to experiment with both cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) and đánh bóng bạt (flat) cú bắt lưới (volley) techniques. I teach my players variations of both for a more complete cú bắt lưới (volley) repertoire.

For example, players can be taught to “cut” drop shots and angle shots, and to cú cắt xoáy ngược (slice) approach volleys, while at the same time learning straight đánh bóng bạt (flat) volleys for reaction and power shots. Players should know how to apply different effects of spin on the ball when volleying, including xoáy ngang (sidespin).

High level volleying is not as simple as a mere đánh bóng bạt (flat) block!
