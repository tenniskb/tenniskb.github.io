77 - Tennis Backhand Volley: Ultimate Guide from Tennis Nation

By Henry Phạm Đức · Tennis For Everyone (2026-02-12) · Bản gốc Blogspot ↗

 Tennis cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley)

This article explains the differences in the tennis cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) compared to the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley). 

Tennis cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley): tay không thuận

The tennis cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) is very similar to the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley) with a few slight differences. Due to having your non-dominate hand grasping the racquet longer on the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) than on the cú thuận tay (forehand) cú bắt lưới (volley), you should know how to use the tay không thuận to your advantage. Many players will grasp the throat of the racquet with their non-dominate hand and utilize it for racquet stability. The tay không thuận can help support the racquet head, help guide the racquet head back slightly to open up the racquet face, and act as a wall to ensure the racquet doesn’t go too far back.

For example, I like to hold the throat of my racquet with my tay không thuận and lift my non-dominant elbow up away from my body creating the wall that prevents my racquet from going further back. Other players keep both their elbows down out in front of their body and inside shoulder width so that the body prevents the racquet from being taken back further. Both ways are equally effective, it boils down to individual preference.

cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) Technique: Staying on Balance

Once the racquet begins to swing forward to contact the ball the tay không thuận releases from the throat of the racquet and travels the opposite direction to help your shoulders stay turned sideways and to help keep your body on balance.

Tennis cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley): Strength and Comfort

Players that are younger or do not feel in control or lack the strength to hit the cú trái tay (backhand) effectively with the one handed cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) may choose to hit the cú bắt lưới (volley) with two hands on the grip. The hitting hand will still hold the cầm vợt số 2 (Continental) and the tay không thuận will grasp the grip similarly to what is described in he two-handed cú trái tay (backhand) grip section.

The two-handed cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) provides more support and can help players gain confidence on the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley). However, the player sacrifices reach when volleying with two hands and in most cases should be transitioned to the one handed cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) when capable.

Tennis cú bắt lưới (volley) Frustrations?

Have you ever felt clueless about what to do when the ball is hit directly at your body? I can remember practicing as a junior and getting frustrated as my friend aimed for my hips on the volleys. I would step backwards, hop out of the way, or do a curtsy as an attempt to try and find space to hit a proper cú bắt lưới (volley). I also wanted to hit a great ball instead of having patience and learning the proper way to cú bắt lưới (volley) the ball when it was hit right at me.

There will be times when you don’t have time to take the steps necessary to set up for a traditional cú bắt lưới (volley). This often happens when two players are up at net volleying agressively or the opponent drills the ball with pace right at you. When this happens hitting the cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley) is typically the best option.

Tennis cú trái tay (backhand) cú bắt lưới (volley): Returning the Ball Hit at Your Body

From the ready position you will bring your hitting elbow up away from your body so that your racquet face is facing the net and out in front of your body’s mid section. From there you want to drive your racquet through the tennis ball with your shoulder, time permitting.

What I have found that helps with volleying balls hit around your hips and you have a little bit of time is taking a step diagonally forward but away from the ball with your inside foot. By taking a diagonal step away from the ball you will be able to create more space and hopefully hit a more formal cú bắt lưới (volley).

Closing Thoughts

Tennis cú trái tay (backhand) volleys are difficult especially when the ball is hit around your hips.  To develop comfort volleying balls around your mid section takes a LOT of practice. Enjoy the process and have patience.
