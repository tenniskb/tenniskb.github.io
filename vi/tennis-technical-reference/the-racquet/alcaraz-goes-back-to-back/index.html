Alcaraz goes back to back

Published: 2022-05-08 · By Matt Lin / The vợt · Original Post ↗

Alcaraz becomes the first player to beat Nadal and Djokovic at the same clay tournament. And in stunning fashion. The last 48 hours have launched a million opinions about what this means, initiating both an under and over-hype feedback loop. While it’s true that these versions of Nadal and Djokovic weren’t exactly vintage, it doesn’t really matter. Those two legends have dovetailed so effectively even into their mid-30’s that when one loses the other invariably picks up the slack, the latter of the two usually feasting on an opponent drained from the task of beating one of them. In this case though, Alcaraz backed up the biggest win of his career with another, immediately. Lesser players would have fallen at one of the hurdles regardless of Nadal and Djokovic’s hình thức. It’s unbelievably impressive no matter which way you spin it.

So how did he do it?

Offence

First of all, one of the most interesting things about Alcaraz’s emergence on tour has been his tấn công potential and execution, coupled with his quite small stature in relation to the ‘fast giant’ playstyles (Medvedev, Zverev, Hurkacz, Tsitsipas et al) that the previous generation display (although he does generally help my theory that trả bóng performance may be a bigger edge right now than giao bóng performance).

Across his last two matches Alcaraz hit 88(!) winners (only 8 were aces) to Nadal and Djokovic’s combined 34. That is an absurd display of power against the two players have have historically been two of the greatest movers ever, and who have routinely exploited offence-leaning opponents.

Droppers and volleys

As I’ve written about a bunch already, Alcaraz posted some more excellent cúp numbers, winning 15/20 cúp points overall, and winning 8/11 of them on his thuận tay side (and 5/9 on trái tay).

Alcaraz also won 24/26 lưới points, including 12/13 giao bóng-and-bắt lưới points.

It’s safe to say that Alcaraz had absolutely no shortage of ways to win points against either Nadal or Djokovic.

AD AD AD

But the most interesting bit of performance in the Alcaraz win over Djokovic came on Alcaraz’s giao bóng.

Djokovic talked about this in press after the trận đấu:

A week or so ago I answered one of the questions in the monthly Q&A, about left-handed advantages, with this:

The lợi thế of being a lefty in tennis is often overstated at the pro and elite levels. But there is an interesting lợi thế when it comes to a particular bit of performance: AD side serving. Right handed players statistically perform better serving to the đều side and lefty players statistically perform better serving to the AD side. Anyone who’s watched lefties hit their slider serves out rộng on the AD side will have a decent understanding of why. But that AD side also happens to be where many áp lực (influential) points happen, including break points at 30-40 and đều-AD. Lefties have about a 2% performance edge on average over righties on those, often crucial or influential, break points on that AD side.

But Alcaraz (a right hander), almost entirely thanks to his excellent kick giao bóng, enjoyed a more important serving edge on the AD than the đều side against Djokovic.

Alcaraz giao bóng Placement vs Djokovic:

Most of Alcaraz’s serves went to that rộng spot on the AD side:

Alcaraz won a slightly ridiculous 85% of those rộng AD serves on 1st giao bóng and a still impressive 57% of them on 2nd serves. As I mentioned above, one of the good things about being a lefty is that increased AD side serving performance, because it often has an effect on important or influential points — 15-30, 30-40, and all đều-AD’s. And guess what kept Alcaraz in the trận đấu time after time when under áp lực?

Break points faced:

  • 30-40 1*-0 1st set: 2nd giao bóng, AD kick rộng giao bóng — unreturned WON

  • 30-40, 3*-2 1st set: 2nd giao bóng AD kick rộng — inside in thuận tay +1 WON

  • 30-40, 4*-3 1st set: 2nd giao bóng AD kick rộng — đánh qua lại with trái tay UE LOST

  • 30-40, 4-4 2nd set: 1st giao bóng AD kick rộng — unreturned WON

  • đều-AD, 5-5, 2nd set: 2nd giao bóng AD kick rộng — đánh qua lại w/ cúp WON

  • đều-AD, 3-3, 3rd set: 1st giao bóng AD kick rộng — unreturned WON

That’s 3 unreturned rộng kick serves to the AD side, two giao bóng+1 winners on the back of kick rộng serves to the AD side, and one trái tay lỗi tự phát. Or 5/6 free, cheap points under áp lực. That rộng kick giao bóng featured on every single điểm break, and saved Alcaraz’s bacon 5/6 times. (Also only 2/6 1st serves landed down break points, but that’s one of the nice things about having a good kick giao bóng, that it can still be quite an chủ động tấn công giao bóng while remaining safe with its high lưới clearance.)

The reason that rộng giao bóng was so effective came down to two things.

First of all, as Djokovic mentioned, the high bounce in Madrid coupled with Alcaraz’s spin, produced difficult contact points high into Novak’s trái tay:

The other problem for Djokovic, and one of the reasons he missed long quite a bit on that AD side trái tay trả bóng off the Alcaraz kick giao bóng, is that if he doesn’t get good penetration on that trả bóng, Alcaraz’s giao bóng +1 thuận tay will have an easy put away trong-vào trong:

This fine thăng bằng is something that Djokovic simply couldn’t manage yesterday. And it was a significant reason why Alcaraz got the win: finding repeatable, winning service patterns on that AD side on influential points:

On the đều side, Djokovic was far more comfortable dealing with Alcaraz’s cắt serves out rộng partly because they came through at an easier height for him to deal with:

And this dynamic played out again in the crucial, decisive 3rd set tiebreaker that secured Alcaraz the win.

Final set tie-break, Alcaraz service points (AD points are in bold):

  • Wins đều giao bóng at 0-0

  • Wins AD giao bóng at 2*-1 — kick rộng giao bóng into 1-2 punch điểm thắng

  • Loses đều point at 3*-1

  • Wins AD point at 4*-3 — kick rộng giao bóng unreturned

  • Loses đều point at 5*-3

  • Wins AD point (điểm trận) at 6*-5 — Kick rộng giao bóng thuận tay trong-vào trong +1 điểm thắng

Alcaraz won all three AD service points with kick serves out rộng in the tie-break. He lost 2/3 đều service points:

An absolutely mammoth serving performance to the AD side from Alcaraz.

Quick add ons

Djokovic and Alcaraz were extremely even when it came to point length performance, to be expected in this matchup and in such a close trận đấu:

  • 0-4 shots: Alcaraz 80, Djokovic 79

  • 5-8 shots: Alcaraz 40, Djokovic 37

  • 9+ shots: Alcaraz 12, Djokovic 12

But partially because of that AD rộng giao bóng from Alcaraz, he enjoyed a marginally easier day on giao bóng than Djokovic:

Average đánh qua lại length on giao bóng:

  • Alcaraz: 4 shots

  • Djokovic: 5 shots

1 shot might not seem like much but over the course of a three and a half hour trận đấu it can be significant exertion-wise.

Moats

All of this combines to mean that, most of all on clay with its higher bounce, Alcaraz actually has an interesting performance quirk in that his AD side giao bóng performance is likely going to be better than his đều side giao bóng performance (I’ll post numbers at the end of the season). But as we’ve already seen with his going-against-the-grain split of more thuận tay drop shots than trái tay, there are a few unusual things about this 19 year old.

Alcaraz now plays Zverev for the title in a few hours (Zverev also may not offer the same trả bóng weakness on that AD side either thanks to his height). But even without topping this week off with the cherry of a Madrid open trophy, he’s done what no one else could do in beating the two toughest outs in tennis back-to-back on clay. Nadal said it best a few days ago:

“When you're young, when you are very good, the process is faster than the normal people. So he (Alcaraz) is not a normal guy, like Novak was not a normal guy, like Roger was not a normal guy, probably like I was not a normal guy.”

Comparisons with the Big 3 of Federer, Nadal and Djokovic are usually boring and irritating for their fans. But when it comes from one of the Big 3 themselves, it’s usually worth taking notice. Rather obviously Alcaraz still has a long and brutal climb to put himself anywhere near those three players, but for now he’s doing all that is asked of a player his age — he’s beating the guys perched above him. And it just isn’t his concern that they’re older and more beat up than they once were. Nadal and Djokovic will both take positives from Madrid as they aim to find their usual peak hình thức for Roland Garros (both actually looked thrilled for their young usurper despite losing). But as is becoming a recurring theme in 2022, this has been Alcaraz’s week.

‘Not normal’ indeed.
