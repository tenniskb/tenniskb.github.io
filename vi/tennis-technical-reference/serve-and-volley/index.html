Roger Federer strikes a bắt lưới at the Wimbledon Championships. Photo courtesy of Scott Heavey/AELTC. Tennis has come a long way over the years. Since sân surfaces changed, vợt technology evolved, and nutrition and fitness improved, tennis modernized.

Today’s players are fitter, stronger and faster than their 20th century counterparts. A chiến thuật that many players practically left for dead is serving and volleying. This maneuver consists of racing up to the lưới after hitting a giao bóng to strike the next shot before it bounces (hitting a ball out of the air is called a bắt lưới). From close to the lưới, a few angles open up that are not conceivable from the đường cuối sân (where a player serves from to start a point), some 39 feet back from the lưới.

The problem is that successfully serving and volleying is somewhat contingent on the trả bóng being weak — a bullet near either đường biên dọc or low enough to lực an awkward bắt lưới can rarely be poked away easily to end a point. With today’s rackets, it is far easier to hit returns with sharp angles or extreme pace than it was 30 or 40 years ago, making serving and volleying a risky proposition.

Not only that, in the late 1900s grass courts were notoriously uneven, resulting in many bad bounces that giao bóng-and-bắt lưới became a viable chiến lược almost out of necessity: giao lại the ball bounce at your own peril. giao bóng-and-bắt lưới is used sporadically in today’s game. But if a returner sees a server race up to the lưới, they are often able to pick a target and rip a trả bóng loaded with xoáy trên that is nearly impossible to control with a bắt lưới. There are many tennis traditionalists out there who believe giao bóng-and-bắt lưới is still a viable chiến thuật, beyond just coming to the lưới off good serves or at unexpected times. On Sunday, world number one Novak Djokovic served and volleyed 22 times in the final of the Rolex Paris Masters tournament, winning 19 points for his endeavors: a phenomenal success rate of 86%. Djokovic would win the trận đấu 4–6, 6–3, 6–3, with his giao bóng-and-volleying a big reason for his victory.

Craig O’Shannessy, a prominent chiến lược coach who writes analytical pieces for the ATP Tour website among other well-known publications, wrote an article after the final that argued that “the death of giao bóng and bắt lưới in our sport is pure misconception” and referred to giao bóng-and-bắt lưới’s decreased use as a “delusional fallacy.” O’Shannessy knows tennis, and there is no denying that Djokovic’s use of lưới play in the Paris final was a pivotal chiến thuật.

But to draw the conclusion that serving and volleying is still a consistently viable chiến lược in today’s game is a stretch. It is important to note that the Paris final was a very particular matchup: Djokovic played Daniil Medvedev, who likes to play points from the back of the sân and returns his opponents’ chủ động tấn công shots without fail, resulting in them eventually making a frustrated error.

Many of Medvedev’s returns go down the giữa of the sân though, so opportune lưới play can result in some easy volleys that do not have to be hit on the stretch. “Opportune lưới play” probably sums up Djokovic’s chiến thuật best: Djokovic has built a career playing similarly to the way Medvedev plays now, but at 34 years of age, endlessly sprinting and sliding from the đường cuối sân to keep points alive does not always work. Djokovic’s lưới skills are far from revolutionary. Though a solid volleyer, Djokovic does not make eye-popping volleys like Roger Federer once did. The chiến thuật will probably work at times, but drawing a sweeping conclusion that giao bóng-and-bắt lưới is not, and has never been, overshadowed by more modern winning techniques is probably buoyed by the temptation of nostalgia.

Despite O’Shannessy’s correct observation that giao bóng-and-bắt lưới was pivotal in the recent Paris final, Djokovic’s execution of the chiến lược was risky and may not work as well in the future. giao bóng-and-bắt lưới may be a relic that can still occasionally be very useful, but it is a relic nonetheless.

The goal of a giao bóng-and-bắt lưới player isn’t complex. Simply put, they’re looking to get in a strong first giao bóng that doesn’t allow a strong cú đánh nền in the trả bóng. As for the returner, it puts them in immediate áp lực.

Even though it’s tough to get a strong trả bóng on a big giao bóng, you can’t hit a light shot that allows the server plenty of time to set up a điểm thắng.

The Fastest Miles Ever Run

Typically, giao bóng-and-bắt lưới players are going to experience more success on hardcourt and sân cỏ surfaces. It can be a daunting task for the opponent, but there are still plenty of ways to get by these giao bóng-and-bắt lưới players with a victory.

Cautious or Reckless?

There are two ways you can tấn công a giao bóng-and-bắt lưới player: cautiously or recklessly. The cautious approach will lead to you just returning the first giao bóng and not attempting any type of passing shots. Essentially, you’re not going to giao lại your own shots lose the point, but rather make the opponent win the point. Conversely, a reckless chiến lược will attempt a high-degree of passing shots. This player will try to hit a big shot with every vung vợt and go for a điểm thắng often.

There are glaring negatives with each approach. A cautious approach can result in an experienced player making quick work of you. Meanwhile, a reckless approach will lead to a high number of unforced errors.

A Little of Both

My best solution is to play a mixture of cautious and reckless. With a giao bóng-and-bắt lưới player, you’re not going to have a chance for a point on every shot. This requires patience. If they make a strong giao bóng, just get a trả bóng in and wait for the opportunity to hit a điểm thắng. Sometimes, you’ll even luck out and they’ll miss on an open shot. The key is to wait for your chances. Don’t give your opponent free points, make them earn it!

Play to Their Weaknesses

Unless you’re playing someone like Roger Federer or Novak Djokovic, chances are your opponent will have some holes in their game. As a player, it is your job to find these weaknesses and take lợi thế of them. Even if this requires you to alter your approach slightly, it is still likely going to be worth it. Do they struggle running back if a cú lốp was hit over their đầu vợt? Is their trái tay or thuận tay stronger? Finding answers to these questions will help greatly as the trận đấu carries on.

As I mentioned earlier, a big giao bóng is a critical part of the giao bóng-and-bắt lưới approach. If they can’t lực you to stay back behind the đường cuối sân, then the giao bóng-and-bắt lưới chiến lược won’t be effective. Assuming they do have a strong giao bóng, the only way to stay in the point is to get the first trả bóng over the lưới. While it would be ideal to get a decent trả bóng in, I recommend making sure you get the first trả bóng in for the early stages of the trận đấu.

This will not only make the games closer, but it will increase your own confidence. At the lõi of giao bóng-and-bắt lưới player’s games is the idea that they want to get to the lưới. They want to put áp lực on you. However, it will keep them closer to the đường cuối sân. The second approach is for when the server is able to get to the lưới. In these situations, you can hit a cú lốp. A cú lốp goes over their đầu vợt and forces them retreat on their sân. Technological advances in both dây and rackets have made passing shots faster and more accurate, giving an added edge to the returner. When evaluating a chiến thuật, we have to start by recognizing that players and coaches generally know what they’re doing. Sure, they make mistakes, and they can fall into suboptimal patterns. If more frequent giao bóng-and-volleying was such a slam dunk, wouldn’t players be doing so?

I dug into trận đấu Charting Project data to get a better idea of how often players are using the giao bóng-and-bắt lưới, how successful it has been. and, just as important, how successful they’ve been when they aren’t using it. The results are considerably more mixed than the giao bóng-and-bắt lưới cheerleaders would have it. giao lại’s start with the women. When we apply the same analysis to the men, the results are downright baffling. Thus, they may give too much weight to frequent netrushers.

I ran the same calculation in two other ways: giving equal weight to each player-trận đấu, and weighting each player-trận đấu by ln(a+1), where a is the number of giao bóng-and-bắt lưới attempts. Data from the women’s game is more encouraging for those who would like to see more giao bóng-and-volleying, but it is still rather modest. It’s possible that some women should come in behind their serves more often. But the overall evidence from a couple thousand matches suggests sticking to the đường cuối sân is just as good of a bet–if not better.
