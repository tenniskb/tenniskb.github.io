Sinner vs Djokovic: Bán Kết Wimbledon - Tốc Độ Vung Vợt vs Phòng Thủ Đỉnh Cao

Tác giả: Hugh Clarke · A Thread of Order · Bài Viết Gốc Substack ↗

Jannik Sinner takes on Novak Djokovic for the first time since their epic Australian Open semifinal. What was pivotal in Djokovic’s upset win there was the forehand down-the-line, clutch serving on break points, and wily energy management across five sets of Sinner-speed tennis.

The forehand aggression has been a feature of Djokovic’s when facing off against Sincaraz. Up until this year’s Aus Open match, The 2023 ATP Finals was the best example of post-peak Novak’s tactics in quicker conditions: lethal pinpoint serving followed up by a huge increase in risk-tolerance on the forehand, both in terms of speed, and change of direction.

It’s well noted that Sinner usually hits the ball heavier (i.e., more speed and spin) off both wings than Djokovic, but the forehand is where Djokovic can step up and even out-shine his usually more-powerful opponents.

Given this, I wonder if Sinner will make an effort to make this a match of more backhands than usual. Novak is often touted as possessing the greatest backhand of all-time, while his forehand is the one that is often overlooked in that conversation. But I think post-prime Novak is something inverted in these affairs; it’s the Serb’s forehand that clearly is a candidate for greatest of all-time, given how well he returns off that wing, changes direction, handles pace, and can be lethal in both directions from end-range. And if we take the AI’s interpretation of shot quality in this matchup, we can see that Novak’s backhand is the shot that has been on a steady decline and is the clear weaker wing (currently good enough for 8th in 2026 shot-quality rankings) whereas the forehand has been getting better and better since 2024.

In Australia, Djokovic got to hit 40 more forehands than backhands, and it was the wing that delivered all the damage and won him the match.

It’s going to be warm out there today, and the court will be playing more like a hardcourt given the dry fortnight they’ve been having. I think this favours Sinner - heat issues aside - as he can inject more pace on serve and off the ground, and creates a slightly higher contact point. I don’t think the heat will become an issue physically on a grass court with shorter points.

Speaking of serves, Gilbert observed that FAA got a lot of reward for the wide serve (I noticed this on the ad-side especially), where the 39-year-old Serb gets punished for losing a bit of reaction time. Keep an eye on the Italian’s serve trends today; it’s been a key shot of his as the forehand has somewhat wavered in 2026.
