Jeff Counts (Hi-Tech Tennis) Analysis Note: Professional strokes maintain đầu vợt and eye stability for 2–3 frames (approx. 12ms) through impact, ensuring absolute repeatability without compensatory cổ tay manipulation.

Due to incredible advancements in vợt and string technology, along with the "xoáy trên revolution" initiated by Bjorn Borg, thuận tay kỹ thuật has changed dramatically over the past two decades. Over the years, players have improved upon Borg's thuận tay, culminating in a modern thuận tay that is both extraordinarly powerful as well as extremely consistent due to heavy xoáy trên.

Unlike the long, linear, arms swings of the 70's (straight back đưa vợt ra sau and kết thúc with your vợt pointing towards your target), modern kỹ thuật taps into two forms of moment động lượng góc. First we have the coiling and uncoiling of the upper body to create a chuỗi động học where năng lượng flows from the legs and uncoiling torso all the way into the second burst of angular moment, where the vợt travels "inside out" into contact through a relaxation of the cổ tay.

The "vung vợt" of the arm has been eliminated. Instead the arm gets pulled forward rather rather than "swung." By opening the vai first and then pulling the vợt, butt cap first, you create trễ. And by pulling the butt of the vợt to the ball, you are able to contact the ball in a perfectly leveraged vị trí which is in front of the body and to the side (at a 45 degree góc) with the vai powerfully positioned behind the hand.

On contact, instead of "hitting through the ball" or "extending to the target", the modern thuận tay has you powerfully, and muscularly lift the arm and vợt. By lifting upward, you get your arm, vai, and hand all working together to powerfully bite into, and lift the ball. The actual "hit" has much more in common with a boxing upper cut motion where the vai, khuỷu tay and hand đường bóng upward. You can see this most clearly in the "reverse thuận tay" or "buggy roi vung thuận tay" that is so popular today where the hand finishes on the right side of the body with the hand behind the đầu vợt. Watch it here in incredible detail This upward thrust of the vai and hand creates a cú đánh that is much more powerful and explosive.

In the Olivier Rochus animation (as well as in the James Blake animation) on the right, tập trung on the how they đường bóng their upper arms upward on contact. In that brief moment from contact to just after contact they are driving upward from the lòng bàn tay of the hand through the arm to the powerful vai. This is very different than "hitting through" the ball. I would call it a powerful push and đường bóng upward of the arm, hand, and vai.

The standard cầm vợt to hit this type of thuận tay is the semi-western or "frying pan" cầm vợt. This cầm vợt positions the hand more under the cán vợt rather than behind it. By having the lòng bàn tay positioned more under the cán vợt it aids in the upward lifting of the ball with the arm and vai.
