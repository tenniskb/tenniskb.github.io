COACHING THE TECHNICAL GAME Chương 41: Dạy Từng Stroke *"A good teacher makes the difficult seem simple. A great teacher makes the simple seem profound."* > — Anonymous Chương 40 đặt nền tảng của coaching philosophy và methodology. Chương này đi vào thực chiến: Làm thế nào để dạy từng stroke cụ thể? Đây là kiến thức mà coaches cần hàng ngày — không phải lý thuyết trừu tượng mà là practical blueprint: cách break down mỗi shot, những errors phổ biến nhất, cách identify nguyên nhân, và cách design progressions để học viên phát triển từ beginner đến advanced.

41.1 Nguyên Tắc Chung Khi Dạy Kỹ Thuật Dạy

Từ Ground Up Mọi stroke trong tennis bắt đầu từ mặt đất: Feet → legs → hips → core → shoulder → arm → wrist → racket → ball. Đây là kinetic chain.

Khi một link bị break — thường làở feet hoặc hips — toàn bộ chain bịảnh hưởng.

Practical implication: Khi học viên có arm error, hãy check feet và hips trước.

Hầu hết arm errors là downstream consequence của upstream problems.

Dạy Pattern Trước Detail Beginners không cần biết về pronation hay external rotation.

Họ cần biết cách swing racket trong đúng shape và timing.

Teaching order: 1.

Overall pattern (shape of swing, timing) 2.

Contact point (where và when to meet ball) 3.

Footwork (how to set up for the pattern) 4.

Finish (follow-through and recovery) 5.

Technical refinements (intermediate và advanced only) Technique Phục Vụ Outcome Technique không phải là mục tiêu — nó là phương tiện.

Mục tiêu là bóng đi đúng hướng, đúng tốc độ, đúng spin, một cách consistent.

Coach's question trước mọi technical intervention: "Is this technique causing a problem?

If not, don't fix it."

41.2 Dạy Forehand Forehand Progressions Level 1: Catch và Feed (complete Beginners) Trước khi swing, học viên phải hiểu contact point.

Drill: Coach toss bóng chậm, học viên catch bằng tayở vị trí contact — in front of body, arm extended.

Sau khi consistent, replace hand với racket.

Teaching point: Contact point in front of front foot, arm slightly extended.

Level 2: Mini Tennis (Beginner) Chơi từ service line.

Khoảng cách ngắn → bóng chậm → thời gian để set up.

Focus: Unit turn, contact point in front, simple follow-through.

Không quan tâm đến power hay topspin.

Level 3: Full Court Groundstroke (Beginner-Intermediate) Coach feed từ basket.

Focus: Split step, adjustment steps, loading position.

Swing path từ low đến high.

Cue: "Wait, wait, NOW" — develop patience for contact point.

Level 4: Topspin Development (Intermediate) Focus: Steeper low-to-high swing path.

Brush back of ball từ 7 o'clock đến 1 o'clock.

Feel cue: "Brush up the back of the ball like wiping a dirty windshield." Common error: Arm swing without body rotation.

Fix: "Turn your belly button to the target as you swing." Level 5: Open Stance và Power (Intermediate-Advanced) Focus: Hip rotation initiates swing.

Shoulders follow hips.

Arm follows shoulders.

Drill: Hit từ open stance only for 20 balls.

Exaggerate hip rotation.

Level 6: Inside-Out Forehand (Advanced) Step around backhand side ball to hit forehand.

Drill: Coach feeds alternately to forehand và backhand side.

On backhand feed, player decides whether to run around it or hit backhand.

Common Forehand Errors Ball goes into net consistently: - Check contact point — is it too late? - Check swing path — is player swinging flat? - Fix: "Finish with your racket on your left shoulder" — teaches upward path.

Ball consistently goes wide: - Check racket face at contact — is it open? - Fix: "After contact, racket crosses your body to the left." No power despite big swing: - Check body rotation — is player all arm? - Fix: "Grip loosely until contact — then firm up at impact." Inconsistency: - Usually contact point varies — sometimes in front, sometimes late. - Fix: Ball toss drill to establish consistent contact height and timing.

41.3 Dạy Backhand One-Handed vs.

Two-Handed Two-handed: Easier to learn, more stable for children, less reach, less slice versatility.

One-handed: More reach, more versatility, higher ceiling, harder to develop.

General recommendation: - Children under 10: Two-handed almost always better. - Adults: Ask preference, observe what feels natural. - Once committed: Don't switch unless compelling reason.

Two-Handed Backhand Progressions Level 1: Wall Rally Stand 2 meters from wall.

Rally two-handed backhand.

Focus: Unit turn to backhand side, contact in front, two hands firm through contact.

Level 2: Feed Drill — Direction Control Coach feeds to backhand.

Player hits to specific target.

Focus: Footwork (load onto back foot, transfer to front).

Unit turn.

Common beginner error: Pushing with bottom hand instead of swinging.

Fix: "Lead with your top hand.

Bottom hand is for support." Level 3: Topspin Two-Hander Steeper upward swing path.

Both arms must swing together.

Cue: "Both elbows point down before contact, both elbows point up after contact." Level 4: Running Two-Hander Open stance two-hander with aggressive hip rotation for wide balls.

At extreme wide balls: One-hand finish allows extra reach.

One-Handed Backhand Progressions Level 1: Fence Drill Stand with back close to fence.

Swing backhand — if fence stops swing, backswing is too big.

Teaches compact backswing.

Level 2: Chip Backhand High-to-low slice motion first.

Simpler, builds contact consistency before topspin.

Level 3: Full Topspin One-Hander Key: Full shoulder rotation in backswing.

Wrist laid back before contact.

Explosive shoulder rotation through contact.

Common collapse: Wrist bends forward before contact → ball into net.

Fix: "Keep your wrist firm and back until after you've hit the ball." Common Backhand Errors Two-hander into net: Bottom arm bent at contact → racket face closed.

Fix: "Keep your bottom arm straighter through contact." One-hander lacks power: Shoulder not fully turned in backswing.

Fix: "Turn until your back is almost facing the net before you swing." Inconsistent direction: Player changing swing path instead of contact timing for direction.

Fix: Same swing path always.

Earlier contact = crosscourt.

Later contact = down the line.

41.4 Dạy Serve Teaching Principles Serve is the most complex shot because of the full kinetic chain, self-initiated motion, multiple variables, và significant individual variation.

Coaching principle: More legitimate individual variation here than any other stroke.

Find the student's natural rhythm and build on it — don't force cookie-cutter technique.

The Trophy Position "Trophy position" = top of backswing, just before forward swing.

Key checkpoints: - Tossing arm extended high toward ball - Racket arm in "L" shape - Knees bent (or extending from knee bend) - Weight loaded and ready to transfer Serve Progressions Level 1: The Helicopter (Complete Beginner) Swing motion without toss.

Arm swings in continuous circle: down, back, up to trophy, forward, follow-through.

Understand shape before adding toss.

Level 2: Toss Placement (Beginner) Toss drill: Tossing arm only, no swing.

Toss must land in specific target zone on court.

Repeat 20 times.

The toss catch test: Toss ball, don't swing, let it fall.

Should land forward of front foot, slightly right of head for right-handers.

Common toss errors: Too far left (player chases ball), too far back (falls back), too low (rushed swing), inconsistent height.

Level 3: Flat Serve (Beginner-Intermediate) Eastern/continental grip.

Contact at maximum height.

Follow-through to opposite hip.

Common error: Bending elbow at contact.

Fix: "Reach as high as you can — like trying to touch the sky with your racket." Level 4: Slice Serve (Intermediate) Continental grip.

Brush right side of ball (right-hander).

Ball curves right and stays low.

Cue: "Cut the right side of the ball like you're slicing an apple." Level 5: Kick Serve (Intermediate-Advanced) Toss slightly further left.

Brush back of ball low-left to high-right.

Heavy topspin → high kick to backhand.

Common error: Swinging too flat.

Fix: "Swing up and to the left, not forward." Teaching Second Serve Confidence Two-part coaching approach: Technical: Ensure kick serve is 80%+ reliable in isolation before using in matches.

Mental: Introduce pressure gradually.

Practice second serves with score.

Verbalize stakes.

Key coaching message: "Your second serve should be an offensive weapon.

Commit to the kick serve with full arm speed." 41.5 Dạy Volley Key Differences From Groundstrokes No backswing: Racket stays in front.

Only forward punch motion.

Continental grip: For both forehand và backhand.

Firm wrist: Unlike groundstrokes where wrist is active.

Step into ball: Weight forward, not side to side.

Volley Progressions Level 1: Wall Volley Stand 1-2 meters from wall.

Volley continuously forehand then backhand.

Focus: No backswing.

Firm wrist.

Push forward.

Goal: 30 consecutive volleys without error.

Level 2: Coach Feed, Static Position Coach feeds alternately to forehand và backhand volleys from baseline.

Focus: Step into each volley.

Punch motion.

Ball placement (depth).

Level 3: Movement + Volley Coach feeds wider balls requiring lateral movement.

Focus: Split step before coach contacts.

Lateral step + volley.

Reset center.

Level 4: Approach + Volley Sequence Full match-realistic sequence: Approach shot → move to net → split step → volley.

Level 5: Half Volley Ball bouncing at feet — hit immediately after bounce.

Focus: Racket below ball.

Short, compact swing.

Soft hands.

Common Volley Errors Volley goes long: Backswing too big.

Fix: "Start with racket in front.

Don't take it back — just punch forward." Volley goes in net: Wrist dropping at contact.

Fix: "Squeeze your grip and lock your wrist when you contact the ball." Ball pops up: Contact point too late (beside or behind body).

Fix: "Meet the ball early — in front of your front foot."

41.6 Dạy Overhead Teaching Priority: Positioning First Most overhead misses come from poor positioning, not poor swing mechanics.

Teaching priority: Position student correctly BEFORE working on swing.

Overhead Progressions Level 1: Positioning Drill Coach lobs.

Student positions so ball lands in front of hitting shoulder — no swing.

Walk through, then jog.

Establish correct body-to-ball relationship.

Level 2: Short Overhead Coach feeds easy lobs from close distance.

Focus: Turn sideways.

Point non-dominant hand toward ball.

Full extension at contact.

Level 3: Full Overhead Coach feeds from baseline.

Full lob height.

Focus: Read lob early.

Turn immediately.

Move backward using crossover step.

Common error: Waiting too long before turning.

Fix: "The moment you see it's a lob, turn your back to the net and run." Level 4: Jump Overhead (Advanced) For deep lobs.

Jump and scissors kick motion.

Contact at peak of jump.

41.7 Specialty Shots Slice Backhand Often first specialty shot worth teaching systematically.

Continental grip.

High-to-low swing path.

Open racket face at contact. Underspin.

Common error: Chopping too steeply → ball pops up.

Fix: "Swing forward, not down.

The racket goes slightly down but mostly forward." Drill: Both players rally crosscourt with slice backhand only.

Drop Shot When to teach: Only after student has reliable groundstrokes.

Setup identical to groundstroke.

At last moment, racket slows dramatically.

Open face generates underspin.

Ball barely clears net.

Common error: Telegraphing — slowing down early, changing stance.

Fix: Practice disguise as much as the shot itself.

Defensive Lob High, deep ball over net player.

Open racket face.

Aim well above opponent's reach.

Teaching approach: Practice with actual net player present — realistic target, realistic pressure.

41.8 Return Of Serve Return Coaching Framework Step 1: Positioning General starting position: 1 meter behind baseline for first serve.

0.5 meter behind for second serve

Step 2: Split Step Timing Must time split step to server's contact.

Drill: Focus ONLY on split step timing — not return quality.

Once timing consistent, add direction.

Step 3: Return Shape Against first serve: Compact backswing, redirect with timing.

Power comes from server.

Against second serve: Full swing, step in, attack.

Step 4: Direction Control Return same serve location to same target 10 times.

Directional control before varying.

Tóm Tắt Chương 41 Dạy từ ground up.

Arm errors usually trace to feet và hips.

Fix root cause.

Pattern trước detail.

Overall shape → contact point → footwork → finish → refinements.

Forehand progression: Mini tennis → full court → topspin → open stance → inside-out.

Backhand: Two-hander easier to start.

One-hander higher ceiling.

Don't switch once committed.

Serve: Toss reliability is foundation.

Second serve = kick serve with commitment, not survival.

Volley: No backswing.

Firm wrist.

Step into ball.

Teach even when students resist.

Overhead: Positioning is primary skill.

Move early.

Swing mechanics secondary.

Specialty shots: Slice backhand first.

Drop shot requires disguise.

Lob needs real net player to practice against.

Return: Positioning + split step timing first.

Shape second.

Direction third.

One error, one fix, one session.
