# The Grand Slam Journey: Defining Moments

## Grand Slams — Tennis's Summit

Nothing in tennis matters more than the Grand Slams. The four majors — the Australian Open, Roland Garros, Wimbledon, and the US Open — are the final measuring stick for any player. The list of the most Grand Slam titles is the list of the sport's greatest names.

Sinner joined that elite list in September 2023 by winning the US Open. At twenty-two, he became the fourth Italian to win a Grand Slam, after Adriano Panatta (Roland Garros, 1976) and Corrado Barazzutti (a finalist, never a champion) — and no one else until Sinner's generation.

His Grand Slam journey reflects his entire tennis philosophy in miniature: not one explosive breakthrough win, but a steady process built round after round, from first round to final, with remarkable consistency.

## US Open 2023: The Opening Chapter

The 2023 US Open wasn't Sinner's first deep Grand Slam run — he'd already reached a few quarterfinals and semifinals. But it was his first final, and how he got there says a lot.

**The road to the final:** Sinner beat several strong players along the way, most notably in the quarterfinal and semifinal. He didn't have an easy set anywhere — what stood out was the quality he maintained match after match without "saving energy" or suffering a mental setback.

**The final against Medvedev:** Medvedev — with an excellent US Open record, a 2021 champion and multiple-time finalist — started the match in complete control. Sets one and two went to Medvedev by scores that didn't hint at any comeback to come.

**Sinner in set three:** the turnaround began. Sinner didn't change his style — no panic, no reckless winners. He executed exactly what Vagnozzi and Cahill had planned: adding depth to Medvedev's backhand side and exploiting the serve-wide pattern. Set three went to Sinner, 6-4.

## The Comeback at US Open 2023: A Historic Moment

Looking closer at the tactics and technique behind the comeback reveals a masterclass in strategic patience.

**Why he lost sets one and two:** Medvedev controlled rally pace superbly through varying speed and depth. Sinner was trying to impose his own tempo too early, without reading Medvedev's adjustments.

**The set-three adjustment:** Sinner stopped trying to force the pace and started "waiting" for the right ball. Specifically, he added two or three neutral shots before every attack, instead of attacking on the first or second shot. This got his rhythm back and gave him time to read Medvedev's adjustments.

**Sets four and five:** Medvedev started to feel the pressure. Sinner had found a way to crack his defense — and, just as important, kept his mental state completely steady despite the enormous pressure. Set five closed out 6-3 — a scoreline hard to believe from a player who'd just lost the first two sets.

> "When I was down 0-2, I wasn't thinking about winning the tournament. I was just thinking: play the next set well. Then the one after that. That's all." — Jannik Sinner, after the 2023 US Open

## Australian Open 2024: Champion in Melbourne

The 2024 Australian Open marked the next leap: his first title in Melbourne — achieved even more impressively than the US Open had been.

**What stood out about this run:** Sinner reached the final without dropping a set, all the way until the semifinal against Djokovic. That match was a serious test — Djokovic, even at 36, remained the tour's toughest opponent when playing well. Sinner got through in five excellent sets.

**The final against Medvedev:** the second Sinner-Medvedev Grand Slam final in five months. Once again, Medvedev started strong, taking an early lead. And once again, Sinner recovered — but faster and more convincingly this time. His US Open experience clearly helped him handle the situation better.

**Result:** Sinner won 3-2. Melbourne became his new home away from home — the fast hard court and the late-year climate suit his style perfectly.

## Roland Garros: The Red Wall

Roland Garros — the legendary Paris clay — is the one Grand Slam Sinner hasn't reached a final at. The story there reveals both his strengths and where he still has room to grow.

**The technical problem on clay:** Sinner's flat, early-hitting style works less effectively on the slower clay surface. Clay gives the ball more time to bounce up higher — time an opponent can use to set up a heavy shot. Clay specialists like Alcaraz, Rune, or Tsitsipas can exploit that.

**Sinner's adjustments for Roland Garros:** increasing spin by 10-15% through a slightly more closed racquet face; extending rallies by two or three extra shots compared to hard court; and increasing the frequency of the active backhand pattern, usually a strength on clay.

**Best result at Roland Garros: semifinal (2024).** Sinner lost to Alcaraz in three sets — Alcaraz playing at his absolute peak at Roland Garros 2024. But the year-on-year improvement is clear: round of 16 (2022), quarterfinal (2023), semifinal (2024). The trajectory is obvious.

> "Roland Garros is where I've learned the most. Every time I lose there, I understand more clearly what I need to improve." — Jannik Sinner

## Wimbledon: Grass Is the Next Challenge

Wimbledon — the oldest and most prestigious major — is the Grand Slam where Sinner has the least experience and the most modest results. But like at Roland Garros, the trajectory is clearly improving.

**The technical challenge on grass:** Wimbledon's grass is particularly low and fast — the ball stays low, skids, and moves quickly. That favors big serving and net play — not Sinner's natural strength. Baseline rallies on grass are much shorter than on hard court or clay.

**Sinner's improvement:** in 2024, he reached the semifinal — his career best there. He lost to Alcaraz in an extremely tight five sets. Particularly notable was his improvement in serve-and-volley and his ability to close out points quickly on grass — two areas he's clearly developing.

**Looking ahead:** given his current trajectory, a Wimbledon final within one or two years is a realistic scenario. Sinner isn't a natural grass player the way Federer or Djokovic were, but he has the technique and fitness to compete at the highest level there if he keeps developing in the right direction.

## Australian Open 2025: Total Dominance

The 2025 Australian Open marks his latest peak to date: a second Melbourne title, this time delivered with far more authority than the first.

**A run without dropping a set:** Sinner went from the first round to the final without losing a single set — six matches, six straight-sets wins. That had never happened for him at any Grand Slam before. It wasn't just the results — the quality of every set was also career-best.

**The final and the numbers:** the 2025 Australian Open final is considered one of his best individual performances at a Grand Slam. 71% serve percentage, 9.1/10 forehand shot quality — historic numbers.

**What it means:** two straight Australian Open titles show Melbourne wasn't a fluke in 2024. This is his court in a literal sense — where his style and technique get maximized.

## The Pressure of Being No. 1 at a Grand Slam

Being world No. 1 carries a specific responsibility at Grand Slams: the highest expectation, from media, and from himself.

**Media expectation:** when Sinner walked into Roland Garros or Wimbledon 2024 as the No. 1 seed, every match got scrutinized. The pressure of "why hasn't he won here yet" is real.

**How Sinner handles it:** as covered in the psychology chapter, he has excellent discipline about not letting media pressure affect his focus during a match. But he admits: "As No. 1, every Grand Slam you haven't won is a question you have to answer for yourself."

**A lesson from Djokovic:** Djokovic, who's been No. 1 many times and still lost unexpected Grand Slams, is a clear reminder that being No. 1 guarantees nothing. Sinner takes the lesson: "The goal isn't to win everything. The goal is to prepare as well as possible and play as well as possible."

## Grand Slam Statistics, Overall

Breaking down his Grand Slam statistics year by year shows a clear, remarkable trajectory.

**2019-2020 (the early years):** reached the third round at Roland Garros 2019 — his first professional Grand Slam appearance. Modest results elsewhere — normal for an 18-19-year-old.

**2021-2022 (the first breakthrough):** first Australian Open quarterfinal, in 2022. Started producing stable multi-round wins instead of deep-then-sudden-loss results.

**2023-2025 (dominance):** the 2023 US Open (champion), the 2024 Australian Open (champion), semifinals at the 2024 Roland Garros and Wimbledon, and the 2025 Australian Open (champion). Total: 3 Grand Slams in 10 months.

## Looking Ahead: Roland Garros 2025

As of this handbook, Roland Garros 2025 is the next Grand Slam Sinner is preparing for. The question is whether he can finally conquer Paris.

**What's changed since 2024:** his clay-court technique has improved substantially through dedicated preparation with the team. In particular, his ability to "sit" on high-bouncing balls and counter an opponent's heavy topspin has been a training focus.

**What remains:** Alcaraz is still the top favorite at Roland Garros, having already won in 2022 and 2024. Sinner needs to beat him specifically in clay conditions — something that hasn't happened yet. But Sinner's improving trajectory and his ability to learn from losses are reasons for optimism.

**Prediction:** Roland Garros will likely be the last Grand Slam Sinner conquers — not because it's the hardest overall (Wimbledon is equally tough for him) but because it's where his toughest opponent, Alcaraz, is strongest. But when he does win it, it will mark the completion of his career Grand Slam.

> "Roland Garros is the tournament I want most out of everything I don't have. Not because clay is my favorite surface — but because it's my hardest challenge. And I like a challenge." — Jannik Sinner
