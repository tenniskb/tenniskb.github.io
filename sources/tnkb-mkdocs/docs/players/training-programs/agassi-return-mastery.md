# Agassi Return Mastery Program

Andre Agassi is widely regarded as having the greatest return of serve in tennis history — not because of raw reaction speed alone, but because of anticipation, positioning, and a relentless habit of turning a defensive shot into the first offensive blow of the point. This program builds those same habits: recognizing serve patterns before contact, taking the ball early, and converting return points into a weapon rather than a survival shot.

**Duration:** 10 weeks · **Frequency:** 3-4 sessions/week · **Session length:** 60-90 minutes

**Equipment:** court, balls, ball machine or serving partner, cones/target zones, agility ladder, reaction balls, video analysis equipment (phone camera is enough)

**Prerequisites:** a return-ready grip (continental or eastern forehand for the forehand side, continental or two-handed for the backhand side), and a working split step.

## Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1-2)

**Goal:** proper stance, honest split-step timing, and basic direction control.

- **Split-step timing (15 min/day):** shadow split steps timed to an imagined serve motion (no ball); jump rope for quick-feet rhythm; reaction-ball catches for hand-eye speed.
- **Positioning and court awareness (20 min):** practice standing at the correct depth for second serve versus a weak first serve; diagonal movement patterns to cover the wide serve; shadow returns to wide/body/T.
- **Basic direction control (15 min):** cross-court and down-the-line returns from both wings, aiming to clear the net by 3-6 feet and land deep.
- **Conditioning:** ladder drills (in-and-out, lateral shuffle, ickey shuffle — 4x30s), cone drills (T-drill, pro-agility — 3 sets each direction), ball-drop reaction catches (3x20), squat jumps (3x10), lateral bounds (3x15/side), single-leg hops (3x20/leg), medicine-ball rotational throws (3x15/side), cable woodchoppers (3x12/side), plank with shoulder taps (3x30s).
- **Practice application:** return-to-targets — deuce and ad court, cross-court/down-the-line/middle, starting with second serves and progressing to first serves.

Sample week: Monday technique + agility (90 min), Wednesday positioning + plyometrics (75 min), Friday direction control + core (90 min), Sunday match simulation focused only on returning (60 min).

## Phase 2 — Reaction and Anticipation (Weeks 3-4)

**Goal:** improve serve recognition, cut reaction time, shift from reactive to proactive positioning.

- **Serve recognition (20 min):** video study of toss patterns, pronation, and shoulder angle; live serving where you call out serve type (flat/slice/kick) before contact; call out location before the bounce.
- **Proactive movement (15 min):** "step in" on second serves to take the ball on the rise; diagonal backward steps for wide serves; crossover steps for the extreme-wide serve.
- **Return positioning strategy (10 min):** when to stand in (second serve, weak first serve) versus stay back (big server), and how to adjust to a server's tendencies mid-match.
- **Conditioning:** light-based or partner-cued reaction shuffles, shuffle-to-ball-drop in random directions, 5-10-5 pro-agility with a visual cue, resisted-band sprints (10 yards x4), falling starts into a sprint (3x5/direction), box drill with explosive exits, med-ball slams (3x15), rotational cable pushes (3x12/side), weighted Russian twists (3x20).
- **Practice application:** return-and-attack (take short serves and approach immediately), consistency-under-pressure targets (aim for the last 3 feet of the court, chase 20 in a row), simulated break points where the return must land in play to "win" the point.

## Phase 3 — Pressure and Tactical Integration (Weeks 5-6)

**Goal:** perform the return under match pressure and build explicit tactical plans against different server types.

- **Pattern recognition (20 min):** study an opponent's deuce-versus-ad and first-versus-second-serve tendencies; build "if-then" return plans; disguise your return intention until the last possible moment.
- **Advanced return techniques (15 min):** block return with minimal backswing for heavy serves; chip-and-charge on second serves; swinging volley off a high-bouncing serve.
- **Mental routines (10 min):** a fixed pre-return routine (feet, grip, focus point), a between-point reset, and a specific plan for managing frustration after a missed return.
- **Conditioning:** 300-yard shuttles (6x50 yards, short rest), Tabata intervals on the ladder or sprints (20s on/10s off x4 min), hill sprints (8x20s), plank variations (3x45s each), hanging knee raises (3x20), Pallof press (3x15/side), eccentric heel drops for Achilles health (3x15), banded external rotations (3x20), scapular wall slides (3x15).
- **Practice application:** return-only games (server scores only on a missed return), serve-and-return sets where only return points count, practicing specific scorelines (love-40, deuce, ad-in/out). Strategy work against archetypes: big server → block deep, wait for the second serve; spin specialist → take the ball early, use its own pace; lefty serve → adjust stance and swing path.

## Phase 4 — Match Integration and Maintenance (Weeks 7-10)

**Goal:** fold the return into full point construction and hold the gains.

- **Return in context (20 min):** return cross-court to set up the forehand; return-move-to-net-volley patterns; using the return to neutralize and restart a point you're losing tactically.
- **Advanced situations (15 min):** returning a serve-and-volleyer, doubles return positioning and communication, attacking second serves with variety instead of a single pattern.
- **Video review (10 min):** compare your return footage to Agassi's technique and decision-making; pick one specific thing to fix each week rather than everything at once.
- **Conditioning:** taper volume, hold intensity, prioritize injury prevention and recovery; in the final week before any competition, cut volume further and protect sleep, nutrition, and hydration.
- **Practice application:** full practice sets and matches, tracking return statistics (see below); a short cool-down stretch and a two-line journal entry on what worked.

## Key Drills Library

| Category | Drills |
| --- | --- |
| Split-step and reaction | Shadow split to serve motion; toss-call (partner serves, you call toss location before contact); reaction-ball shuffle; light-cued sprint to a cone |
| Positioning and movement | Baseline-to-target-and-recover; diagonal recovery after a shadow return; in-and-out steps for short/deep serves; full return-and-recover to center |
| Direction and depth | Cone-alley returns; depth ladder (short → medium → deep zones); cross-court/down-the-line alternation; target pyramid (wide-body-T-wide) |
| Pressure and match play | Return-only games; break-point simulation (server starts at 0-40); consistency streak challenge; specific-scoreline returning |

## Weekly Metrics to Track

- Return percentage (overall, first serve, second serve)
- Aggressive return percentage (returns that produce an approach, a winner, or a forced error)
- Break points converted
- Return points won
- Unforced errors on return

**Monthly assessment:** compare technique on video against your baseline, track return stats from practice matches, retest 10-yard sprint time, pro-agility time, vertical jump, and medicine-ball throw distance, and rate your own return confidence on a 1-10 scale.

## Adapting by Level

- **Beginner (NTRP 2.5-3.5):** extend the split-step and positioning work through weeks 1-4, use slower machine-fed serves, use larger direction targets, and prioritize consistency over aggression through week 10.
- **Intermediate (NTRP 4.0-4.5):** run the program as written, with emphasis on developing aggressive return options and gradually increasing serve speed.
- **Advanced (NTRP 5.0+):** compress to 6-8 weeks, emphasize tactical variation and pressure performance, and incorporate real opponent scouting.
- **Senior or return-from-injury:** substitute jumps with step patterns, add recovery days, prioritize precision and placement over explosive movement, and consider softer balls or a smaller court if needed.

## Coach's Notes

Adjust the program to the player's physique and return style (blocker versus puncher), use delayed or live video feedback for correction, and expect 6-8 weeks before the return genuinely changes under pressure — this is not a two-week fix. Keep sessions engaging with games and challenges, and don't let return work crowd out balanced development of the rest of the game.

**Safety:** always complete a dynamic warm-up before reaction work, static-stretch the hips, legs, shoulders, and back afterward, hydrate through intense sessions, watch for slippery court conditions, and use footwear with real lateral support.
