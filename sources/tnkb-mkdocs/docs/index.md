# Tennis Knowledge Base

I wrote this handbook for myself first. When I started, I'd already played tennis for years and still couldn't explain why my forehand kept flying long, why my elbow started hurting at 45, or why the pros made it all look effortless. I read a lot of books, watched a lot of video, took a training course in Canada, and asked better players more questions than they probably wanted to answer. What's on this site is the synthesis of all of that — tested on my own body, not theorized in the abstract. Tennis isn't a hammer sport. It's a whip sport. And if you're playing it for the next 20 years, that distinction matters more than almost anything else you'll read here.

I'm writing this for players around 3.5 aiming for 4.0, over 50, who are done with generic advice like "hit out in front" or "use your legs" and actually want the mechanism behind it. In here you'll find how I fixed my forehand with an L-angle and later acceleration, how I took my serve from 85 to 95 mph at age 53, the grip-pressure rules that got rid of my elbow pain, and the mental rituals that keep me from tightening up at 5-5. None of it is theoretical — everything here has been tested on a 50+ recreational player's actual body, which is to say, mine.

Give the handbook about 90 minutes to read through. Then pick one section, work on it for two weeks, and only then move to the next. Print the cheat sheet, keep it in your racket bag, bring it to the court. I wrote this bilingually in English and Vietnamese so my Vietnamese friends could read it too — pick whichever language works for you in the header above.
