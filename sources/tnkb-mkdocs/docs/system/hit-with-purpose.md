# Hit With Purpose — The Mental Framework

Four words, in order: **Target. Focus. Commit. Execute.** A simple sequence, but it's the mental counterpart to the automation principle behind [Pre-Compensation](01.3-racquet-face-pre-compensation.md) — the body can only run on autopilot if the mind has already decided what it's doing.

## Target

Know where you want the ball to go *before* you swing — not "hit it and see." Pick the spot: deep, cross-court, down the line, drop shot. A shot without a chosen target is a shot without a purpose, and the body executes worse when the mind hasn't committed to an outcome.

## Focus

Eyes lock onto the target. Everything else — crowd, opponent, scoreboard — gets tuned out. The field of vision narrows down to just the ball and the target.

## Commit

Once the target is picked, commit to it fully. No second-guessing partway through the swing. "Hit with purpose" means no hesitation — a half-committed swing toward a target you're already doubting is worse than a fully committed swing toward the wrong one.

## Execute

Let the body do the work it has already trained to do. This is where the automation built through drilling and repetition pays off: the brain is freed from managing stroke mechanics and can instead spend its attention on tactics and controlling the match's momentum.

## Why This Works

The four steps only work in order, and only because the technical work happens elsewhere. If the racket face still has to be manipulated at contact instead of arriving pre-set — see [Pre-Compensation](01.3-racquet-face-pre-compensation.md) — the "execute" step gets hijacked by conscious correction, and the whole sequence collapses back into hesitation. This is also why grip choice matters at the mental level, not just the technical one: a grip built for stability (see [Continental & Semi-Western Grip](04.1-continental-vs-semi-western-grip.md)) reduces the number of decisions the hand has to make, leaving more of that freed-up attention for target and commitment.

In practice, this framework is what a short daily routine like the [5-Minute Shadow Drill](10.1-drill-5-minute.md) is training toward: grooving the mechanics until they're automatic enough that on court, all that's left to think about is target, focus, and commitment.
