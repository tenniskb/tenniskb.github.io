# DIT (Dynamic Inertia Transfer)

## The Concept

Use the racquet's own mass to let the racquet do the work for you.

## Static Weight vs. Swingweight

Static weight is the racquet's total mass in grams — it decides how heavy or light the racquet feels in the hand. Swingweight is the racquet's resistance to rotating around the wrist axis — it decides how the racquet actually performs during the swing.

## The Moment of Inertia Formula

$$I = \int \rho(x) \, x^2 \, dx$$

Because distance $x$ is squared in this equation, a single gram of lead tape placed at 12 o'clock on the racquet head affects swingweight many times more than the same gram placed near the handle.

## Conservation of Momentum

$$p = m \times v$$

| Racquet | Interaction With the Ball | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | The ball "pushes" the racquet backward, canceling energy | The ball feels "hollow" |
| Heavy (high swingweight) | The racquet stays steady and drives through the ball | A "heavy ball" |

## The DIT Formula

$$DIT = \frac{m_{player} \cdot I_{swing} \cdot \Delta\omega}{1}$$

The point of this: to swing a heavier racquet without slowing down, you need to use body weight together with DET to "pull" the racquet through, rather than muscling it with the arm alone.

## Lead Tape Strategy

| Placement | Effect | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 o'clock (head) | Maximum swingweight, more leverage | Harder to change direction quickly |
| 3/9 o'clock (sides) | Torsional stability against twisting | More forgiving on off-center mishits |
| Handle | Pulls the balance point back toward the hand | More maneuverable feel |

## Head-Heavy vs. Head-Light

| Type | Best For | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head-heavy | Baseline play, power shots | Maximum penetrating power |
| Head-light | Volleys, fast reactions | More agility at the net |

## Prerequisites

This only works if you already have DET in place to channel the force, NKF in place for correct timing, and a wrist stability index ($K_{wrist}$) of at least 0.85 to protect the joint.

## Watch Out

$$\tau = F_{impact} \times L_{lever}$$

That's the torque that rebounds back into the wrist on impact. If the wrist is loose without adequate stability underneath it, this is exactly what leads to tennis elbow.
