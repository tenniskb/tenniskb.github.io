# Compact Power: Hop + Hip (Big Shot From a Short Stroke)

## Two Principles

First, a very brief take-back creates momentum along with a counter-force via the hop. Second, the hip moves forward.

## Low Ball vs. High Ball

On a low ball, keep the handle low, the racquet face closed, and the top of the frame pointing down. On a high ball, keep the handle high, the racquet face still closed, and the top pointing up. In both cases the face stays closed — you change direction through racquet-face orientation, not by changing the size of the swing.

## Why This Works

The hop isn't there for height — it creates a sudden ground reaction force that you then reverse. The hip moving first is what starts the kinetic chain. The modern solution to a low ball is not opening the face and swinging harder — it's keeping the face closed and using the hop and hip to supply the lift instead.

## The Tensegrity Groundstroke (Open Stance)

On the forehand groundstroke from a semi-open or open stance: the left arm, the left shoulder, and the right shoulder set up a frame, a single plane. The right hand needs to stay on that same plane to integrate into it and create tension — pressing the racquet head down as part of that tensegrity structure. Once all four of these elements are aligned, the body simply rotates around the spine — there's no need to "hit." All you're doing is driving the racquet toward the ball and guiding it to the target.

## Recovery: Foot Position

On the forehand, one foot stays planted flat (the anchor, the front foot) while the other rests on its toes and kicks back as a counterbalance. On the backhand, the planted foot and the kicking foot reverse roles.

On the return of serve, stay low and keep the racquet away from the body.
