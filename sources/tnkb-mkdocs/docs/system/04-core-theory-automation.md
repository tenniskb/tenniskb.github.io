# Core Theory: Automation

## The Principle of "Correct Grip"

The correct grip is the one where you never feel like you have to adjust the racket face angle at contact - it happens naturally, turning the face from open to closed automatically.

## Three Consequences

### 1. Pre-Compensation Beats Manipulation

Most recreational players try to square the face right at contact. Do the opposite instead:

- **Forehand:** start palm-up, so natural pronation through the swing brings the face vertical on its own.
- **Backhand:** start palm-down ("see the watch"), so you never have to flip the face open.

Either way, there's less timing to get wrong.

### 2. Time Dictates the Engine

When there's no time - a return, a quick volley - use a small wrist flip: the thumb rotates from top to bottom and the face closes in one motion. When there's time - a baseline rally - use a big shoulder loop and keep the wrist locked in place. At 52 years old, that big-loop version is also what protects the wrist and elbow.

### 3. Volleys and Slice Serves Are Waiting Games

"Wait for the ball with the face already in the right position." You're not swinging to create the angle - you're holding it and letting the ball arrive. That's why good volleyers look like they're doing nothing at all.

## The Common Trap

Mixing the two loops - a big shoulder loop followed by a compact wrist flip at the end - closes the face too much and the ball sprays wide left. Keeping the wrist position stable throughout the big loop is the guardrail against this.

## A Practical Way to Feel It

**Standing in front of a mirror, no ball.** Take the backswing, freeze, and check your palm: on the forehand it should face the sky; on the backhand, the "watch face" should point to the sky.

**For the compact version.** Practice the thumb-top-to-thumb-bottom flip in slow motion, twenty repetitions - this is exactly the prep you need for a return of serve.

**For the big-loop version.** Shadow-swing keeping the racket face pointed at the side fence through the entire loop. If the face wobbles, you're using the wrist instead of the shoulder.

## Build Your Own Decision Tree

No time = wrist flip. Time = shoulder loop. Either way, set the face before the ball arrives.
