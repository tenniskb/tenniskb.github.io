# Tennis Analysis System & Internal Engine

This is the master notebook tying together every modern tennis technical analysis in this project, cross-referenced against an Eastern internal-power framework — Dantian (center of energy), Mingmen (the structural "gate" at the low back), and Center of Gravity.

Think of it as one integrated system rather than a pile of separate tips. Everything below links back to a small set of core ideas, and every page in this section builds toward the same picture: your body as an engineered chain that moves force from the ground into the ball with as little waste as possible.

## How the System Is Organized

**Forehand fundamentals** cover the building blocks — stance selection (open, square, closed), how much coil to use and when, setting the racquet face early instead of fixing it at contact, and keeping the wrist loose with proper ulnar deviation.

**Advanced technique** goes deeper into connection and tension — the tensegrity "cradle" for volleys, the compact hop-and-hip method for generating big power from a short stroke, continuous rotation (the "Salsa figure-8"), the optimal C-shaped arm configuration, and redirecting pace at the net rather than swinging at it.

**Foundational theory** lays out the physics underneath all of it, including the Power Wave Theory (drawn from *Tennis Without Talent*) and the continental-vs-semi-western grip debate around where power actually lives in the hand.

**Balance and posture** covers the head-position control loop — how your vestibular system and the vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR) keep your gaze locked on the ball — plus forward-head training with an 80/20 practice split.

**Eastern internal power** brings in Dantian, Mingmen, Center of Gravity, tennis-specific recovery principles, and a case study on Alcaraz's wide serve.

**Pro player case studies** break down seven different professional models: Rublev's "hydraulic press," Alcaraz's long rubber-band whip, Sinner's leather-whip racquet flip, Federer's wrist pre-stretch, Djokovic's compact kinetic chain, Fonseca's long loop and hip throw, and Kecmanovic's modern open stance.

**Personal analysis** applies the system to the author's own forehand and a basic technique video focused on the wrist drop.

**Discus and throwing** borrows the five-phase kinetic sequence from track and field as a cross-sport reference point.

**Drills** closes the loop with practical routines: a 5-minute shadow-swing session (the "5:30" position), a 15-minute pre-compensation session, and a wall-volley drill.

## Core Concepts (Quick Reference)

The whole system rests on three layers of alignment working together to produce whole-body power: the Dantian leads the energy, the Mingmen opens the structural pathway, and the Center of Gravity gets projected in the right direction mechanically.

A few golden rules run through every page in this section. Tensegrity: keep tension between adjacent joints at all times — nothing goes fully slack. Whip, don't push: the hand and arm stay relaxed and let the big muscles do the work. Pre-stretch: create lag first, then let it release. And set early: adjust the racquet face during the take-back, never at the moment of contact.
