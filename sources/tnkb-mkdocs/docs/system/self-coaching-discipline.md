# Self-Coaching Discipline — The Engineer in the Mirror

Most recreational players "coach themselves" by intuition alone. After a match: "I should have done X." After practice: "I hit Y better today." That's passive self-coaching — observation without structure. It generates the occasional insight but no actual improvement system. The disciplined version runs the same protocol every day, every week, every month, whether you feel like it or not — because the protocol works even when your mood doesn't. Four non-negotiable pillars, done consistently, produce more improvement in six months than five years of unfocused practice.

## Pillar 1 — The Daily Journal

The minimum viable version is five lines, five minutes, after every practice session, match, or recovery day:

1. **Date and activity** — "6/18 — Practice session, 60 min."
2. **Today's focus** — pulled from your [myelination](myelination.md) protocol, e.g. "Wrist firm at contact."
3. **Success rate** — "60% (18/30)."
4. **What I noticed** — one specific observation, not a vague impression. "Contact felt firm but recovery was slow — drifted into a poor ready position after contact."
5. **Tomorrow's adjustment** — one change. "Add a 'let go' cue after contact."

The loop closes on itself: today's "tomorrow's adjustment" becomes tomorrow's "today's focus." That's what turns a diary into a daily improvement cycle. Use whatever tool fits how you think — paper for tactile learners, a phone note for quick searchable logging, a voice memo if you'd rather speak the five lines right after practice, or a spreadsheet (date, focus, success%, note, adjustment) if you like seeing the data laid out.

Review the journal at three checkpoints. Day 30: look back — top 3 successes, top 3 struggles, any pattern in the success rate. Day 60: reset — keep whichever focus has the highest success rate, replace whichever has the lowest. Day 90: synthesize a one-page summary of the quarter. That page becomes your own self-coaching manual.

## Pillar 2 — The Video System

Feel is real-time but unreliable without calibration; video is after-the-fact but reliable. Self-coaching without video is self-coaching blind.

The minimum viable setup is a phone, a $20-30 tripod, and 240fps if your phone supports it — 240fps reveals the roughly 30ms contact window that's simply invisible at 30fps. Run the **3-shot drill** at least once a week: set the tripod at the baseline corner in profile, capture full body, hit three shots at 70% speed, then review at 0.25x speed and note three things — contact window, take-back shape, follow-through shape.

The review itself has a protocol: watch at normal speed first for your first impression, then at 0.25x and note what looks different, then compare it against what you *felt* in the moment — where did feel and video agree, and where did they disagree? Write one line: "My feel said ___. Video showed ___." That line is your feel-calibration data, and it accumulates. Tag every clip with date, focus, and a one-line observation, and after 90 days you have a 90-clip archive of your own progress.

## Pillar 3 — SMART Goals

"I want to be a better player" is a wish, not a goal — no specific outcome, no measurement, no deadline. Run every goal through the SMART checklist:

| Letter | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific | What exactly will you do? | "30 forehands/day, focus on wrist firm at contact." |
| Measurable | How will you know? | "Success rate measured, target 60% minimum." |
| Achievable | Is it possible given your time/energy? | "30 forehands = 10 min/day. Yes." |
| Relevant | Does it serve a bigger goal? | "Wrist firm is a prerequisite for a topspin forehand." |
| Time-bound | By when? | "Next 21 days." |

Run goals in **21-day cycles**: write and date the goal on day 0, execute and journal daily through days 1-20, then review on day 21 — did you hit 60%+ success on at least 14 of the 21 days? If yes, write the next 21-day goal; the cycle is the system. If no, the goal was probably too ambitious — adjust and try again. Four consecutive 21-day cycles stack into a 90-day arc, for example: wrist firm at contact (days 1-21) → widen the contact window (22-42) → automate retrieval of the new pattern (43-63) → integrate everything into live match play (64-90).

## Pillar 4 — The 5-Question Weekly Review

Thirty minutes, same day every week (Sunday evening works well), non-negotiable.

1. **What worked this week?** Pull the top 3 successes from journal and video, and be specific — not "I hit well" but "25/30 forehands with firm contact, 83%." This is your reinforcement: what to keep doing.
2. **What didn't work this week?** Top 3 struggles, equally specific — "Only 12/30 firm contacts (40%) on the day I was tired after a long workday." This is your diagnostic: what to investigate.
3. **What did I learn about my body this week?** One synthesized insight from journal plus video — "I perform noticeably better on high-recovery days; low-recovery days need a longer warm-up."
4. **What's my performance index this week?** Pick 3-5 measurable indicators — contact window in ms, retrieval time on a specific pattern, stroke-sequence mastery, match win/loss and unforced-error counts — and plot them weekly. The trend over time is your actual trajectory, not your mood about the week.
5. **What's next week's one focus?** Exactly one. Not two, not three — weekly attention is finite.

## Where the Pillars Meet the Rest of the System

Self-coaching isn't a fifth tool sitting next to the others — it's the operating system that runs them. The daily journal absorbs your myelin session log, your recovery notes, and whatever specific drill focus you're on. Video review is where you actually see things like grip and stance signatures, weight transfer, and recovery timing that feel invisible in real time. Each 21-day SMART goal should target one specific tool from elsewhere in this system — a myelination target, a [12-Week Master Plan](10.2-12-week-master-plan.md) phase, an item from the [Final Checklist for the Player](10.1-player-checklist.md). The weekly review is simply where all of that data gets read back to you in one sitting.

## The Five Failure Modes

1. **Skipping the journal "because I feel fine."** The most common failure. Feeling fine isn't the same as improving, and you can't improve without data.
2. **Watching video only to confirm what you already think.** Confirmation bias is the enemy of discovery — watch with the question "what is my body doing that I don't know about?" instead.
3. **Setting goals too ambitious.** "5.5 in 90 days" fails not from lack of talent but because the goal was unrealistic. Small, consistent goals beat big, abandoned ones.
4. **Skipping the weekly review.** If you don't have 30 minutes, do 5 — but don't skip it. The review is the integration step; skip it and the whole system fragments.
5. **Confusing self-coaching with self-criticism.** Three errors in a match is data, not a verdict. Data is fuel. The next match starts with new data, not old judgment.

The master cue to carry: **discipline, not mood; data, not judgment.** Every tool in this system — myelination, kinetic sequencing, recovery, the engines — is only as good as the discipline that actually uses it day to day.
