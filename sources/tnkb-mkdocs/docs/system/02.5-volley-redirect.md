# Volley: Redirect Instead of Swing

## Three Key Points From the Notebook

First, the palm isn't fully compressed against the handle — this leaves room for the racquet handle to make tiny movements inside the grip. The thumb and index finger act as "feelers," forming a soft V, while the rest of the hand simply cradles the racquet.

Second, the key is to redirect the ball. The grip stays loose, the racquet face is set early, and you step in and let the incoming pace do the work. Your job is angle, not force.

Third, brushing the back of the ball generates backspin — this is what lets you control the racquet and take the pace off the ball.

## Why a Loose Grip Beats Squeezing Tight

**Physics: absorb, don't repel.** A tight grip (8–10/10) behaves like a stiff plank — contact time is tiny, peak force is high, and the ball rebounds fast. A loose grip (3–4/10) lets the racquet head give back two or three millimeters, which lengthens contact time and bleeds off pace.

**It self-corrects mishits.** A death grip means an off-center hit torques the whole forearm. A soft hand lets the racquet pivot slightly inside the grip instead, keeping the face stable.

**Feel: the hand is a sensor, not a vise.** A tight grip floods the mechanoreceptors with pressure and drowns out feedback. A loose grip keeps the thumb pad and the base of the index finger as the primary contact points — which is exactly why good volleyers talk about "feeling the ball on the strings."

**The tension cascade.** A 9/10 grip fires the forearm flexors, which fires the biceps, which fires the shoulder — the reaction becomes slow and jerky. A soft hand keeps the forearm quiet, lets the elbow stay out in front, meets the ball out in space, and simply guides it.

## Three Common Mistakes

Squeezing at the moment of contact kills your feel and pops the ball up. Swinging from the shoulder breaks your timing and causes you to miss the sweet spot. And gluing the palm fully to the grip eliminates the mini-pivot that keeps the face stable.

## Three Steps to Build the Feel

**Off court.** Hold the racquet in a continental grip and loosen your hand until you can wiggle the handle 2–3 millimeters inside your palm using only the thumb and index finger. Tap the string bed with your other palm — you're listening for a dull thud, not a sharp clack.

**On court (wall volleys from six feet).** No backswing. Set the face, say "redirect" as the ball hits the strings. If the ball dies at your feet with backspin, you've done it correctly.
