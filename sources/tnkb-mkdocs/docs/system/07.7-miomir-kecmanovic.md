# Miomir Kecmanovic — Forehand Model (Reference)

This page is a reference case, not a full pro breakdown: Kecmanovic's open-stance forehand is used here as the clean illustration of modern open-stance defense — the exact lesson pulled from the author's own footwork footage in Surrey. See [My Forehand — Personal Analysis](08.1-my-forehand-analysis.md) for the full self-assessment this reference supports.

---

## Why This Reference

It's a near-perfect illustration of the modern open stance under time pressure — specifically, the defensive posture needed when a ball arrives fast on the forehand side and there's no time to set up a neutral or closed stance.

## What to Notice

Both knees bend deep — neither one is ever locked straight. The outside leg (right leg, for a right-hander) carries the load, with the thigh loaded like a spring. The hips sit noticeably lower than a typical recreational stance — roughly 15–20 cm lower in the comparison footage. The back stays straight and the chest stays open rather than collapsing forward. The off-hand rises for balance, and the torso is already pre-rotated, ready to fire. On clay, this posture also has to account for sliding — sitting low is partly a slide-control requirement, not just a power one.

## Compared to My Own Open Stance (Surrey, Hard Court)

Same open-stance forehand shape, same underlying intent — but the height differs. My hips stay higher; I haven't sat down into the shot the way Kecmanovic does. His stance is visibly lower and more loaded.

## Three Things to Try

**Drop one notch.** When setting up, think "sit into a chair" for about 5 cm of extra depth — not by hunching the back, by bending the knees.

**Keep the off-hand up.** Hold the left arm raised, the way Kecmanovic does, all the way until the racket makes contact — it isn't just for show, it's what keeps the shoulders pre-rotated and ready.

**Count "1-2."** One is bending the knees to build tension; two is driving off the back leg. Don't try to do both at the same time — the sequence matters as much as the depth.
