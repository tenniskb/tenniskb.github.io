# Carlos Alcaraz — Forehand Model

**Style: "The Long Elastic Band."** A high loop, a free-falling racket, and legs and hips that throw the whole thing into the ball — producing racket-head speed that looks effortless on camera. See [Forehand Technique Comparison - Rublev vs Alcaraz vs Sinner](forehand-technique-comparison-rublev-vs-alcaraz-vs-sinner.md) for how this stacks up against Rublev and Sinner; this page walks through the mechanism and the specific 2024–2025 refinements.

---

## Eight Performance Qualities

1. Semi-open stance
2. Elbow up and away from the body
3. Racket head tilted slightly forward — not standing straight up
4. Full kinetic chain used: feet, hips, upper body, arm, wrist
5. Arm extends long through the swing
6. Loose wrist to build pre-stretch before contact
7. Contact out in front of the body, driving through the ball
8. Complete follow-through

## Where the Power Actually Comes From

Tennis.com's read on it: "powerful forehand begins with his feet." At the 2021 US Open his average forehand speed was 78 mph off a compact backswing — the core coils, the arm extends at contact, and a **late release** stretches the forearm muscles into a sharp angle that accelerates the racket head at the last instant. The whip effect is the sum of three things happening in order: compact backswing, coiled core, extended arm. The forehand *looks* light. The ball does not.

## Alcaraz vs. Rublev, Side by Side

| Factor | Rublev | Alcaraz |
|---|---|---|
| Backswing construction | Left arm extends straight — a "ruler" | Off-hand stays on the racket longer, building maximum upper-body torsion |
| Main power source | Push from the trunk, butt cap pointed straight at the ball | Starts at the feet, uses weight transfer and very fast hip rotation |
| Whip mechanism | Almost no wrist lag | Whip effect plus pre-stretch |
| Trajectory and targeting | Penetrating, straight, low spin variance | Highly variable: flat 78 mph, heavy topspin, or a drop shot from the same setup |
| Off-hand after separation | Stays extended parallel to the baseline throughout | Retracts toward the chest quickly — a counterbalance for the whip |

The mechanism that makes this variability possible is [Wrist Lag and Release](wrist-lag-and-release.md): a relaxed wrist stores elastic energy passively rather than snapping deliberately, which is what lets one setup produce three different outcomes.

## Practice vs. Match Footage: The Same Model, Turned Up

Alcaraz's practice-court forehand (Australian Open footage) matches all eight performance qualities from match play, but exaggerates them — because there's no scoreboard pressure:

- Elbow rides higher
- The jump is higher
- Follow-through runs longer

## The 2024–2025 Adjustments

Coaching notes on his recent changes point to three shifts: lifting the right foot higher, extending the left arm deeper, and — the key phrase — deliberately "loading the back hip upward" rather than simply pushing up through the shoulder. In the vocabulary of the [Dantian-Mingmen-COG Framework](dantian-mingmen-cog-framework.md), this is the dantian and mingmen doing the loading, not the shoulder muscling it.

**Why his wide-serve recovery is faster than most players':**

- The dantian activates at the moment of the toss — the deep reach of the left arm stretches the obliques and diaphragm, so by the time the arm comes down, the dantian has already begun rotating before the right hip fires.
- The mingmen acts as the spring in the lower back: lifting the right foot higher creates more flexion at the waist, stretching the thoracolumbar fascia.
- The center of gravity projects diagonally inward rather than being muscled back — the left foot lands 30–40 cm further inside the court than the contact point. See [Recovery Mechanics](recovery-mechanics.md) for the full breakdown of this recovery pattern.

## If You Want to Copy Alcaraz

Don't copy the speed. Copy the order of operations:

1. Set the semi-open stance first.
2. Bring the elbow up the instant the shoulders start turning.
3. Let the racket head tilt slightly forward — don't stand it straight up.
4. Relax the wrist and let the legs drive first, arm second.

Slow it down to match the 3.2–4.3 second window in the source footage, and you'll feel the whip without needing to swing hard.
