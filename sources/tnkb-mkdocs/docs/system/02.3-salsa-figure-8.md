# Salsa Figure-8: Continuous Rotation

## The Concept

Instead of the usual "turn, hit, recover" sequence, map your shoulders onto four corners and rotate through them in the shape of a figure 8.

## The Four Corners

Corner 1, top-left, is left-shoulder preparation. Corner 4, top-right, is right-shoulder preparation. Corner 2, bottom-left, is the left shoulder at full coil. Corner 3, bottom-right, is the right shoulder at full coil.

## The Sequence

Turn the torso 45° and the left shoulder sits at position 1 while the right shoulder sits at position 3. Turn the torso to 90° and the left shoulder moves to position 2 while the right shoulder moves to position 4. Turn back 90° and you return to the start — left shoulder back at 1, right shoulder back at 3.

## The Upper Body Pattern

The upper body rotates through this figure-8 pattern in rhythm, and because it's a figure 8, there's no stopping point. The left shoulder traces 1 → 2 → 1 while the right traces 3 → 4 → 3, all in the same rhythm — exactly how salsa dancers keep their momentum going, and exactly how pros keep the upper body flowing between shots instead of resetting stiffly after each one.

## Why This Works

No stop means no deceleration. Continuous rotation means a fluid whip. This connects directly to the tensegrity cradle concept, which supplies the pre-tension that this rotation releases.
