# Approach Shot Strategy Beyond the Slice

*A superset of Slice Approach — The Senior-Friendly Way to the Net, covering the full
approach-shot decision tree.*

## Why This Matters

The site's existing Slice Approach content covers one excellent, low-impact way to approach net.
But "approach shot" is a broader tactical category, and knowing when a flat/topspin approach,
swinging volley, or serve-and-volley beats the slice approach rounds out your net game instead of
giving you one tool for every situation.

## Core Concepts

**The approach-shot decision tree.** Ball height and depth at the moment you're approaching
determine the right shot: a low or skidding ball favors the slice (already well covered on this
site); a shoulder-high or short ball favors a flat or topspin approach, hit with pace and depth to
follow behind; a very short, sitting ball can be taken as a swinging volley approach, closing even
faster than a standard groundstroke approach allows.

**Serve-and-volley and chip-and-charge** are approach-shot tactics in their own right, not just
retro curiosities. Serve-and-volley on a well-placed first serve, or chipping a return and
following it in against a weak second serve, both apply approach-shot principles (depth,
direction, splitting on the opponent's shot) before the point has even become a groundstroke
rally.

**Surface and split-step timing matter as much as shot selection.** Faster surfaces reward more
approach attempts since the opponent has less time to pass; on any surface, splitting your feet
exactly as the opponent makes contact (not before, not after) is what actually determines whether
the first volley is comfortable or rushed — the approach shot only sets up the point, the split
step wins it.

## Action Steps

1. **Practice reading ball height before choosing the approach shot** — treat "always slice the
   approach" as one tool among three or four, selected by the ball you're given.
2. **Add one serve-and-volley or chip-and-charge point per practice set** specifically against a
   weaker second serve, to build comfort with committing forward before the rally develops.
3. **Drill the split-step timing separately from the approach shot itself** — many missed first
   volleys are a timing problem, not a technique problem.

## Common Mistakes

- Using the same approach shot (usually slice) regardless of ball height, when a shoulder-high
  ball calls for a different tool.
- Approaching without committing to the split-step timing, arriving at net already off-balance.
- Treating serve-and-volley/chip-and-charge as outdated rather than as situational tools that are
  especially effective against weaker servers or returners.

## Quick Reference

| Ball You Receive | Best Approach Option |
|---|---|
| Low or skidding | Slice approach (see Slice Approach Coaching Guide) |
| Shoulder-high / short | Flat or topspin approach with depth |
| Very short, sitting | Swinging volley approach |
| Weak opponent 2nd serve | Chip-and-charge |
| Strong own 1st serve | Serve-and-volley |
