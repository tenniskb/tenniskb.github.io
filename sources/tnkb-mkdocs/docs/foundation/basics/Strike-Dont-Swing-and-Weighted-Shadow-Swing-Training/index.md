# "Strike, Don't Swing" & Weighted Shadow-Swing Training

*A perception-action model for contact, plus the drill that trains it.*

## Why This Matters

Most technical cues focus on the shape of the swing. This concept flips the focus to the moment
of contact itself: aim the strike, don't pre-visualize the swing. It's a subtle shift with a real
effect on timing and adaptability against balls that don't arrive exactly as expected.

## Core Concepts

**Late commitment to a strike vector, not an early commitment to a swing shape.** Committing early
to "what my swing will look like" locks you into a plan that breaks down the moment the ball's
speed, spin, or bounce differs from what you predicted. Committing instead to "where and how I'll
strike the ball" as late as possible keeps you adaptable through the last moment before contact —
the swing becomes a means to deliver that strike, not a fixed pattern you're performing regardless
of what the ball actually does.

**Why this matters more as balls get faster or spin gets heavier.** A pre-committed swing shape is
most exposed exactly when the incoming ball is least predictable — heavy spin, awkward bounce, or
unexpected pace. A strike-focused approach is more robust here because the "target" (the strike)
can be adjusted later than a full swing pattern can.

**Weighted shadow-swing training** as the drill that builds this feel: performing your stroke
motion with a slightly weighted racket (or added grip weight) without a ball, at reduced speed,
focusing entirely on the proprioceptive feel of the kinetic chain delivering the racket to a
strike point — not on hitting a target. This builds the body-awareness that "strike, don't swing"
requires, since you can't rely on ball feedback to correct you.

## Action Steps

1. **Practice the cue explicitly during rally drills**: mentally say "strike," not "swing," as the
   ball approaches, and notice whether your preparation feels more adaptable.
2. **Add weighted shadow swings 2-3x/week**, slow and controlled, focusing purely on the felt
   sequence of the kinetic chain — no ball involved.
3. **Progress weight and speed gradually.** Start light and slow; increasing weight or speed too
   quickly risks poor movement patterns being grooved in rather than good ones.
4. **Apply the late-commitment principle specifically against unfamiliar spin or pace** in
   practice — this is where the difference between "strike" and "swing" thinking shows up most.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating "strike, don't swing" as purely a mental cue with no physical training behind it —
  the weighted shadow-swing work is what actually builds the proprioceptive feel.
- Adding too much weight or speed too quickly in shadow-swing training, risking poor technique
  patterns.
- Reverting to full swing-shape commitment under match pressure, precisely when the adaptive
  benefit matters most.

## Quick Reference

| Model | Commits To | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Swing-shape commitment | A pre-visualized full swing | Breaks down against unpredictable balls |
| Strike commitment | Where/how to contact the ball, decided late | Requires more proprioceptive training first |
