# Slice, Drop Shot & Lob

The Touch & Disruption Game

Not every shot is about power. The slice disrupts rhythm. The drop shot breaks positioning. The lob buys time or attacks.

## Why Touch Shots Matter

*Power wins points. Touch wins matches. The player who can change pace, spin, and height controls the opponent.*

— Henry Pham

These three shots share one DNA: they use the opponent's pace against them. They're Hua Jin shots — absorb and redirect.

| V        | Vestibular | The head stays still — especially critical on the slice (side-on) and the drop shot (disguise).         |

| Brake    | Impulse    | No hard brake — soft absorption. Hua Jin means transforming the pace.                                   |

RULE: Touch shots run on constant firmness (4 to 5 out of 10). No gradient. The face locks early. Hua Jin — absorb and redirect.

## Slice (Backhand and Forehand)

A slice isn't a chop downward. It's a controlled glide with a slightly open face, moving outside-to-inside on the backhand or inside-to-outside on the forehand.

| Element | Backhand Slice                        | Forehand Slice                            |
| ------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Grip    | Continental or Eastern backhand       | Continental                               |
| Face    | Slightly open (5 to 10 degrees)       | Slightly open (5 to 10 degrees)           |
| Path    | High to low, outside to in            | High to low, inside to out                |
| Wrist   | Firm extension, locked                | Firm extension, locked                    |
| Contact | In front, on the side                 | In front, slightly across                 |
| Finish  | High, across the body                 | High, across the body                     |
| Key     | The non-dominant hand guides the edge | The non-dominant hand rests on the throat |

**Coach's Tip.** Slice error number one: opening the face too much, so the ball floats long. The fix: feel the pinky edge leading. The face squares at contact naturally. A slice isn't "open face" — it's "edge leads, face follows."

## Drop Shot

*The drop shot isn't a soft shot. It's a disguised shot. The opponent has to think drive until it's too late.*

— Henry Pham

| Element        | Detail                                                                           |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grip           | Continental on both sides                                                        |
| Face           | Open 20 to 30 degrees at contact                                                 |
| Path           | Short, compact — high to low to high, tiny                                       |
| Disguise       | Same prep as a drive. Unit turn, racket back. Drop the wrist at the last moment. |
| Contact        | In front, waist-high. Absorb the pace.                                           |
| Target         | Just over the net, 1 to 2 meters inside the service line                         |
| Follow-through | Short, freeze. The racket face stays visible.                                    |
| When           | The opponent is deep and expecting a drive — not when they're already in.        |

PRINCIPLE: Drop shot means the 3-1 drill. Hit three drives, one drop. Same prep. The opponent cannot read it.

**Coach's Tip.** If you feel a wrist flick on the drop shot, you're muscling it. A drop shot means relaxing — let the racket head drop from gravity. The face opens. The ball kisses the strings.

## Lob

| Type          | When                          | Technique                                     | Target                                   |
| ------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Defensive lob | Pulled wide, on the run       | Open face, high finish, just get it up        | Deep, high, buy time                     |
| Offensive lob | Opponent at net, you balanced | Topspin brush up, 45-degree plane, firm wrist | Over the net player, near the baseline   |
| Topspin lob   | Neutral or attacking          | Brush up the back of the ball, closed face    | Deep with kick, pushes the opponent back |

The offensive lob is a topspin shot, not a moonball. Brush up at 45 degrees. The face closes through contact.

## Fault Signals

| Shot      | Symptom               | Root Cause                          | Fix                                               |
| --------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Slice     | Floats long           | The face is too open                | The pinky edge leads, the face squares at contact |
| Slice     | Into the net          | Contact is late or low              | Take contact further in front, bend the knees     |
| Drop shot | The opponent reads it | Different prep from the drive       | The 3-1 drill: same prep, drop at the last moment |
| Drop shot | Into the net          | Too much cut, or the face is closed | Open the face more, softer touch, higher contact  |
| Drop shot | Too long              | Too much pace, or the face is open  | Absorb more pace, softer hands                    |
| Lob       | Short or smashed      | No topspin, or the face is open     | Brush up, close the face, a 45-degree plane       |
| Lob       | Too long              | Too much power                      | Touch, not power — brush up.                      |

## Drills

1. Slice wall: hit 50 slices against a wall. Focus on the edge leading, a constant face, and rhythm.

2. The 3-1 drill: your partner feeds. Three drives, one drop. Same prep. 20 cycles.

3. Drop and pass: you drop, your partner runs in, you pass. This trains disguise plus the follow-up.

4. Lob practice: your partner is at net. You hit offensive topspin lobs. Target: the baseline corner.

5. Touch circuit: backhand slice, forehand slice, backhand drop, forehand drop, offensive lob — five reps each. Focus on constant firmness and face control.

## Checklist

- Slice: Continental grip, edge leads, face constant, high to low, finish high.

- Drop shot: same prep as the drive, Continental grip, open face at contact, absorb the pace, freeze the finish.

- Lob: offensive means a topspin brush up. Defensive means an open face, high finish.

- All touch shots: a firm wrist (4 to 5 out of 10), constant, no gradient. Hua Jin.

- Disguise: the 3-1 drill. The opponent cannot read it.

- If the slice floats, the face is too open. If the drop nets, there was too much cut. If the lob falls short, there's no topspin.
