# Court Geometry & Shot Selection

Tactics

The court dictates. You don't choose shots — the geometry chooses for you.

## The Court Is a Math Problem

*78 feet long, 27 feet wide for singles, a 3-foot net at center. Every shot is a geometry solution.*

— Henry Pham

Court geometry creates mandatory shot windows. You don't "decide" crosscourt versus down the line — the position of the ball and your own position dictate the high-percentage play.

## The Five Zones

| Zone            | Location                                | Default Play                             | Why                                                                           | Risk                  |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| 1. Deep rally  | Behind the baseline                     | Crosscourt deep                          | The longest distance, the most margin over the net, pulls the opponent wide   | Low                   |
| 2. Mid-court   | On or inside the baseline               | Crosscourt or inside-out                 | You can attack but still need margin. An inside-out forehand is a weapon here | Medium                |
| 3. Attack zone | 1 to 2m inside the baseline             | Down the line or a sharp angle           | A short distance to the target, the net is lower, time to take time away      | Medium to high        |
| 4. Transition  | No man's land, service line to baseline | A deep approach shot, then come to net   | Don't stay here — hit and move forward                                        | High if you get stuck |
| 5. Net         | Inside the service line                 | A volley to the open court or a put-away | The shortest distance, huge angles, the first volley stays deep               | Low if executed well  |

RULE: Deep means crosscourt. Short means down the line or an angle. The geometry is not negotiable.

## Wardlaw's Directionals (Simplified)

| Incoming Ball          | Your Position            | High-Percentage Response                  | When to Change                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Crosscourt deep        | Deep                     | Crosscourt deep                           | Only if short or weak — attack down the line         |
| Crosscourt deep        | Mid-court                | Crosscourt deep or inside-out             | If weak, attack down the line                        |
| Down the line          | Deep                     | Crosscourt (change direction)             | Only if you have time and position                   |
| Down the line          | Mid-court                | Crosscourt, or down the line if attacking | Attack down the line if balanced                     |
| Short ball (mid-court) | Any                      | Attack down the line or a sharp angle     | Don't hit crosscourt short — it gives away the angle |
| High floating ball     | Mid-court or attack zone | Put away down the line or an angle        | Don't lob unless defending                           |

**Coach's Tip.** Directionals aren't rules — they're probability maps. Crosscourt deep makes about 80% of the time. Down the line deep makes about 50%. Play the math until the ball gives you permission to attack.

## The 4-Shot Rally Pattern (70% of Points)

- Serve or return.

- Serve+1 or Return+1 — the first groundstroke after the serve or return.

- Serve+2 or Return+2 — the second groundstroke.

- The point ends: a winner, an error, or a net approach.

The first four shots decide 70% of points. Tactics means optimizing Serve+1 and Return+1.

| Phase     | Server                         | Returner                    | Goal                               |
| --------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Serve     | Target a weakness, set up S+1  | Neutralize, a deep return   | Start the point on your terms      |
| S+1 / R+1 | Forehand if possible. Dictate. | Crosscourt deep, neutralize | Control the center of the court    |
| S+2 / R+2 | Attack short, finish at net    | Counter-attack or defend    | Take time away or extend the point |
| Finish    | Volley or overhead put-away    | Pass or lob                 | End the point                      |

## Shot Selection Decision Tree

At contact, ask in this order:

1. Can I attack? (Short, high, mid-court.) Yes: attack down the line or an angle. No: go to 2.

2. Can I hurt them? (Opponent off-balance, a weak side.) Yes: target the weakness. No: go to 3.

3. Can I neutralize? (Deep, heavy.) Crosscourt deep, reset. No: go to
4.

4. Can I survive? (Stretched, late.) A high crosscourt ball or a lob. Buy time.

RULE: The default is crosscourt deep. Only deviate when the ball gives you a green light — short, high, weak, or the opponent vulnerable.

## Court Positioning: Where You Stand Changes Everything

| Your Position          | Effective Net Height      | Target Window          | Required Face     | Default Shot                          |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 3m behind the baseline | Low (3 ft)                | Large (3 to 6 ft over) | Slightly open     | Heavy crosscourt                      |
| On the baseline        | Medium (3.5 ft)           | Medium (2 to 3 ft)     | Neutral to closed | Crosscourt deep                       |
| 1m inside the baseline | High (looks 4 ft or more) | Small (1 to 2 ft)      | Closed            | Down-the-line attack or a sharp angle |
| At net                 | Very high — must clear    | Tiny — clearance only  | Slightly open     | An angled volley or a put-away        |

## Recovery Positioning

- After a crosscourt shot: recover to the center of your own crosscourt angle, not the center of the court.

- After a down-the-line shot: recover slightly toward the down-the-line side, since you opened up the crosscourt.

- After an approach shot: move to net, at the center of the angle bisector.

- Never recover to "the middle of the baseline." Recover to the middle of your own angle.

**Coach's Tip.** Recovery position is the bisector of the opponent's possible replies. If you hit crosscourt, the opponent can go crosscourt or down the line. You recover to cover the middle of that range.

## Tactical Patterns by Player Type

| Opponent Type        | Their Pattern                         | Your Counter                                                         | Key Shots                             |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Baseline grinder     | Deep crosscourt, waiting for an error | Move them: inside-out forehand, down-the-line backhand, short angles | Drop shot, down-the-line attack, net  |
| Aggressive baseliner | A big forehand, down-the-line attacks | High-percentage crosscourt, take time away, serve at the body        | Heavy spin, deep, serve wide          |
| Serve and volley     | Serve wide, angled volley             | Return low at their feet, pass crosscourt, lob                       | Return at the feet, lob, passing shot |
| Pusher or moonballer | High, deep, no pace                   | Step in, take it early, attack the net                               | Drive volley, overhead, drop shot     |
| All-court player     | Varied, unpredictable                 | Disrupt the rhythm, serve patterns, first strike                     | Serve+1, Return+1, variety            |

## Tactics Checklist

DEEP MEANS CROSSCOURT. SHORT MEANS ATTACK. NET MEANS ANGLE. RECOVER TO THE BISECTOR.

- Default rally ball: crosscourt deep, 80%+ make rate.

- Attack down the line only on: a short ball, a weak reply, an off-balance opponent, or when you're inside the baseline.

- Change direction on a crosscourt ball only when you're balanced and the ball is attackable.

- Recover to the bisector of the opponent's possible replies, not the center of the court.

- Serve+1: forehand if possible. Dictate the direction.

- Return+1: neutralize. Deep crosscourt. Deny the server's S+1.

- The first four shots decide 70% of points. Win the S+1 / R+1 battle.

- Against grinders: move them (angles, drop shots, net). Against attackers: high percentage, deep, take time away.
