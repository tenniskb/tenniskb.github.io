# Physical

Tennis-Specific Fitness & Periodization

Tennis isn't a marathon. It's 100 to 300 sprints of 3 to 7 seconds each, with 20 to 25 seconds of rest. Train the energy systems, not the distance.

## Energy Systems: the Engine

*If you train like a marathoner, you play like a marathoner — slow, tired, with no explosive power. Tennis runs on ATP-PC plus lactate plus aerobic recovery.*

— Henry Pham

Tennis demands three energy systems in specific ratios:

| System                         | Duration  | Intensity                   | Tennis Role                                                | Training                                                                           |
| ------------------------------ | --------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ATP-PC (phosphagen)            | 0 to 10s  | Maximal (100%)              | Every shot: serve, return, first strike, put-away          | Short sprints (5 to 10m), medicine ball throws, plyometrics, 1 to 5 rep power work |
| Lactate (anaerobic glycolysis) | 15 to 60s | High (85 to 95%)            | Long rallies (15 to 30s), repeated high-intensity points   | Intervals: 20 to 30s work, 60 to 90s rest. 400m repeats, court suicides            |
| Aerobic (oxidative)            | 60s+      | Low to moderate (60 to 75%) | Recovery between points, endurance for 2 to 3-hour matches | Zone 2 steady state (30 to 60 min), long intervals (3 to 5 min)                    |

RULE: Tennis is ATP-PC (sprint) plus lactate (rally) plus aerobic (recovery). Train all three. Marathon running kills ATP-PC power.

## The Five Physical Pillars for Tennis

| Pillar                                | What It Is                                        | Tennis Application                                   | Key Exercises                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Power (rate of force development) | How fast you can apply force                      | Serve speed, the first step, explosive groundstrokes | Olympic lifts (clean and snatch), medicine ball throws, box jumps, plyometric push-ups                   |
| 2. Speed and agility                 | Acceleration, deceleration, change of direction   | The split step, recovery, court coverage             | Ladder drills, the 5-10-5 shuttle, cone change-of-direction drills, resisted sprints                     |
| 3. Repeat sprint ability (RSA)       | Maintain power across 100 to 300 efforts          | Point after point, set after set, match after match  | Repeated 10 to 20m sprints (6 to 10 reps, 20s rest), court suicides                                      |
| 4. Rotational core                   | Transfer force from legs to trunk to arm          | Every groundstroke, serve, overhead                  | Medicine ball rotational throws, Pallof press, Russian twists, cable chops                               |
| 5. Prehab and resilience             | Joint health, tendon capacity, managing asymmetry | Longevity: shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle  | Rotator cuff (ER and IR), scapular stability, wrist and forearm work, hip mobility, ankle proprioception |

## Session Design: the Two-Hour Tennis-Specific Session

| Phase                       | Time   | Content                                                                       | Focus                                                        |
| --------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Warm-up                 | 15 min | Dynamic mobility, band activation, short sprints, shadow strokes              | Neuromuscular priming. Core temperature. Movement prep.      |
| 2. Technical               | 40 min | Blocked, then variable, then random drills. Two to three shots maximum focus. | Motor learning. Quality over quantity.                       |
| 3. Tactical or situational | 30 min | Point play with constraints (serve+1, return+1, patterns)                     | Decision making. Pressure simulation.                        |
| 4. Physical                | 20 min | Power, RSA, core, prehab — tennis-specific, not general fitness               | Energy system development. Injury prevention.                |
| 5. Cool-down               | 15 min | Static stretch, foam roll, breathing, review                                  | Parasympathetic reset. Tissue quality. Mental consolidation. |

## Periodization: the Yearly Plan

Periodization aligns physical peaks with the competitive calendar:

| Phase                    | Duration      | Focus                                            | Volume         | Intensity      | Tennis Focus                                                          |
| ------------------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------- | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Off-season (transition)  | 4 to 8 weeks  | Recovery, general physical prep, fix asymmetries | High           | Low to medium  | Technical rebuild. High-volume tennis. No matches.                    |
| Pre-season (preparation) | 8 to 12 weeks | Strength to power to RSA. Build the engine.      | Medium to high | Medium to high | Pattern drilling. High volume. Simulated matches.                     |
| Pre-competition          | 4 to 6 weeks  | Peak power, speed, RSA. Sharpen.                 | Low to medium  | Very high      | Match play. Tactics. Mental game. Taper volume.                       |
| In-season (competition)  | Ongoing       | Maintain power, manage fatigue                   | Low            | High (matches) | Match prep. Recovery priority. One to two maintenance lifts per week. |
| Peak (major event)       | 1 to 2 weeks  | Supercompensation. Fresh.                        | Very low       | High (quality) | Taper. Mental game. Visualization. Sleep. Nutrition.                  |

RULE: You can't peak year-round. Periodize. The off-season builds. The pre-season converts. The in-season maintains. Peak for two to three majors a year.

## Recovery: the Invisible Training

| Modality        | When                 | Protocol                                                                   | Why                                               |
| --------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Sleep           | Nightly              | 8 to 10 hours. Cool, dark. A consistent wake time.                         | Growth hormone, CNS recovery, motor consolidation |
| Nutrition       | Around workouts      | Protein at 1.6 to 2.2g/kg. Carbs matched to load. Stay hydrated.           | Glycogen, repair, inflammation control            |
| Cold or heat    | Post-match           | Cold water immersion at 10 to 15°C for 10 min, or a sauna for 15 to 20 min | Inflammation, HRV, parasympathetic activity       |
| Compression     | Post-match or travel | Normatec or compression garments for 20 to 30 min                          | Venous return, metabolite clearance               |
| Breathing / HRV | Every morning        | 4-7-8 breathing, five reps. Track HRV.                                     | Autonomic balance, readiness                      |
| Soft tissue     | Daily                | Foam roll, lacrosse ball, banded mobility                                  | Tissue quality, range of motion                   |

**Coach's Tip.** If you don't measure it, you can't manage it. Track: HRV (daily), RPE (per session), sleep (hours), soreness (1 to 10), power output (medicine ball throw or sprint time, weekly). Adjust load based on the trends.

## Age 40+ / 50+ Adjustments

| Factor          | Adjustment                                                             | Why                                                     |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recovery speed  | More rest days. A 2:1 work-to-rest ratio.                              | Slower tissue repair, lower HRV                         |
| Tendon capacity | More isometrics, slower eccentrics. Heavy, slow resistance.            | Collagen turnover is slower. Tendinopathy risk.         |
| Power           | Maintain with lower volume, higher quality. 3 to 5 rep maximums.       | Motor unit loss. Quality over quantity.                 |
| Mobility        | Daily, 10 to 15 minutes. Hip, thoracic spine, ankle, shoulder.         | Fascial stiffness increases. Movement options decrease. |
| Strength        | Twice a week, full body. Compound lifts. 3 to 5 reps. RPE 7 to 8.      | Sarcopenia. Bone density. Metabolic health.             |
| Tennis volume   | Shorter, sharper sessions. 90 minutes max. More drills, less grinding. | Recovery cost per hour is higher.                       |

PRINCIPLE: Masters tennis isn't "less tennis." It's smarter tennis — higher quality, better recovery, specificity over volume.

## Physical Checklist

ENERGY: ATP-PC PLUS LACTATE PLUS AEROBIC. PILLARS: POWER, SPEED, RSA, ROTATIONAL CORE, PREHAB. PERIODIZE: OFF TO PRE TO COMPETITION TO PEAK. RECOVER: SLEEP, NUTRITION, COLD, BREATHE, SOFT TISSUE.

- Train all three energy systems: ATP-PC (sprints), lactate (intervals), aerobic (zone 2).

- Five pillars weekly: power, speed and agility, RSA, rotational core, prehab.

- Periodize: off-season builds, pre-season converts, in-season maintains, peak tapers.

- Weekly microcycle: high, low, high, low, medium, match, off.

- Recover daily: sleep 8 to 10 hours, nutrition, breathing, soft tissue.

- Track: HRV, RPE, sleep, soreness, power output. Adjust load.

- 40+/50+: more recovery, more prehab, maintain power with quality, daily mobility.
