# The CORE System

How Court and Ball Flow Into Grip, Contact, Takeback, and Tension

Every shot is one loop: the court tells you what face you need. The ball tells you what takeback you get. You deliver both with grip, contact, and tension.

*Court plus ball gives you the problem. Grip plus contact plus takeback plus tension is your solution. The loop closes at contact.*

— Henry Pham

## The CORE Equation

The CORE loop runs in real time, on every single shot:

CORE loop: court plus ball → required face → grip plus contact → takeback plus tension → deliver the face at contact → the result feeds the next loop.

| Variable | Input                        | Function                                   | Key Rule                                                |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| C        | Court position + ball height | Dictates the required face angle at impact | Deep = open, inside = closed, low = open, high = closed |
| O        | Oncoming ball speed          | Dictates takeback size                     | Fast = short, slow = full, medium = medium              |
| R        | Racket system — grip         | Sets baseline face angle                   | Choose so ideal contact = required face                 |
| E        | Energy — takeback + tension  | Stores and transmits energy to the face    | Match loop to time, gradient to contact                 |

## The Decision Hierarchy (0.8 Seconds)

Your brain processes this in a fixed order. You can't skip steps.

| Time   | Phase                    | Decision                                  | Output                                        | If Wrong                                        |
| ------ | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| -0.8s | Opponent contact         | Read trajectory, speed, spin              | Court position + ball height + speed          | Wrong position = wrong face window              |
| -0.6s | Ball crosses net         | Confirm height, finalize face requirement | Required face angle at impact                 | Wrong face = unfixable later                    |
| -0.5s | Unit turn + split step   | Select grip, start takeback               | Grip set, takeback size initiated             | Late turn = rushed takeback                     |
| -0.3s | Ball bounces (your side) | Takeback complete, forward swing starts   | Takeback size locked, tension gradient begins | Still taking back = late                        |
| -0.1s | Forward swing            | Tension gradient ramps up                 | Peak tension at contact                       | Tight early = no power; loose late = no control |
| 0ms    | Contact                  | Face delivered, tension peaks             | Ball launched with required face              | Wrong face = wrong ball                         |
| +0.1s | Hold                     | Quiet Eye holds, tension drops            | Face stable through the exit                  | Early peek = face opens                         |
| +0.3s | Release + recover        | Head releases, feet reset                 | Ready for the next loop                       | Stuck = slow recovery                           |

**The Rule.** The loop is sequential: court, then ball, then face, then grip, then contact, then takeback, then tension. Skip one link and the chain breaks.

## Integration Example: Same Ball, Different Court Positions

Take a waist-high ball arriving at 80 km/h. The required face is the same everywhere: slightly closed, about 3 degrees. But your position changes everything else about how you deliver it.

| Court Position                  | Required Face        | Grip Choice                        | Contact Point           | Takeback              | Tension Key                     |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Deep (4m behind baseline)       | Slightly closed (3°) | Semi-Western (natural 3° at waist) | Front foot, waist-high  | Full loop, high elbow | Loose drop, firm at contact     |
| Baseline (on the line)          | Slightly closed (3°) | Semi-Western                       | Front foot, waist-high  | Medium loop           | Loose drop, firm contact        |
| Inside (1m inside the baseline) | Slightly closed (3°) | Semi-Western or Eastern            | Further in front, waist | Short, compact loop   | Firmer earlier, punch feel      |
| At net (volley position)        | N/A — volley instead | Continental                        | In front, chest-high    | No takeback, just set | Firm from the start, block/push |

**Coach's Note.** Same ball, four positions, four different solutions. The ball didn't change. Your position changed the required face's context, which changed the grip, contact, takeback, and tension solution. That's CORE in action.

## Integration Example: Same Position, Different Balls

Now flip it. You're standing at the baseline, and three different balls arrive.

| Incoming Ball           | Required Face           | Grip                 | Contact                | Takeback            | Tension                  |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Low, slow (knee height) | Slightly open (2° open) | Eastern/Semi-Western | Front foot, low        | Full loop, low drop | Loose drop, push up      |
| Waist, medium (rally)   | 3° closed               | Semi-Western         | Front foot, waist      | Medium loop         | Loose drop, firm contact |
| High, fast (shoulder+)  | 6° closed               | Semi-Western         | Further in front, high | Short, high loop    | Firm earlier, punch down |

**The Principle.** Same position, different balls, means different face requirements, which means different grip, contact, takeback, and tension solutions. The ball dictates.

## The CORE Loop in Practice: The Three-Second Ritual

Between points, run the CORE loop consciously:

1. Where am I? (Deep / Baseline / Inside / Net) — sets your face tendency.

2. What ball is coming? (Fast/Medium/Slow, High/Medium/Low) — sets your takeback and face.

3. What face do I need? (Open/Neutral/Closed) — confirms grip and contact zone.

4. Where is my contact point? (Front foot, height, side) — move your feet now.

5. What takeback? (None/Short/Medium/Full) — sets your loop size.

6. Tension plan: "loose drop, firm at contact, loose finish."

COURT → BALL → FACE → GRIP → CONTACT → TAKEBACK → TENSION → EXECUTE.

## Common CORE Breakdowns

When something in the chain breaks, it shows up as a specific, recognizable symptom.

| Breakdown               | Symptom                                        | Broken Link                                        | Fix                                                  |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Wrong face for position | Long/net errors in specific zones              | Court → face mapping is wrong                      | Drill: position-specific face windows                |
| Late takeback           | Rushed, jammed, muscled swing                  | Ball speed → takeback link is broken               | Split step earlier, turn on opponent contact         |
| Grip mismatch           | Consistent long or net errors on the same side | Grip → face baseline is wrong                      | Test: freeze at ideal contact, check the face        |
| Contact point drift     | Inconsistent depth or spin                     | Contact → face tuning is broken                    | Drill: call your contact point out loud for 20 balls |
| Tension wrong           | No power, or no control                        | Tension gradient is broken                         | Shadow: 3/10 → 6/10 at contact → 3/10                |
| Early head lift         | Face opens at contact                          | Quiet Eye / energy link breaks → face control lost | Quiet Eye drill: "park, hold, release"               |

## The CORE Drill: Position Plus Ball Equals Solution

Partner feeds from a basket. Before each feed, you call out loud:

"Position: [Deep/Baseline/Inside/Net]. Ball: [Fast/Medium/Slow, High/Medium/Low]. Face: [Open/Neutral/Closed]. Grip: [Continental/Eastern/Semi-Western/Western]. Contact: [where]. Takeback: [None/Short/Medium/Full]. Tension: [loose/firm/loose]."

- Partner feeds. You execute. Partner confirms: "yes," or corrects one variable.

- 20 balls. Goal: 18 out of 20 perfect verbal calls plus execution.

- Advanced version: partner varies the feed randomly, so you have to adapt the CORE loop in real time.

**The Rule.** The CORE loop isn't theory. It's the real-time decision engine. Saying it out loud makes it conscious. Automating it makes it fast.

COURT → BALL → FACE → GRIP → CONTACT → TAKEBACK → TENSION → EXECUTE.

## Key Takeaways

- Before every point, ask: where am I, what ball, what face, what grip, where's my contact, what takeback, what tension?

- Court position determines face tendency: deep means open, inside means closed.

- Ball speed determines takeback size: fast means short, slow means full.

- Choose your grip so your ideal contact point matches the required face.

- Move your feet to bring the ball into that ideal contact zone.

- Finish the takeback by the bounce.

- Tension: 3/10, up to 6/10 at contact, back to 3/10.

- Park your Quiet Eye on the contact zone from the bounce all the way through contact.
