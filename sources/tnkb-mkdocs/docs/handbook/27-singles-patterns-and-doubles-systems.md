# Singles Patterns & Doubles Systems

Strategy

Patterns are repeatable sequences. Systems are coordinated team behaviors. Both beat raw talent.

## Singles: the Five Core Patterns

*Tennis isn't random. It's five patterns, repeated a hundred times. Master the patterns and you master the match.*

— Henry Pham

Every singles point follows one of these patterns. Recognize it, execute it, repeat it.

| Pattern | Name                | Sequence                                                                             | When to Use                                          | Key Shot                                |
| ------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 1       | Serve+1 Forehand    | Serve wide or at the body, run around the backhand, forehand inside-out or inside-in | Most points. Your forehand is a weapon.              | S+1 forehand dictation                  |
| 2       | Return+1 Neutralize | Return deep crosscourt, rally crosscourt, wait for a short ball                      | Against big servers. Deny their S+1.                 | R+1 depth                               |
| 3       | First-Strike Attack | A short return or weak reply, attack down the line or an angle, come to net          | The opponent gives you a short ball — a green light. | Attack shot plus approach               |
| 4       | Grind and Disrupt   | Deep crosscourt, then a drop shot, angle, or net approach                            | The opponent is stuck deep. Break the rhythm.        | Drop shot or a sharp angle              |
| 5       | Defend and Counter  | Pulled wide, a high crosscourt ball or a lob, recover, counter                       | You're in trouble. Buy time.                         | Defensive lob or a high crosscourt ball |

RULE: Pattern 1 (Serve+1 forehand) and Pattern 2 (Return+1 neutralize) make up 80% of pro tennis. Master these first.

## Serve+1 Patterns: the Weapon

| Serve Target       | S+1 Shot                                      | Goal                                                          | If the Opponent...                              |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Wide (deuce or ad) | Forehand inside-out                           | Pull the opponent off court, open the court for the next shot | Returns crosscourt: a forehand inside-in winner |
| Body               | Forehand inside-in                            | Jam the returner, force a weak reply                          | Blocks it back: attack down the line            |
| T (deuce or ad)    | Forehand inside-out or backhand down the line | Take away the angle, dictate the center                       | Returns weak: step in and finish                |
| Second serve       | Forehand if possible, otherwise backhand      | Neutralize — don't give up an attack                          | Attacks the return: defend deep                 |

**Coach's Tip.** S+1 doesn't mean "hit a forehand." S+1 means "hit a forehand to a target that sets up the next shot." Wide serve, then inside-out to the open court, then the next ball is a down-the-line put-away. It's a two-shot sequence.

## Return+1 Patterns: the Neutralizer

| Return Type                              | R+1 Shot                           | Goal                                                       | Recovery                        |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Deep crosscourt                          | Crosscourt deep                    | Neutralize the server's S+1, start the rally on your terms | Center of the crosscourt angle  |
| Chip or block (against a big serve)      | Deep middle                        | Take the pace off, deny the angle                          | Slightly toward the return side |
| Aggressive (against a weak second serve) | Attack down the line or inside-out | Take time away, pressure immediately                       | Move forward, to the bisector   |

RULE: The return goal is to make the server hit a groundstroke. No aces, no S+1 winners. Deep crosscourt is the highest-percentage choice.

## Rally Patterns: Control the Center

Whoever controls the center of the court — the "T" zone — wins the rally.

| Situation                       | Your Shot                             | Why                                        | Next                                   |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| The opponent hits down the line | Crosscourt deep                       | Changes direction safely, pushes them wide | Recover to center                      |
| The opponent hits crosscourt    | Crosscourt deep                       | Keeps them on the run, high percentage     | Recover to the center of your angle    |
| You have a short ball           | Attack down the line or a sharp angle | Time to attack — don't give up crosscourt  | Move to net or recover to the bisector |
| You are pulled wide             | A high crosscourt ball or a lob       | Buys time, resets the geometry             | Sprint to center                       |

## Doubles: the Three Systems

*Doubles isn't two singles matches. It's a system. Two people, one brain.*

— Henry Pham

Three systems cover 95% of doubles. Pick one, drill it, trust it.

| System | Name                           | Formation                                       | Core Principle                                      | Best Against                                         |
| ------ | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| 1      | Two up (both at net)           | The server and returner both come in            | Control the net, cut off angles, finish points fast | Baseliners, weak returns                             |
| 2      | One up, one back (I-formation) | The server stays back, the partner is at net    | The net player poaches, the server covers lobs      | Big servers, strong returners                        |
| 3      | Australian or stacking         | Both players on the same side (serve or return) | Force the return to one side, hide a weakness       | Lefty and righty pairs, extreme forehand or backhand |

## Doubles Roles and Responsibilities

| Position           | Primary Job                             | Secondary Job                        | Key Rule                                                           |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Server             | Serve to the target, set up the partner | Cover the middle on the return       | Serve percentage beats pace. Wide serve means the partner poaches. |
| Server's partner   | Poach — cut off the crosscourt          | Cover the alley if not poaching      | Move on the server's toss, not on contact.                         |
| Returner           | Return low, crosscourt                  | Move to net if the return is good    | Never miss the return wide. Middle or low is safe.                 |
| Returner's partner | Cover the middle, read the poach        | Cover the alley if the partner stays | Eyes on the returner's contact, not the net player.                |

RULE: The net player moves on the server's toss, not on contact. Early movement means a successful poach. Late movement makes you the target.

## Doubles Patterns

| Pattern                        | Sequence                                                   | Trigger                                     | Finish                   |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Serve wide, then poach         | Serve wide, the partner crosses, a volley put-away         | The opponent returns crosscourt             | Angled volley            |
| Serve at the body, then middle | Serve at the body, the partner covers the middle, a volley | The returner is jammed and pops the ball up | A punch volley           |
| Return low, then come in       | A low crosscourt return, both players move to net          | The return forces a weak reply              | Volley or overhead       |
| Lob return, then defend        | A lob over the net player, both players retreat, reset     | The net player is too aggressive            | A deep lob, then recover |

## Communication: the Three Signals

| Signal                   | Meaning                  | When                 | Who            |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------ | -------------------- | -------------- |
| Fist (poach)             | I'm crossing             | Before the serve     | The net player |
| Open hand (stay)         | I'm staying              | Before the serve     | The net player |
| Two fingers (fake)       | I fake, then stay        | Before the serve     | The net player |
| Verbal "mine" or "yours" | Overhead or lob coverage | During the point     | Both players   |
| Verbal "switch"          | Switch sides             | After a lob or poach | Both players   |

## Fault Signals — Strategy

| Situation       | Error                             | Cause                                   | Fix                                                 |
| --------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Singles: S+1    | The inside-out forehand goes long | Contact is late, the face is open       | Take contact further in front, close the face       |
| Singles: R+1    | The return falls short crosscourt | Late on the serve, blocked              | Shorten the takeback, contact in front              |
| Singles: rally  | A down-the-line error             | Changed direction on a deep ball        | Only change direction on a short or attackable ball |
| Doubles: poach  | A missed volley, or got passed    | Moved late, or read the wrong cue       | Move on the toss, read the returner's hips          |
| Doubles: return | The return goes wide or long      | Swinging at the serve                   | Block it. Short takeback. Low crosscourt.           |
| Doubles: net    | Got lobbed                        | Standing too close, or no communication | The partner calls "switch" early                    |

## Strategy Checklist

SINGLES: S+1 FOREHAND. R+1 DEEP. CROSSCOURT DEFAULT. ATTACK SHORT. RECOVER TO THE BISECTOR.

DOUBLES: SERVE PERCENTAGE. POACH EARLY. RETURN LOW CROSSCOURT. COMMUNICATE. CONTROL THE NET.

- Singles: the serve+1 forehand pattern is drilled until automatic.

- Singles: return+1 deep crosscourt neutralizes 80% of serves.

- Singles: crosscourt deep is the default. Down the line only on a green light (short, high, or weak).

- Singles: recover to the bisector of the opponent's possible replies.

- Doubles: serve percentage first. A wide serve is the partner's poach signal.

- Doubles: the net player moves on the toss, not on contact.

- Doubles: return low, crosscourt. Never miss wide.

- Doubles: communicate — poach, stay, fake, switch, mine, yours.

- Doubles: control the net means control the match. The first team to net wins 65 to 70% of points.
