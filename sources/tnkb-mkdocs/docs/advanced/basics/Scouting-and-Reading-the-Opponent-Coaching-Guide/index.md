# Scouting & Reading the Opponent Coaching Guide

*A different skill from "anticipation" — this is a pre-match and in-match analysis method.*

## Why This Matters

The site's Decision Latency and Hidden Speed pages cover the split-second, reactive side of
reading an opponent. Scouting is the slower, deliberate side: identifying tendencies before and
during the match so your shot selection is informed rather than generic.

## Core Concepts

**Weakness identification, not just "find the weaker side."** Watch the warm-up and first few
games for specific, exploitable patterns: does the opponent's second serve sit up? Do they rush
shots hit to their body? Do they struggle moving forward for short balls, or backward on lobs?
Specific patterns are more actionable than a vague "their backhand is weaker."

**Data over impressions.** After 3-4 games, mentally tally simple counts: how many unforced
errors came off which wing, how many net approaches they attempted and won, whether they served
predominantly to one side on big points. A few data points beat a general impression formed in
game one.

**Building the plan from the data**, not from your preferred game. If the data says their
backhand return is the pattern to attack, commit to it even if it means playing a style you
don't love — a game plan you execute beats a game plan you merely prefer.

## Action Steps

1. **Use the warm-up deliberately.** Hit to both wings and to the body during warm-up and note
   which shots produce hesitation or poor recovery, not just which look "weaker."
2. **Track for 4 games before committing** to a full game plan — early games are data-gathering,
   not the time to panic if the plan isn't working yet.
3. **Re-scout every set.** Opponents adjust; a pattern that worked in set one may be closed off
   by set two. Keep updating the read rather than running the same plan on autopilot.
4. **Write it down between changeovers** if allowed — even three bullet points ("serve wide on
   big points," "they don't like pace at the body," "avoid short balls, they attack forward well")
   keeps the plan concrete under pressure.

## Common Mistakes

- Deciding the game plan from the first two points instead of a real sample.
- Sticking to a scouting read from set one after the opponent has visibly adjusted.
- Scouting the opponent but then playing your default game anyway under pressure.

## Quick Reference

| Scouting Signal | What It Suggests |
|---|---|
| Second serve sits up | Attack the return, move in |
| Slow recovery after wide balls | Target the same corner repeatedly |
| Errors spike under time pressure | Increase pace/depth on that wing |
| Avoids net | Bring them forward with short, low balls |
