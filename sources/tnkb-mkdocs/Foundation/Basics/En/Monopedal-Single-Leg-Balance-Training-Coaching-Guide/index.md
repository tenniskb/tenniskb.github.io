# Monopedal (Single-Leg) Balance Training

*Nearly every tennis shot is hit from single-leg support at some point in the motion — training
that support directly pays off everywhere.*

## Why This Matters

Groundstrokes, serves, and volleys all pass through moments where your weight is fully or mostly
on one leg. If single-leg stability is weak, the body compensates with tension elsewhere (grip,
shoulders) and the kinetic chain leaks power. Balance isn't a soft, secondary quality — it's the
platform every stroke is built on.

## Core Concepts

**A progression ladder, not one drill.** Balance training works best as a staged progression:
single-leg stance → single-leg with eyes closed (removing visual compensation, forcing the
vestibular and proprioceptive systems to do the work) → single-leg on an unstable surface (a
cushion or wobble pad, adding a further layer of difficulty). Jumping straight to the hardest
version skips the adaptation the earlier stages build.

**The balance–gaze link.** Stable balance and stable gaze reinforce each other: a wobbling base
makes it harder to track the ball cleanly, and an unstable gaze makes balance corrections less
accurate. Training single-leg balance while keeping a fixed visual target builds both systems
together rather than in isolation.

**Sport-specific application, not static holds alone.** Beyond simply standing on one leg, drills
like sideways jump-and-land (landing and sticking the balance on one leg) and single-foot shadow
swings (rehearsing a stroke while balanced on one leg) transfer the stability directly into stroke
production, which a static hold alone doesn't fully train.

## Action Steps

1. **Start the progression ladder 2-3x/week**: 30 seconds single-leg stance per side, then add
   eyes-closed reps once the basic stance is solid, then add an unstable surface once eyes-closed
   is solid.
2. **Add sideways jump-and-land reps**: jump laterally and stick the landing on the outside leg,
   holding for 2-3 seconds — this mimics the loaded, single-leg moment of a wide groundstroke.
3. **Practice single-foot shadow swings** for forehand and backhand, balancing on the back leg
   through the loading phase of the swing, to connect balance training directly to stroke
   mechanics.
4. **Pair with a fixed gaze target** during static holds to train the balance-gaze link together.

## Common Mistakes

- Jumping straight to unstable-surface training before single-leg-with-eyes-closed is solid,
  which builds compensations rather than real stability.
- Treating balance work as generic "core stability" rather than something to connect explicitly
  back to stroke-specific single-leg moments.
- Only doing static holds and never progressing to dynamic (jump-and-land) versions, which is
  where most match-relevant balance demands actually occur.

## Quick Reference

| Stage | What It Trains | Progress To Next When |
|---|---|---|
| Single-leg stance | Basic static balance | 30 sec solid, both legs |
| + eyes closed | Vestibular/proprioceptive reliance | 20 sec solid, both legs |
| + unstable surface | Full dynamic stability | Comfortable with eyes-closed stage |
| Jump-and-land | Sport-specific dynamic balance | After static progression is solid |
