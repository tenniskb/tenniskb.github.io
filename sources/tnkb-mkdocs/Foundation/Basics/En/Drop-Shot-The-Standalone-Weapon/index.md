# Drop Shot: The Standalone Weapon

*A companion to Lob and Overhead — the drop shot deserves the same standalone treatment as its
"opposite number" at the net.*

## Why This Matters

The site's Lob and Overhead Coaching Guide treats the lob well but only mentions the drop shot in
passing. Against recreational opponents — especially ones who camp on the baseline or are less
mobile moving forward — the drop shot is one of the highest win-rate shots available, and it
deserves its own technique and tactical treatment.

## Core Concepts

**Disguise is the whole shot.** A drop shot that's visible in the backswing is a gift to the
opponent. The technique goal is to make the preparation look identical to a normal groundstroke
until the very last moment, when the racket face opens and decelerates through contact instead
of accelerating.

**Selection criteria, not "hit it whenever."** The drop shot works best off a shorter, slower ball
you're already moving forward to (adding backspin to an already-short ball is easier than
disguising one off a deep, fast ball), against an opponent who is clearly behind the baseline, and
on slower surfaces where the ball sits up rather than skidding through.

**The physical and mental fatigue effect.** Beyond winning the immediate point, a well-placed drop
shot forces the opponent to cover ground forward that they otherwise never have to cover,
compounding fatigue over a match and planting doubt about their court coverage — a tactical
dividend beyond the single point won.

## Action Steps

1. **Practice the disguise specifically**: have a partner call out "drive or drop" only after your
   racket has started its normal backswing, forcing you to keep the preparation identical until
   the last instant.
2. **Restrict early drop-shot practice to short, slow balls only** — the shot is far easier to
   disguise and execute off these than off deep, fast ones, and building confidence here first
   prevents low-percentage attempts later.
3. **Track opponent court position as a selection cue**: any time your opponent is more than a
   step behind the baseline after their shot, treat the next short ball as a drop-shot candidate.
4. **Follow the drop shot in** when hit from inside the baseline — a drop shot is not just a
   "win the point outright" shot; be ready to cover a weak reply at net.

## Common Mistakes

- Telegraphing the shot with an obviously shorter, slower backswing that opponents learn to read
  after one or two attempts.
- Attempting drop shots off deep, fast balls where disguise and touch are both much harder to
  execute reliably.
- Treating the drop shot as purely a surprise/junk shot rather than a repeatable, criteria-based
  tactical choice.

## Quick Reference

| Selection Cue | Favors Drop Shot? |
|---|---|
| Short, slow incoming ball | Yes |
| Opponent well behind baseline | Yes |
| Slow surface (clay, some hard courts) | Yes |
| Deep, fast incoming ball | No — low disguise/success rate |
| Opponent already at/near net | No |
