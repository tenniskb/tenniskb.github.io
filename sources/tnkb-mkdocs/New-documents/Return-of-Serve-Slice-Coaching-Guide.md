# The Return of Serve Slice — "Safety First"

Let's talk about the return of serve, because I think you're overcomplicating it.

## The Big Idea: This Shot Is About Survival, Not Heroics

A big serve at your level isn't usually about raw speed — it's about pace and spin messing with your rhythm before you've even had a chance to set up. Try to swing back hard with topspin against that, and you'll bury a lot of returns in the net or send them long. That's exactly why the slice return exists. It's not a weapon. It's a redirection tool.

Here's the mindset shift I want you to make: **you don't need to be a hero on this shot.** A deep, unhurried slice that lands safely in the middle of the box beats a "winner attempt" that clips the tape and dies in the net, every single time. Get the ball back in play, make your opponent hit one more shot, and let the point develop from there.

## The Technique: Block and Redirect

**Split-step first — no exceptions.** This is the one piece you can't skip. You need to split-step the moment your opponent makes contact with the ball. Skip it, and you're already late before the ball has even crossed the net.

**Don't swing — block.** This is the part that trips people up, because everything in you wants to take a full cut at the ball. Resist it. Your opponent has already supplied all the pace you need. Your job is simply to block it back — keep the racket short, compact, and steady through contact. Think less "swing," more "catch and redirect."

**Let the grip do the work.** Stay in Continental. As the ball arrives, all you're really doing is angling the racket face to send the ball deep into your opponent's court. It's basically a volley — you're just doing it from the baseline instead of at the net.

## Strategy: Where Should You Actually Aim?

Not every return needs the same target. A few go-to options:

- **Deep middle** is your safest, highest-percentage play. It takes away your opponent's angles and gives them the least to work with. When in doubt, go here.
- **At the server's feet** — if they're following their serve in to the net, a low slice at their shoes is a great way to force an awkward half-volley.
- **Wide to the backhand** opens up the court for whatever you want to do with your next shot, though it's a bit riskier than the deep middle.

## The Drill: Return-Wall Practice

Stand about 6 meters from a wall. Throw the ball hard against the wall, and as it rebounds back at you, slice-block it back — just like you would a real return. Keep your hands quiet, your wrist firm, and let the racket absorb the pace rather than adding to it.

**Goal:** 10 returns in a row that land "deep" — close to where the baseline would be on the wall.
