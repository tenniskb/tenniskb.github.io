Foundation Basics¶

The entry point. The work that every other level of tennis assumes you've done.

Why This Section¶

The basics are layered. What you couldn't feel a year ago, you feel clearly now. What you can feel now, you'll feel differently in another year. The masters I respect most spend half their practice time on what you'll find below.

Topics in This Section¶

Work through these in order. Each one builds on the previous.

  • Grip — Foundations and Grip System — the four grips, the change map, the pressure map
  • Backhand — the two-hander, the one-hander, the slice
  • Forehand — modern topspin, unit turn, contact out front
  • Serve — trophy position, racket-head drop, pronation
  • Volley — punch, no swing, body control
  • Overhead — the highest-percentage smash
  • Doubles — patterns, formations, communication

New Additions (2026-07)¶

  • Injury Prevention & Joint Health Coaching Guide — why the elbow, back, and shoulder pain happens, and the technique/equipment fixes that actually stop it
  • Fitness, Conditioning & Recovery Coaching Guide — the physical base underneath every technical fix: core, mobility, interval conditioning, sleep
  • Kick Serve / Topspin Second-Serve Coaching Guide — companion to The Gravity Serve; the grip/toss/brush mechanics that keep your second serve out of trouble
  • Approach Shot Strategy Beyond the Slice — the full approach-shot decision tree: flat/topspin, swinging volley, serve-and-volley, chip-and-charge
  • Drop Shot: The Standalone Weapon — disguise, selection criteria, and the fatigue dividend of a well-placed drop shot

New Additions (2026-07, Round 2)¶

  • Serve Stance: Platform vs. Pinpoint & the "Hitch" — companion to The Gravity Serve; why a stalled trophy position is usually a stance problem, not a swing problem
  • Monopedal (Single-Leg) Balance Training — a progression ladder for the single-leg stability every stroke is built on
  • Foot-Strike Mechanics: Forefoot vs. Heel — the overlooked layer underneath every footwork drill
  • "Strike, Don't Swing" & Weighted Shadow-Swing Training — a late-commitment perception-action model for contact, and the drill that builds it
  • Pre-Movement Readiness: The Idle Engine & Micro-Bounce System — the state your body should be in while waiting, before the split step

How to Use¶

Each Basics article is structured as: 1. What it is — plain-English definition 2. Why it matters — the failure modes if you skip it 3. How to practice — concrete drills you can do on court 4. What good looks like — the felt sense of having it 5. Common mistakes — and the cues to fix them

Start at the top. Don't skip. The grip work is the most boring part and the most important. Everything else becomes harder if the grip is fuzzy.

A Note on the 50+ Player¶

Many of these basics include a slice variation for the older body. The slice isn't a compromise. For the 50+ player, it's often the highest-percentage choice on any given ball. If you're 50+, work the slice variations alongside the topspin variations — they're not beginner versions of the real thing. They're the real thing, in a different shape.
