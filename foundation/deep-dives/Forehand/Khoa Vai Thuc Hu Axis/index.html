*The Lock-Shoulder, Free-Other-Side Principle¶

Chapter 1 — The Big Idea: Strong Side, Free Side¶

🇺🇸 English

| Tennis works the same way. On a forehand, your left shoulder is "thực" (locked, anchoring the rotation) and your right shoulder is "hư" (free, swinging). On a backhand, they switch. | | Recreational players do the opposite: they use both shoulders to swing. This is the #1 cause of "I swung hard but the ball went nowhere." | | The strong side makes the axis. The free side makes the whip. | 🇺🇸 English | | --- | | On a forehand, after the unit turn, your left shoulder is frozen. It points to the side fence. It does NOT come around. | | Your right shoulder is the one that flies — but it only flies because the left is locked. | | If you try to swing both shoulders forward, you lose the axis and you "muscle" the ball. |

🎨 Visual cue Imagine a clock face on your chest. The "12" is straight up, "3" is to the right, "6" is down, "9" is to the left. After the unit turn on a forehand, the "12" on your chest is pointing to the right side fence. When you swing, the "12" rotates to point at the ball. Your spine is the pin in the center of the clock — it doesn't move. Your chest is the face — it rotates around the pin.**

🎯 Cue 2 — The 70/30 Weight (Forehand)¶

🇺🇸 English

| Your front leg (left) is the bridge — slightly bent, receiving the rotation but not pushing. | | The transfer of weight from back to front happens after contact, not before. | 🇺🇸 English | | --- | | The axis is the line around which everything rotates. Most players think the axis is the lower back. It's not. The axis is the spine, and the lower back should stay relatively still. | | Think of your spine as a flagpole. Your chest is a banner. Your arms are ribbons. The flagpole doesn't bend. The banner catches the wind. The ribbons fly. | |

🎯 Cue 1 — The Towel Test¶

🇺🇸 English
Put a small towel under your left armpit (for right-handers doing forehand).
Do a slow forehand. The towel must not fall.
If the towel falls, your left shoulder came forward — the axis broke, and you used your arm to swing.
If the towel stays, your left shoulder stayed back — the axis held, and the chest rotated around the spine.
🇺🇸 English
---
The "diamond" is the frame formed by your chest, both shoulders, and both hands when you hold the racket in front of you.

| The diamond breaks the moment you drop your left hand or rotate your left shoulder forward. | | When the diamond breaks, the swing becomes an "arm swing." When it holds, the swing becomes a "body swing." | 🇺🇸 English | | --- | | Most players think rotation is just "turning the shoulders." It's not. Real rotation includes a side-tilt — your upper body tilts slightly toward the ball as your hips rotate. | | This is the "coiled spring" effect. The tilt pre-loads the side of your body, like a spring being compressed. When you release, the spring fires. | | The cue: "pour water out of your right hip" — like you're tipping a glass sideways. |

🎨 **Visual cue Watch a baseball pitcher. Their upper body tilts dramatically toward the plate before they release. That's the side-tilt. They're loading a spring. You're doing the same thing on a forehand — just smaller.

Chapter 3 — The 3 Tiers: Top Stable, Middle Soft, Bottom Springy¶

🇺🇸 English
Your body has 3 tiers, and each has a different job. Top tier (chest + shoulders) = stable. Middle tier (waist + hips) = soft. Bottom tier (legs) = springy.
Most recreational players mix up the tiers. They have a soft top (chest wobbles), a tight middle (waist locked), and a stiff bottom (legs straight). Result: no power, lots of arm effort.
The fix is simple but counter-intuitive: tighten the top, loosen the middle, bend the bottom.

🏛️ Tier 1 — Top Stable (Chest + Shoulders)¶

🇺🇸 English

| The left shoulder (forehand) is the anchor. It points to the side fence. It does NOT come around until after contact. | | The right shoulder is free to fly, but it only flies around the locked left. |

💪 The "proud chest" cue Imagine there's a button on your chest. After the unit turn, the button is facing the side fence. When you swing, the button rotates to face the ball — but your spine stays vertical the whole time. Spine = vertical. Chest = rotating. Two different jobs.**

🌊 Tier 2 — Middle Soft (Waist + Hips)¶

🇺🇸 English
The middle tier is the "shock absorber" of the body. If the waist is tight, the rotation stops at the hips and the upper body has to swing by itself.
The cue: "soft belly, loose hips." A slightly soft midsection lets the rotation flow from the bottom tier to the top tier.

🌊 The "water in a glass" cue Imagine your midsection is a glass of water. If the glass is rigid, water sloshes violently. If the glass is slightly soft (like a flexible cup), water moves smoothly. A slightly soft waist = smooth rotation. A tight waist = violent jerk.**

🦵 Tier 3 — Bottom Springy (Legs)¶

🇺🇸 English

| The back leg pushes the ground. The ground pushes back. That force travels up through the hips, through the soft middle, into the stable top. | | This is the kinetic chain — the famous "power comes from the ground" idea. |

🦵 The "coiled spring" cue Your back leg is a coiled spring. The deeper you bend it, the more energy you store. The straighter you keep it, the less energy you have. But — never lock the knee. Coiled ≠ locked.**

Chapter 4 — The Switch: Forehand Locks Left, Backhand Locks Right¶

🇺🇸 English
Forehand (right-hander): left shoulder is "thực" (locked), right shoulder is "hư" (free). Back leg is right leg (deep bent, coiled).
The pattern: the shoulder away from the swing is the locked one. The shoulder on the swing side is the free one.

🧭 The simple rule "Thực = away from the racket. Hư = on the racket side."**

🛠️ Drill — The Switch Test¶

🇺🇸 English
Stand in ready position. Do a forehand unit turn. Notice: left shoulder locked.
Reset. Do a backhand unit turn. Notice: right shoulder locked.

| If you can feel the switch, you understand the axis. If both shoulders try to swing, you don't. |

Chapter 5 — The 50+ Adjustments: Save the Lower Back, Save the Shoulders¶

🇺🇸 English

| But the axis must be done correctly. Done wrong, it puts stress on the shoulder joint (rotator cuff). Done right, it saves both the back and the shoulders. |

🛡️ Rule 1 — The "Locked" Shoulder is Soft, Not Stiff¶

🇺🇸 English

| The shoulder muscles (deltoids, rotator cuff) should be relaxed. They're just holding the arm in position — they're not pushing. | | If you feel your shoulder muscles working hard, the rotation is going through them instead of the chest. Stop, reset, and try again. |

💆 The "dead arm" test After the unit turn, ask a friend to lift your right arm (the free side). It should feel light, like a dead fish.** If it feels heavy and tense, your arm is "active" when it should be "passive."

🛡️ Rule 2 — The Lower Back Stays Vertical¶

🇺🇸 English
During the rotation, the lower back should stay vertical (or very slightly tilted, not curved sideways).
The rotation happens above the lower back (in the chest) and below (in the hips). The lower back is the stable column between them.
If you feel the lower back curving or twisting, the rotation is going through the wrong place.

🧊 The "wall check" test Stand with your back to a wall. Do a slow forehand. Your lower back should not lose contact with the wall.** If it does, the lower back is twisting — and that's where tennis back injuries come from.

🛡️ Rule 3 — The Hips Lead, Not Follow¶

🇺🇸 English
The hips start the rotation. The chest follows. The arms follow the chest. The racket follows the arms. It's a chain, in that order.
If the arms swing first, the chain is broken. The hips are the engine, not the brakes.
At 50+, this matters even more because the lower back will compensate if the hips don't lead.

🏎️ The "tow truck" cue Imagine your hips are a tow truck. Your chest is the car being towed. Your arms are the rope. The tow truck moves first, the car follows, the rope goes along for the ride.** Reverse the order, and the rope breaks.

Chapter 6 — 4 Drills You Can Do This Week¶

🛠️ Drill 1 — The Towel Under Armpit (Daily, 3 min)¶

🇺🇸 English
Put a small towel under your left armpit. Do 20 slow forehands. The towel must not fall.

| Goal: 20 clean reps, no falling towel. |

🛠️ Drill 2 — The Diamond Hold (Daily, 2 min)¶

🇺🇸 English
Stand in ready position. Bring both hands together in front of your chest, racket in the middle. Hold 2 seconds.
This trains the "diamond frame" — the load-bearing structure that holds during rotation.
Goal: 10 clean reps, eyes closed, 2 seconds each.

🛠️ Drill 3 — The Hip-First Rotation (3x per week, 5 min)¶

🇺🇸 English
Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Hold a light object (racket, broom) in both hands at your chest.
Rotate by moving the hips first. The chest follows. The arms follow the chest.
The order should feel like a chain reaction.
Goal: 20 reps. Focus on the order, not the speed.

🛠️ Drill 4 — The Wall Check (Warmup, 2 min)¶

🇺🇸 English
Stand with your back to a wall. Do a slow forehand. Your lower back should not lose contact with the wall.
If your lower back leaves the wall, the rotation is going through your back instead of your hips.
This is the 50+ safety check — if you can't pass this, you're not ready to play.
Goal: 10 clean reps per day as warmup.

Chapter 7 — Cues, Warnings & The Court Cheat Sheet¶

🧠 The 6 Cues to Tattoo on Your Brain¶

| # | Cue |

|---|---|---| | 1 | Thực = xa vợt. Hư = cùng bên vợt. | | 2 | Frozen shoulder, flying shoulder. |

| 3 | Spine = vertical. Chest = rotating. Two different jobs. |

| 4 | Tighten the top, loosen the middle, bend the bottom. |

| 5 | Hips first, chest second, arms third, racket last. |

| 6 | Locked = soft, not stiff. |

⚠️ The 5 Warnings to Remember¶

| # | Warning |

|---|---|---| | 1 | Đừng căng vai "khóa" — nó ở lại bằng cách thả lỏng, không bằng cách căng. | | 2 | Don't rotate the lower back — keep it vertical. Rotation goes through the hips. |

| 3 | Don't transfer weight before contact — let the back leg stay bent through the swing. |

| 4 | Don't swing both shoulders — one is locked, one is free. Always. |

| 5 | Don't lock the front knee — it should be a bridge (slightly bent), not a pillar. |
