Foundation Level¶

This is where every tennis journey begins — and where experienced players return when something breaks down.

Why Start Here?¶

Foundation material covers the non-negotiable basics: grip, stance, footwork, the fundamental stroke shapes, return of serve, doubles positioning, mental game. These aren't "beginner topics" you graduate from — they're the ground you stand on for everything that follows.

I get asked all the time: "I've been playing for 10 years. Do I really need to review the basics?" Yes. Every time, without exception. Not because you're doing it wrong, but because basics are layered. What you couldn't feel last year, you feel clearly now. What you can feel now, you'll feel differently next year.

What's in This Section¶

Basics¶

Core fundamentals taught through simple, repeatable drills: - Grip foundations — Continental, Eastern, Semi-Western, the change map, the pressure map - Footwork — split step, recovery, unit turn - Forehand — the modern topspin forehand, the unit turn, contact out front - Backhand — the two-hander, the one-hander (Dimitrov/Wawrinka/Federer model), the slice - Serve — the trophy position, the racket-head drop, the pronation - Return of Serve — neutral return, slice return, block return - Doubles — patterns, formations, communication, poaching - Mental Game — between-point routines, focus, dealing with pressure

Deep Dives¶

Focused exploration of specific foundation topics: - The Complete Manual — the master reference - The 1-Page Pocket Card — printable one-page summary - Long-form biomechanics articles on every stroke - Common mistakes and how to fix them

How to Approach This Material¶

  1. Start with the Basics section in order. Grip → Footwork → Forehand → Backhand. Each one builds on the previous.
  2. Then move to Serve and Return of Serve. These are the strokes that decide matches before they start.
  3. Then Doubles patterns and Mental Game. These are the strokes that decide matches after the physical even out.
  4. The Deep Dives are reference material. Come back to them when a specific question comes up, or when you want to understand why a particular technique works.
