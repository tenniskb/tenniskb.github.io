# ART-137: The Science of Injury Prevention

## Executive Summary
Injury is not random bad luck — it is the predictable output of load exceeding capacity. This article examines the biomechanical root causes of common tennis injuries and provides a 5-Pillar prevention system that builds capacity while managing load. It provides a framework for building a custom, injury-adapted prevention system.

## The Injury Integration Framework

### 1. The Injury Decision Cycle
Elite players use a 5-step decision cycle:
1.  **Pillar Analysis:** Identify which pillar needs optimization (e.g., "My shoulder pain is due to poor kinetic chain alignment").
2.  **Injury Gap Analysis:** Calculate what the injury doesn't provide (e.g., "My shoulder pain is due to poor kinetic chain alignment").
3.  **Margin Analysis:** Calculate the cost of each option (e.g., "If I adjust my kinetic chain alignment, I'll have to adjust my serve technique but gain better shoulder health").
4.  **Implementation:** Adjust training to accommodate the new injury prevention system.
5.  **Evaluation:** Measure the impact on injury prevention (e.g., "My shoulder pain decreased by 50%").

### 2. The Injury Memory System
The brain stores three types of injury knowledge:
*   **Procedural:** Automatic responses to common situations (e.g., "When I hit a kick serve, I always move to the backhand side").
*   **Declarative:** Explicit rules (e.g., "I never attack a serve-and-volleyer's second serve").
*   **Episodic:** Context-specific memories (e.g., "In this match, he's been hitting his first serves to the deuce side").
