# ART-084: Neuromuscular Integration & Plyometrics

## Executive Summary
Neuromuscular integration is the ability to coordinate the kinetic chain with the neurological system. It is not just about running faster; it is about running more efficiently. This article examines the neuromuscular integration protocols that allow elite players to maintain high-intensity performance throughout the match. Understanding these protocols is essential for developing a complete, integrated athlete.

## The Neuromuscular Integration Framework

### 1. The Depth Jump
The depth jump is a simple but effective way to improve neuromuscular integration. It allows the athlete to develop the ability to produce high-intensity effort for short periods of time.
*   **The Ideal Protocol:** The athlete completes depth jumps with 30-second rest intervals.
*   **The Common Errors:**
    *   **The "Long" Jump:** The athlete completes jumps that are too long, causing them to fatigue too quickly.
    *   **The "Short" Jump:** The athlete completes jumps that are too short, causing them to not develop the ability to produce high-intensity effort.

### 2. The Broad Jump
The broad jump is a more advanced way to improve neuromuscular integration. It allows the athlete to develop the ability to produce high-intensity effort for longer periods of time.
*   **The Ideal Protocol:** The athlete completes broad jumps with 30-second rest intervals.
*   **The Common Errors:**
    *   **The "Long" Jump:** The athlete completes jumps that are too long, causing them to fatigue too quickly.
    *   **The "Short" Jump:** The athlete completes jumps that are too short, causing them to not develop the ability to produce high-intensity effort.

### 3. The Single-Leg Hop
The single-leg hop is a more advanced way to improve neuromuscular integration. It allows the athlete to develop the ability to produce high-intensity effort for even longer periods of time.
*   **The Ideal Protocol:** The athlete completes single-leg hops with 30-second rest intervals.
*   **The Common Errors:**
    *   **The "Long" Hop:** The athlete completes hops that are too long, causing them to fatigue too quickly.
    *   **The "Short" Hop:** The athlete completes hops that are too short, causing them to not develop the ability to produce high-intensity effort.
