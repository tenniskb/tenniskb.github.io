# ART-011: Pelvic Snap & Hip Bump Mechanics

## Executive Summary
The "Pelvic Snap" is the pivotal transition point where linear ground force is converted into rotational torque. It is not a smooth rotation, but an explosive, impulsive movement of the pelvic girdle that initiates the upper body's uncoiling. This movement, often coupled with a "Hip Bump" (the lateral drive of the hip toward the target), is the primary engine for power in modern groundstrokes.

## The Biomechanics of the Snap

### 1. The Impulsive Start
The snap begins with the explosive extension of the outside leg. As the leg drives, the pelvis is forced to rotate forward. The key is the **speed of onset**—the pelvis must accelerate from zero to peak velocity in milliseconds.

### 2. The Hip Bump (Lateral Drive)
In the open-stance forehand, the "Hip Bump" occurs when the outside hip is driven forcefully toward the target line. 
*   **Mechanism:** This creates a lateral shift in the center of mass that "throws" the pelvis into its rotation.
*   **Result:** It increases the rotational impulse, providing more raw energy for the core to transmit upward.

### 3. The Micro-Stop (Deceleration)
The most critical part of the snap is the sudden stop. The pelvis does not spin indefinitely; it drives forward and then undergoes a rapid deceleration.
*   **The Transfer:** This sudden stop creates a "whip" effect, transferring the momentum from the pelvis to the torso.
*   **Clinical Key:** Without the micro-stop, the energy remains in the lower body, and the player "spins out" without transferring power to the racket.

## The Kinetic Integration
GRF (Outside Leg) → Pelvic Acceleration → Micro-Stop → Torso Uncoiling

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Mechanical Failure | Result | Corrective Drill |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "Spinning" instead of "Snapping" | Lack of Micro-Stop | Power is lost; ball lacks penetration. | **Wall-Stop Drill:** Rotate hips toward a wall and "stop" the movement abruptly 2 inches before contact. |
| Weak rotation | No Hip Bump | Low racket speed; arm-dominant shot. | **Med-Ball Hip Toss:** Stand sideways and toss a medicine ball forward using only hip drive. |
| Unstable balance | Poor outside leg anchor | Body tilts; inconsistent ball flight. | **Single-Leg Balance Snap:** Perform pelvic snaps while balancing on the outside leg. |

## Practical Application: The "Snap" Sensation
The sensation should be like snapping a heavy rubber band. There is a moment of tension (the load) followed by an instantaneous, violent release (the snap).
