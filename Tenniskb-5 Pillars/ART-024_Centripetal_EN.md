# ART-024: Centripetal Force & Pull-Back Vectoring

## Executive Summary
To generate maximum racket head speed, an athlete must not only push the racket forward but also pull it *inward* toward the axis of rotation. This is the application of Centripetal Force. By exercising an inward radial force on the handle, the player reduces the radius of the swing at the critical moment, causing the outer tip of the racket to accelerate violently.

## The Physics of the "Inward Pull"

### 1. Centripetal Force (Fc)
Centripetal force is the "center-seeking" force that keeps an object moving in a curved path.
**Fc = (m × v²) / r**
Where m is mass, v is velocity, and r is the radius.

### 2. The Radius Manipulation
In a tennis swing, the "r" (radius) is the distance from the shoulder/core to the racket head.
*   **The Wide Arc:** During the start of the forward swing, the radius is relatively large.
*   **The Pull-Back:** Elite players execute a subtle "pull-back" vector on the grip. By pulling the handle toward the body, they momentarily decrease the radius (r).
*   **The Velocity Spike:** According to the law of conservation of angular momentum, as the radius (r) decreases, the velocity (v) must increase. This creates a "snap" at the end of the arc.

## Vectoring: The Path of the Hand
The hand does not move in a simple circle; it moves in a complex vector.
*   **Forward Vector:** Drives the racket toward the ball.
*   **Inward Vector:** Pulls the racket toward the body.
*   **The Result:** The combination of these two vectors creates a "whipping" trajectory rather than a "pushing" trajectory.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Vector Failure | Result | Corrective Drill |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "Pushing" the ball | No Inward Vector | Racket head speed is linear and slow; no "pop". | **The "Inside-Out" Swing:** Focus on pulling the handle toward the hip before extending the arm. |
| "Slicing" across the ball | Excessive Inward Pull | Racket face closes too early; ball flies wide or into net. | **Target-Axis Drill:** Ensure the inward pull is timed to end exactly at the point of contact. |
| Rigid arm | No radius change | Swing feels like a board; lack of acceleration. | **Wrist-Flick Shadows:** Use a light racket or a weighted string to feel the "inward" pull. |

## Practical Application: The "Hula-Hoop" Sensation
Imagine you are swinging a hula-hoop. To make the hoop spin faster, you don't just push it forward; you pull the center toward you. The racket is the hoop, and your hand is the center. Feel the "pull" to create the "push."
