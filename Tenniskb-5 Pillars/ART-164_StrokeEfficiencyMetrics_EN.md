# ART-164: Stroke Efficiency Metrics — Quality Over Quantity

## Executive Summary
Not all practice balls are created equal. This article establishes objective metrics for evaluating stroke quality — spin rate, depth consistency, and margin indicators — so athletes can train for efficiency rather than mere repetition.

## The Efficiency Problem

### 1. The Rep Trap
Hitting 500 balls with 60% quality builds a 60% game. The neuromuscular system adapts to whatever pattern you repeat — good or bad. **Repetitions reinforce the pattern, not the quality.**

### 2. The Quality Metrics
| Metric | How to Measure | Elite Benchmark |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Spin Consistency | Standard deviation of RPM across 10 balls | < 15% coefficient of variation |
| Depth Consistency | Standard deviation of landing position | < 1.5 m from target line |
| Net Clearance | Average height over net | 0.8–1.2 m |
| Contact Point | Distance from ideal strike zone | < 5 cm average error |

### 3. The 5-Pillar Quality Integration
*   **Pillar I (Biomechanics):** Quality reflects kinetic chain efficiency
*   **Pillar III (Technique):** Consistency reflects stroke repeatability
*   **Pillar IV (Tactics):** Depth consistency reflects tactical control
*   **Pillar V (Conditioning):** Quality maintenance under fatigue reflects fitness

## The Quality Training Protocol
1.  **Max-Q Sets:** 10 balls maximum per set — stop if 3 consecutive balls miss quality threshold
2.  **Quality Scoring:** Each ball scored 1-4 (1 = error, 2 = playable, 3 = good, 4 = weapon). Set average must be ≥ 3.0
3.  **Constraint-Q:** Place targets at 80% of personal best — only perfect execution scores

## Practical Application: "Perfect Practice"
Don't count the balls you hit — count the balls you hit well. One perfect rep is worth more than a hundred sloppy ones.
