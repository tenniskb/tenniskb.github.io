# ART-166: HRV-Guided Training — Training by the Numbers

## Executive Summary
Heart Rate Variability (HRV) is the window into your autonomic nervous system. This article explains how to use daily HRV readings to modulate training intensity, prevent overtraining, and ensure every session is precisely calibrated to your body's readiness.

## The Science of HRV

### 1. What Is HRV?
HRV measures the variation in time between consecutive heartbeats. Higher HRV = parasympathetic dominance = recovery ready. Lower HRV = sympathetic dominance = stress/fatigue present.

### 2. The 5-Pillar HRV Integration
*   **Pillar V (Conditioning):** HRV is THE recovery and readiness biomarker
*   **Pillar II (Neuro-Cognition):** Low HRV = slower reaction times, impaired decision-making
*   **Pillar I (Biomechanics):** Low HRV = altered movement mechanics, injury risk
*   **Pillar III (Technique):** Low HRV = degraded stroke quality
*   **Pillar IV (Tactics):** Low HRV = rigid thinking, poor adaptation

## The HRV-Guided Protocol

### 1. Morning Measurement Protocol
*   Measure immediately upon waking (before getting out of bed)
*   Use a chest strap or finger sensor + validated app (Elite HRV, HRV4Training)
*   Record 1-2 minutes of still, supine measurement

### 2. Daily Interpretation
| Reading | Status | Training Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Within 7-day avg ± 5% | Green | Proceed as planned |
| > 10% below 7-day avg | Yellow | Reduce intensity 20%, volume 30% |
| > 20% below 7-day avg | Red | Recovery day only — no intense training |

### 3. Weekly HRV Trends
*   **Rising trend:** Adapting well — can increase load
*   **Plateau trend:** Maintaining — stable load appropriate
*   **Falling trend:** Overreaching — must reduce load

## Practical Application: "Train the Readiness"
Don't train the plan — train the body. HRV tells you what your body can handle today. Listen to it.
