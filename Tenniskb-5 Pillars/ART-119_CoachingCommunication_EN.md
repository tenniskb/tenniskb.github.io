# ART-119: Coaching Communication — Cue Systems Across the 5 Pillars

## Executive Summary
The right cue at the right time accelerates learning; the wrong cue creates interference. This article establishes a disciplined cueing framework for each pillar: external versus internal cues, constraint-based learning, and feedback timing protocols that respect how motor skills are actually acquired.

## The Cue Discipline Framework

### 1. External vs Internal Cueing
Research consistently shows external cues (focus on effect) outperform internal cues (focus on body parts) for skilled performance.
*   **Internal (avoid in competition):** "Bend your knees," "Rotate your hips," "Extend your elbow."
*   **External (prefer):** "Push the court away," "Crack the whip at the ball," "Send the ball over the high part of the net."

### 2. Pillar-Specific Cue Libraries

**Pillar I (Biomechanics) — Cues:**
*   GRF loading: "Load the ground like a spring."
*   Pelvic snap: "Belt buckle to the target."
*   Core bracing: "Brace like taking a punch."

**Pillar II (Neuro-Cognition) — Cues:**
*   Quiet Eye: "Watch the seam."
*   Split-step: "Float when they hit."
*   Anticipation: "Read the shoulder."

**Pillar III (Technical) — Cues:**
*   Forehand lag: "Drag the racket butt forward."
*   Serve pronation: "Hit up and out to 2 o'clock."
*   Volley firmness: "Catch, don't swing."

**Pillar IV (Tactical) — Cues:**
*   Recovery: "Own the bisector."
*   High-percentage: "Big target first."
*   Patterns: "Two to the backhand, then change."

**Pillar V (Conditioning/Flow) — Cues:**
*   Between points: "Exhale, reset, next."
*   Pressure: "Embrace the moment."
*   Fatigue: "Attack with your legs."

### 3. Feedback Timing Protocols
*   **During play:** Zero technical feedback. Cue only intention or tactic ("push through the middle").
*   **Immediate post-rep:** One cue maximum. Bandwidth: one correction per repetition.
*   **Block review:** Video + data, pattern-level discussion, never a list of 10 fixes.

### 4. Constraint-Led Environment Design
Instead of telling, design the environment to force the desired behavior:
*   Want deeper balls? Place a rope 1 m inside the baseline as a bonus landing zone.
*   Want better split-step timing? Use a ball machine with randomized feed intervals.
*   Want forward momentum? Place a cone line to cross after the approach shot.

## Practical Application: The "One Cue at a Time" Rule
Coach the constraint, not the athlete's body. The brain self-organizes around clear goals; overload it with corrections and it freezes.
