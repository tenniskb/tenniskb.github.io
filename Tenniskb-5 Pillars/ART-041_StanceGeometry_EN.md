# ART-041: Forehand Stance Geometry & Ground Reaction Vector Alignment

## Executive Summary
The geometry of the forehand stance determines which Ground Reaction Force (GRF) vectors are harvested and how they are converted into rotational torque or linear momentum. The three primary stances—Open, Semi-Open, and Closed—each align the body differently relative to the target line, altering the dominant force vectors (Fx, Fy, Fz) and the resulting kinetic chain dynamics.

## The Stance Geometry Framework

### 1. Open Stance (Modern Power Stance)
*   **Foot Alignment:** The feet are parallel to the baseline or slightly open to the target. The outside leg is heavily loaded.
*   **Dominant Vector:** Medial-Lateral (Fx). The lateral load on the outside leg creates a powerful rotational torque through the pelvis.
*   **GRF Harvesting:** Vertical (Fz) is moderate; Linear (Fy) is minimal. The energy is primarily rotational.
*   **Best For:** High-velocity open-stance forehands with extreme topspin; handling high balls.

### 2. Semi-Open Stance (The Hybrid)
*   **Foot Alignment:** The front foot is angled toward the net, while the back foot remains closer to parallel with the baseline.
*   **Dominant Vectors:** A balanced mix of Linear (Fy) and Lateral (Fx). The front foot allows forward weight transfer, while the back foot provides rotational anchor.
*   **GRF Harvesting:** All three vectors (Fx, Fy, Fz) are harvested in balanced proportion.
*   **Best For:** Versatile groundstrokes; transitioning between defense and offense.

### 3. Closed Stance (Classic Linear Stance)
*   **Foot Alignment:** The feet are perpendicular to the target line; the body is fully turned sideways.
*   **Dominant Vector:** Anterior-Posterior (Fy). The weight transfers directly forward through the ball.
*   **GRF Harvesting:** High Linear (Fy) and Vertical (Fz); Low Lateral (Fx). Rotation is minimized.
*   **Best For:** Deep, penetrating shots; approach shots; closed-court situations.

## The Vector Alignment Impact

| Stance | Fx (Lateral) | Fy (Linear) | Fz (Vertical) | Energy Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Open | **High** | Low | Medium | **Rotational Power** |
| Semi-Open | **Medium** | Medium | Medium | **Balanced Power** |
| Closed | Low | **High** | **High** | **Linear Penetration** |

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Geometry Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ball sprays wide | Incorrect Stance for Shot Type | Using a linear stance for a rotational shot (or vice versa). | **Stance-Matching Drill:** Practice the same shot with all three stances to feel the difference. |
| Lack of penetration | Over-use of Open Stance | Too much rotation, not enough forward weight transfer. | **Closed-Stance Drill:** Force forward weight transfer by aligning feet perpendicular to target. |
| Unstable balance | Mixed Foot Alignment | The body is not aligned with the dominant vector, causing energy leaks. | **Foot-Position Check:** Use court lines as reference points to ensure consistent alignment. |

## Practical Application: The "Geometry Match"
The athlete must match the stance to the tactical situation. A defensive high ball may require an open stance for rotational recovery; a deep approach shot requires a closed stance for linear penetration. The geometry of the feet determines the physics of the shot.
