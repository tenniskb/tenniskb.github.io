# ART-187: The Science of Periodization — Planning the Season

## Executive Summary
Champions are made in the off-season and revealed in the tournament season. This article provides a comprehensive periodization framework covering all 5 Pillars, ensuring peak performance when it matters most.

## The Periodization Framework

### 1. The Annual Plan Structure
*   **Off-Season (8-12 weeks):** Build foundation, address weaknesses, physical development
*   **Pre-Season (4-6 weeks):** Transition to tennis-specific work, intensify conditioning
*   **Competition Season (20-30 weeks):** Maintain fitness, peak for major tournaments, manage load
*   **Transition Phase (2-4 weeks):** Active recovery, mental reset, injury rehabilitation

### 2. The 5-Pillar Periodization

**Pillar I (Biomechanics):**
*   Off-Season: Movement pattern correction, mobility development
*   Pre-Season: Tennis-specific movement integration
*   Competition: Maintain movement quality under fatigue
*   Transition: Address chronic movement issues

**Pillar II (Neuro-Cognition):**
*   Off-Season: Foundational cognitive training, reaction time development
*   Pre-Season: Tennis-specific decision-making drills
*   Competition: Maintain cognitive sharpness, manage cognitive load
*   Transition: Mental recovery and reset

**Pillar III (Technical):**
*   Off-Season: Major technique changes, grooving new patterns
*   Pre-Season: Integrate techniques into match play
*   Competition: Maintain technique under pressure
*   Transition: Light feel work, equipment experimentation

**Pillar IV (Tactics):**
*   Off-Season: Learn new patterns, study opponents
*   Pre-Season: Integrate tactics into practice matches
*   Competition: Adapt tactics to opponents, refine game plans
*   Transition: Tactical review and planning

**Pillar V (Conditioning):**
*   Off-Season: Build aerobic base, strength development
*   Pre-Season: Anaerobic power, RSA development
*   Competition: Maintain fitness, manage load, peak for majors
*   Transition: Active recovery, recreational activities

### 3. The Taper Protocol (1-2 weeks before major tournament)
*   Reduce volume 40-60%
*   Maintain intensity (keep the sharpness)
*   Increase recovery (sleep, nutrition, mental preparation)
*   Reduce cognitive load (simpler game plans)

## Practical Application: "The Long Game"
Tennis is not won in a week — it's won over a year. Plan the season, peak for the majors, and trust the process.
