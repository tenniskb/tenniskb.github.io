# ART-143: Advanced Forehand Mechanics — The Foundation of Power

## Executive Summary
The forehand is the most used stroke in tennis, yet it is often the most neglected in training. This article breaks down the biomechanics of elite forehand production, showing how the 5-Pillar system integrates into a power foundation. It provides a framework for building a custom, power-adapted forehand system.

## The Forehand Integration Framework

### 1. The Forehand Decision Cycle
Elite players use a 5-step decision cycle:
1.  **Pillar Analysis:** Identify which pillar needs optimization (e.g., "My forehand is inconsistent because of my kinetic chain alignment").
2.  **Forehand Gap Analysis:** Calculate what the forehand doesn't provide (e.g., "My forehand is inconsistent because of my kinetic chain alignment").
3.  **Margin Analysis:** Calculate the cost of each option (e.g., "If I adjust my kinetic chain alignment, I'll have to adjust my forehand technique but gain better forehand speed").
4.  **Implementation:** Adjust training to accommodate the new forehand.
5.  **Evaluation:** Measure the impact on performance (e.g., "My forehand speed increased by 5 km/h").

### 2. The Forehand Memory System
The brain stores three types of forehand knowledge:
*   **Procedural:** Automatic responses to common situations (e.g., "When I hit a kick serve, I always move to the backhand side").
*   **Declarative:** Explicit rules (e.g., "I never attack a serve-and-volleyer's second serve").
*   **Episodic:** Context-specific memories (e.g., "In this match, he's been hitting his first serves to the deuce side").
