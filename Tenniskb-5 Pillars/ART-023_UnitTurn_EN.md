# ART-023: Unit Turn Coiling Velocity & Energy Storage

## Executive Summary
The "Unit Turn" is the foundational movement of every tennis stroke. Rather than simply rotating the shoulders, a professional unit turn is a full-body "coil" that prepares the kinetic chain for acceleration. The goal is to maximize the velocity of the coil to store the highest possible amount of elastic potential energy in the core and shoulders before the forward drive begins.

## The Anatomy of the Coil

### 1. Synchronized Rotation
A proper unit turn involves the simultaneous rotation of the shoulders, chest, and hips. 
*   **The "Unit" Concept:** The upper body moves as one single piece. If the arms move independently of the torso, the coil is broken, and energy is leaked.
*   **The Angle:** The goal is to achieve a significant angular separation between the chest and the target line.

### 2. Coiling Velocity (The Speed of the Turn)
The speed at which the player turns is just as important as the depth of the turn.
*   **Slow Coil:** Allows for stability but results in lower stored energy.
*   **Fast Coil:** If the turn is executed with a specific "snap," it creates a dynamic stretch in the muscles, significantly increasing the elastic potential energy stored in the fascial lines.

## The Energy Storage Mechanism
The unit turn acts as a biological battery.
*   **Elastic Potential Energy:** As the obliques and lats are stretched, they store energy.
*   **Torsional Strain:** The twist in the spine and core creates a "winding" effect, similar to twisting a rubber band.
*   **The Trigger:** This stored energy is what allows the player to explode forward without needing to "push" with the arms.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Coil Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "Arm-only" turn | Lack of Unit movement | Shoulders rotate but hips stay flat. | **Wall-Touch Drill:** Stand with back to wall; turn so that the shoulder touches the wall while the hips stay square. |
| Slow, lethargic turn | Low Coiling Velocity | Low power in the shot; "lazy" swing. | **Rhythm-Taps:** Use a metronome to practice fast, sharp unit turns. |
| Inconsistent coil | Poor timing | The turn is too early or too late relative to the ball. | **Ball-Drop Timing:** Turn only when the ball is at a specific height during a drop-feed. |

## Practical Application: The "Winding" Sensation
The unit turn should feel like winding up a clock. You are not just moving your body; you are "charging" the system. The faster and more synchronized the wind-up, the more explosive the release.
