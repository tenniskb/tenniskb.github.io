# ART-133: Post-Match Decompression — The Recovery Window

## Executive Summary
The match doesn't end at the final point; the recovery window begins. This article examines the physiological and psychological "cooldown" phase, focusing on how to effectively transition the body and mind from high-arousal competition to a state of optimal repair.

## The Decompression Timeline

### 1. Immediate Post-Match (0-30 min)
*   **Physiological:** Gradual heart rate reduction. Avoid sudden stops; perform 5-10 min of light walking/stretching.
*   **Hydration/Nutrition:** Immediate intake of fast-absorbing electrolytes and simple carbs to stop cortisol spike.
*   **Cognitive:** "The Cool-Down Zone." No immediate tactical analysis. Allow emotions to settle.

### 2. Short-Term Recovery (30 min - 4 hours)
*   **Temperature Therapy:** Contrast baths (hot/cold) or ice baths to reduce systemic inflammation and muscle soreness.
*   **Compression:** Use of compression garments to assist venous return and reduce edema.
*   **Nutrition:** High-protein window (20-30g) to initiate myofibrillar repair.

### 3. Long-Term Reset (4 hours - 24 hours)
*   **Sleep Hygiene:** Prioritize 8-9 hours of sleep. The brain processes tactical errors and consolidates technical gains during REM.
*   **Active Recovery:** Light movement (swimming, walking) to maintain circulation without adding load.
*   **The "Analytical Shift":** Only now is it time for the detailed match review (refer to ART-129).

## The Psychological Decompression Loop
Post-match "highs" and "lows" can distort the perception of performance.
*   **Victory Trap:** Over-estimating skill, leading to complacency in training.
*   **Loss Trap:** Over-estimating failure, leading to "over-fixing" and technical instability.
*   **The Solution:** Objective data tagging (ART-129) overrides the emotional narrative.

## Pillar Integration

*   **Pillar I:** Myofascial release (foam rolling) returns the kinetic chain to its resting length.
*   **Pillar II:** Sleep is the ultimate neurological reset; it clears metabolic waste from the brain.
*   **Pillar III:** Technical errors are processed subconsciously during sleep, improving "feel" the next day.
*   **Pillar IV:** Tactical failures are analyzed logically only after the arousal has dropped.
*   **Pillar V:** Recovery protocols are the foundation of long-term conditioning.

## Practical Application: The "Off-Switch"
The ability to "turn off" the competitive drive is as important as the ability to "turn it on." Master the decompression to ensure the next session starts at 100%.
