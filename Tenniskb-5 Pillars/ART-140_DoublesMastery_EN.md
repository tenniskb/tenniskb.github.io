# ART-140: The Science of Doubles Mastery

## Executive Summary
Doubles is a different sport: the geometry shrinks horizontally, the net dominates, and coordination between partners outweighs individual brilliance. This article applies the 5-Pillar framework to doubles, covering formations, communication, and point patterns that win at elite doubles level. It provides a framework for building a custom, doubles-adapted system.

## The Doubles Integration Framework

### 1. The Doubles Decision Cycle
Elite players use a 5-step decision cycle:
1.  **Pillar Analysis:** Identify which pillar needs optimization (e.g., "My doubles partner's serve is inconsistent because of his serve technique").
2.  **Doubles Gap Analysis:** Calculate what the doubles partner doesn't provide (e.g., "My doubles partner's serve is inconsistent because of his serve technique").
3.  **Margin Analysis:** Calculate the cost of each option (e.g., "If I adjust my serve technique, I'll have to adjust my serve technique but gain better serve speed").
4.  **Implementation:** Adjust training to accommodate the new doubles partner.
5.  **Evaluation:** Measure the impact on performance (e.g., "My serve speed increased by 5 km/h").

### 2. The Doubles Memory System
The brain stores three types of doubles knowledge:
*   **Procedural:** Automatic responses to common situations (e.g., "When I hit a kick serve, I always move to the backhand side").
*   **Declarative:** Explicit rules (e.g., "I never attack a serve-and-volleyer's second serve").
*   **Episodic:** Context-specific memories (e.g., "In this match, he's been hitting his first serves to the deuce side").
