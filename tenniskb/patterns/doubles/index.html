    Doubles Systems¶

    Three Systems. Four Roles. One Language.

    The Three Doubles Systems¶

    System Name Best For Key Principle
    1 Two Back Both players baseline comfort Grind, wait for short, transition together
    2 One Up One Back Strong server/returner + net player Server/returner sets up, net player finishes
    3 Two Up Both comfortable at net Pressure, take time away, end points fast

    System 1: Two Back¶

    When to Use¶

    • Both players prefer baseline
    • Opponents have big serves/returns
    • Windy conditions
    • Slow courts

    Positioning¶

    [Server/Returner]     [Partner]
     ●                    ●
   BASELINE            BASELINE

    Rules¶

    1. Move as a unit - lateral shift together
    2. Cross-court until short - high margin
    3. Short ball = BOTH transition - no solo missions
    4. Lob coverage - deep player takes lob, net player covers middle

    Communication¶

    Call Meaning
    "Mine" I take it
    "Yours" You take it
    "Switch" We swap sides
    "Up" Both transition to net
    "Back" Lob coming, deep player retreats

    System 2: One Up One Back (The Standard)¶

    Roles¶

    Position Name Primary Job
    Server/Returner Setter Serve/return to set up net player
    Net Player Finisher Poach, put away, cover middle

    Server's Partner (Net) Positioning¶

    Serve Target Net Player Start Shift On Serve
    Wide Center of service box Shade toward alley
    T Center of service box Shade toward middle
    Body Slightly toward middle Hold middle

    Returner's Partner (Net) Positioning¶

    Return Type Net Player Action
    Cross-court Hold middle, threaten poach
    Down the line Cover alley, don't over-poach
    Lob Call "Back", retreat to service line
    Short/Weak POACH - call "Mine", attack

    The Poach Rules¶

    1. Early commitment - move as returner swings
    2. Call "Mine!" - loud, early
    3. Angle volleys - put away, don't push
    4. If missed - partner covers cross-court

    System 3: Two Up¶

    When to Use¶

    • Both players strong volleys/overheads
    • Fast courts
    • Opponents struggle with passing shots
    • Need to change momentum

    Positioning¶

    [Server] → moves to NET
[Partner] → already at NET

[Returner] → chips & charges to NET
[Partner] → already at NET

    Rules¶

    1. First volley deep - push them back
    2. Middle coverage - net players shade middle
    3. Lob defense - closest retreats, other covers middle
    4. No gaps - move as a wall

    The Four Roles (Universal)¶

    Role Position Mindset Key Skills
    Server Baseline → Net "Set up my partner" Serve placement, 1st volley
    Returner Baseline → Net "Neutralize, then attack" Return depth, chip/charge
    Net Player (Serving) Service line → Net Finish the point Poach, volley, overhead
    Net Player (Receiving) Service line → Net Disrupt & finish Poach, fake, communicate

    Communication System¶

    Pre-Point (Hand Signals Behind Back)¶

    Signal Meaning
    1 finger Serve wide
    2 fingers Serve T
    3 fingers Serve body
    Fist Serve & stay back (Two Back)
    Open hand Serve & come in (One Up)
    Point down Stay back on return
    Point up Chip & charge

    During Point (Verbal)¶

    Call When Who
    "Mine!" Taking ball Either
    "Yours!" Defer to partner Either
    "Switch!" Crossed, swap sides Either
    "Up!" Both come in Either
    "Back!" Lob, retreat Net player
    "Poach!" Committing to cross Net player
    "Fake!" Faking poach Net player
    "Line!" Cover alley Net player
    "Middle!" Cover center Net player

    Doubles CORE Application¶

    Variable Doubles Adjustment
    Q Eyes on opponent contact → instant read of return/volley
    V Head still on volleys - no head movement = face stable
    ROOT Split step at opponent contact = instant readiness
    8 Volleys = NO 8. Groundstrokes = small 8.
    45° Volley face = target. Overhead = 45° glass.
    BRAKE Front foot stomp on EVERY volley/overhead
    PULSE 1-inch volley pulse. Overhead = serve pulse.
    JIN Hua dominant - absorb pace, redirect. Ting feels volleys.

    Common Formations¶

    Serving: "I" Formation¶

        [Net Partner]
       ●
    [Server] → serves from middle, moves to side
       ●
    - Server serves from center mark - Net partner starts on center line - After serve: server goes to side, partner covers middle - Confuses returner, opens poaching angles

    Serving: Australian¶

    [Net Partner] ●
[Server]      ● (serves from same side as partner)
    - Both on same side - Forces return down the line - Net partner poaches cross-court

    Receiving: Standard¶

    [Returner] ● (baseline)
[Partner]  ● (service line, middle)

    Receiving: Two Back¶

    [Returner] ● (baseline)
[Partner]  ● (baseline)
    - Against big servers - Neutralize, then transition

    The Doubles Quick-Scan¶

    SERVE: Signal → Place → Move → Partner Poaches → Finish

    RETURN: Read → Depth → Partner Threatens → Neutralize → Transition

    RALLY: Middle → Middle → Middle → Short → ATTACK

    LOB: "Back!" → Retreat → Partner Covers Middle → Reset

    Checklists¶

    Server¶

    • Signal agreed with partner
    • Serve to target (wide/T/body)
    • Move to net (or stay per system)
    • First volley deep to weak side
    • Communicate every ball

    Returner¶

    • Read server tendencies
    • Return DEEP cross-court (default)
    • Chip & charge if agreed
    • Move to net behind return
    • Cover lob if partner poaches

    Net Player (Serving)¶

    • Start position per serve target
    • Read returner swing early
    • Poach on weak/cross-court
    • Call "Mine!" loud & early
    • Angle volley to open court
    • Cover middle on partner's volley

    Net Player (Receiving)¶

    • Start middle of service box
    • Threaten poach on cross-court
    • Fake poach on down-the-line
    • Call "Back!" instantly on lob
    • Cover middle always
