Approach Shot Fundamentals

The transition game — ball selection, directional control, and net approach. The approach shot is the bridge from baseline to net.

Key Principles

  • Hit and move forward — don't stay in no-man's land
  • Deep approach shot — push opponent back, give yourself time to close
  • Directional control — down-the-line or sharp angle; crosscourt approach = vulnerable to passing shot
  • Split step at opponent's contact — even when moving forward

Approach Shot Types

Situation Approach Shot Target Follow-up
Short ball, mid-court Drive approach (flat or topspin) Down-the-line or sharp angle Move to net, first volley deep
Short ball, inside baseline Angle approach Sharp crosscourt angle Close net, put-away volley
Floating ball Put-away approach Down-the-line winner or angle Net for finish
Weak second serve return Chip-and-charge Deep down-the-line Immediate net pressure

Technical Keys

  1. Contact in front — same as groundstroke but moving forward
  2. Compact takeback — no time for full loop; short preparation
  3. Firm wrist — control face angle through contact
  4. Step into the shot — weight transfers forward through contact
  5. Eyes on contact zone — Quiet Eye locks target; head still

Fault Signals

Symptom Root Cause Fix
Approach floats long Face open, late contact Contact further in front, close face
Approach into net Face closed, tight arm Loose arm, open face slightly
Passed down the line Crosscourt approach Approach down-the-line or sharp angle
Stuck in no-man's land Didn't move forward after hit Hit and move — immediate split step

Drills

  1. Approach + volley sequence: Partner feeds short ball; hit approach, move to net, hit volley. 20 reps each side.
  2. Directional control: Target cones for down-the-line vs angle approaches. 15 each.
  3. Chip-and-charge: Return second serve with chip, immediate net rush. 20 reps.

Source: United Tennis Handbook — Stroke Systems
