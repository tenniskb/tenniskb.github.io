## Executive Abstract

While wide slice serves are popular in singles, in doubles, serving out wide exposes the serving team to dangerous sharp crosscourt angles and down-the-line returns into the open alley. Serving down the center 'T' or directly into the receiver's dominant hip reduces the returner's available angle of reflection from 42° down to 14°, setting up guaranteed poach volleys for the net partner.

## 1. Angle of Reflection Geometric Compression

A serve hitting the center 'T' pins the receiver's contact point to the center axis of the court. The receiver cannot hit a sharp angle without pulling the ball wide into the net.

## 2. Jamming the Returner's Dominant Hip

Directing the serve at the returner's right hip (for a right-hander) prevents a full unit turn, forcing a weak, floating block return straight to the waiting poacher at the net.

## 3. Doubles Serve Target Allocation Matrix

Deuce Court: 65% 'T', 25% Body, 10% Wide; Ad Court: 70% 'T', 20% Body, 10% Wide.
