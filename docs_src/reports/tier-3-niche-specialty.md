## 1. Real Tennis / Jeu de paume

**Books in library on this topic:** 1 (referenced)
**Source:** `Review_of_tennis_ball_aerodynamics.pdf` (historical background section)
**Suggested repo location:** new article `history/Real-Tennis-Jeu-de-Paume.md`

### What the books say

- Real tennis originated in France as *jeu de paume* ("the game of the
  palm"), 12th century.
- Played on a stone surface surrounded by four high walls and a sloping
  roof.
- The present lopsided racket head emerged around 1750, enabling players
  to scoop balls out of corners and put *cut* (spin) on the ball.
- The oldest surviving real tennis court is at Hampton Court Palace, built
  by King Henry VIII around 1530.
- Lawn tennis was derived from real tennis in 1873 by Major Walter
  Wingfield (Welsh army officer).

## 2. "Rough" / "Smooth"

**Books in library on this topic:** 1 (referenced)
**Source:** `Review_of_tennis_ball_aerodynamics.pdf` (historical background)
**Suggested repo location:** new article `history/Rough-and-Smooth.md`

### What the books say

Historical tiebreaker convention: when rackets were strung by looping
side strings around the main strings, the resulting surface was uneven.
Players would "call rough or smooth" — whichever side they ended up with
after winning the racket spin at the start of a match. The phrase
survives in tennis vocabulary even though modern rackets no longer have
a rough / smooth distinction.

## 3. Junior red / orange / green / yellow ball progression (ITF Tennis 10s)

**Books in library on this topic:** 7
**Source:** `Coaching_Tennis_Successfully.docx`, `ACQUIRING_VISION_SKILLS_ESSENTIAL_FOR_TENNIS.pdf`, `ITF_-_Coaching_and_Science_Review.pdf`, `Revolution_Tennis_Vision_drills.pdf`, `Technical_Tennis.docx`
**Suggested repo location:** new section `junior/ITF-Tennis-10s-Ball-Progression.md`

### What the books say

The ITF Tennis 10s curriculum stages junior development by ball compression
and court size:

- **Red ball** — age 5–8, 25 % court, foam / low-compression ball
- **Orange ball** — age 8–10, 50 % court, low-compression ball
- **Green ball** — age 10–11, full court, low-compression ball
- **Yellow ball** — age 11+, full court, standard ITF-approved ball

Each stage has its own rally-length target, racket size, and recommended
training drill set.

The repo has `high-performance-toc` and `starting-kids-right-the-forehand`

## 4. Open-skill sport pedagogy (Wayne Elderton)

**Books in library on this topic:** 2
**Source:** `LEARN_TENNIS_AS_AN_OPEN_SKILL_SPORT-_Wayne_Elderton.pdf`, `Movement-for-Tennis.pdf`
**Suggested repo location:** new section `coaching/Open-Skill-Sport-Pedagogy.md`

### What the books say

Open-skill vs closed-skill sport distinction:

- **Closed skill** — predictable environment (golf swing, free throw).
  Practice the same motor pattern repeatedly.
- **Open skill** — unpredictable environment (tennis rally). Practice
  *reading* the environment and adapting.

Elderton's framework: tennis is a paradigmatic open-skill sport. The
ecological-dynamics approach (perception-action coupling, constraints-led
coaching) replaces the traditional *techniques-first* pedagogy with a
*constraints-first* one.

### Why the repo doesn't have this

The repo has `agentic-strategy` but not this specific pedagogical
framework. The constraints-led approach is increasingly the dominant
framework in tennis coaching research.

## 5. Open Era timeline

**Books in library on this topic:** 15
**Source:** `Brief-History-of-Tennis.docx`, `Advantage_Canada__a_tennis_centenary.pdf`, multiple player biographies including `Carlos_Alcaraz.docx` and `Tennis_Books_Collection_Trailblazers_-_Billie_Jean_King.pdf`
**Suggested repo location:** new section `history/Open-Era-Timeline.md`

### What the books say

The Open Era began in 1968 when Grand Slam tournaments allowed professional
players to compete alongside amateurs. Key milestones:

- 1968 — first Open Slam (Rod Laver wins the Grand Slam)
- 1968–1977 — early Open Era, dominated by Laver, Rosewall, Smith
- 1977–1983 — Borg / Connors / McEnroe era
- 1984–1994 — Lendl / Becker / Edberg / Chang era
- 1995–2003 — Sampras / Agassi era
- 2003–2016 — Federer / Nadal / Djokovic "Big Three" era
- 2017–present — post-Big-Three, Alcaraz / Sinner era

The repo has player profiles but no chronological timeline tying them
together.
