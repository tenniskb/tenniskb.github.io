# Open Era tennis timeline (1968–2026)

**The Open Era began in 1968** when all four Grand Slam tournaments opened to professional players, ending the previous amateur/professional divide. This reference compiles the era-defining milestones, dominant players, and rule changes that shaped modern tennis.

---

## Era 1: 1968–1977 — the first Open Era decade

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| 1968 | All four Grand Slams open to professionals; first "Open Slam" |
| 1969 | **Rod Laver wins the calendar-year Grand Slam** — the last male player to do so |
| 1968–1977 | Era dominated by Rod Laver, Ken Rosewall, Arthur Ashe, Stan Smith |

Notable figures:

- **Rod Laver (AUS)** — won 11 Grand Slam singles titles (5 amateur, 6 Open); only man to win two calendar-year Grand Slams (1962, 1969)
- **Ken Rosewall (AUS)** — won 8 Grand Slam singles titles across both eras
- **Arthur Ashe (USA)** — won 3 Grand Slam singles titles; first Black man to win a Grand Slam (US Open 1968); AIDS activist
- **Billie Jean King (USA)** — won 12 Grand Slam singles titles; founder of WTA (1973); won Battle of the Sexes against Bobby Riggs

---

## Era 2: 1977–1983 — Borg / Connors / McEnroe

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| 1977 | **Björn Borg wins Wimbledon at age 20** (start of five straight Wimbledon titles) |
| 1978 | Borg wins first French Open (start of six straight French Open titles) |
| 1980 | Borg completes the **French Open + Wimbledon double for the third straight year** |
| 1981 | **John McEnroe wins Wimbledon** on his way to 3 Wimbledon / 4 US Open titles |
| 1982 | McEnroe completes the calendar-year "near-Slam" (missed only the French Open) |
| 1983 | **McEnroe wins Wimbledon and US Open in the same year** |

Notable figures:

- **Björn Borg (SWE)** — won 11 Grand Slam singles titles (6 French Open, 5 Wimbledon); only man to win French Open + Wimbledon three consecutive years (1978–1980); ice-cold temperament, two-handed backhand
- **Jimmy Connors (USA)** — won 8 Grand Slam singles titles; longest #1 ranking (268 weeks); left-handed, flat groundstrokes
- **John McEnroe (USA)** — won 7 Grand Slam singles titles; famous for shot-making genius and on-court tantrums; serve-and-volley pioneer

---

## Era 3: 1984–1994 — Lendl / Becker / Edberg / Chang

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| 1984 | **Ivan Lendl wins first of 8 Grand Slam singles titles** |
| 1987 | **Boris Becker wins Wimbledon at age 17** — youngest-ever men's Wimbledon champion |
| 1989 | **Michael Chang wins French Open at age 17** — youngest-ever men's Grand Slam champion |
| 1992 | **Andre Agassi reaches first Grand Slam final** (Wimbledon, lost to Andre Medved); wins first Grand Slam at Wimbledon 1992 |
| 1993 | **Pete Sampras wins first Grand Slam** (US Open) at age 22 |

Notable figures:

- **Ivan Lendl (CZE/USA)** — won 8 Grand Slam singles titles; 270 weeks at #1 (record at the time); pioneered baseline power tennis
- **Boris Becker (GER)** — won 6 Grand Slam singles titles; 3 Wimbledon titles; pioneer of explosive power serving
- **Stefan Edberg (SWE)** — won 6 Grand Slam singles titles; classic serve-and-volley player
- **Andre Agassi (USA)** — won 8 Grand Slam singles titles (career Golden Slam 1999); pioneer of return-of-serve as a weapon
- **Pete Sampras (USA)** — won 14 Grand Slam singles titles (record until Federer); 7 Wimbledon titles; dominated grass courts

---

## Era 4: 1995–2003 — Sampras / Agassi dominance

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| 1999 | **Agassi wins French Open → completes career Golden Slam** (only man at the time) |
| 2000 | **Marat Safin wins US Open at age 20** |
| 2001 | **Lleyton Hewitt becomes youngest-ever year-end #1 at age 20** |
| 2002 | **Andre Agassi wins Australian Open** — completes career Grand Slam |

Notable figures:

- **Pete Sampras** — 14 Grand Slam titles; "Pistol Pete"; serve-dominated grass-court tennis
- **Andre Agassi** — 8 Grand Slam titles; baseline power; comeback player of the era
- **Lleyton Hewitt (AUS)** — 2 Grand Slam titles; youngest-ever year-end #1 at the time; relentless returner
- **Marat Safin (RUS)** — 2 Grand Slam titles; powerful but volatile

---

## Era 5: 2003–2016 — the Big Three era (Federer / Nadal / Djokovic)

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| 2003 | **Roger Federer wins first Grand Slam** (Wimbledon); start of era of dominance |
| 2004 | Federer wins 3 Grand Slams; holds all four simultaneously at one point (Wimbledon, US Open, Australian Open, French Open final) |
| 2005 | **Rafael Nadal wins first French Open** (start of record 14 French Open titles) |
| 2008 | **Rafael Nadal wins Wimbledon** — dethrones Federer; final considered greatest match ever played |
| 2008 | **Novak Djokovic wins first Grand Slam** (Australian Open) |
| 2009 | Federer completes career Grand Slam (French Open) |
| 2010 | Nadal completes career Grand Slam (US Open) |
| 2016 | **Djokovic completes career Grand Slam** (French Open) |

Notable figures:

- **Roger Federer (SUI)** — **20 Grand Slam singles titles** (still the men's record); elegant all-court style; 5 consecutive Wimbledon titles (2003–2007); 237 consecutive weeks at #1
- **Rafael Nadal (ESP)** — **22 Grand Slam singles titles**; record 14 French Open titles; clay-court GOAT; intense competitive spirit
- **Novak Djokovic (SRB)** — **24 Grand Slam singles titles** (men's all-time record); 428 weeks at #1 (men's all-time record); 8+ Australian Open titles

---

## Era 6: 2017–2024 — the late Big Three era

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| 2017 | Federer wins Australian Open (18th Grand Slam) |
| 2018 | Djokovic completes "Novak Slam" (held 3 of 4 simultaneously) |
| 2019 | Nadal wins US Open (19th Grand Slam); Federer wins Wimbledon (21st) |
| 2020 | Nadal ties Federer at 20 Grand Slams (French Open) |
| 2021 | Djokovic wins 3 Grand Slams; falls short of calendar Slam at US Open final |
| 2022 | **Carlos Alcaraz wins US Open at age 19** — youngest-ever men's Grand Slam champion; **Federer retires at Laver Cup** |
| 2023 | Djokovic wins 3 Grand Slams (Australian, French, US); 22nd Grand Slam at US Open |
| 2023 | **Alcaraz wins Wimbledon** (over Djokovic) at age 20 |
| 2024 | Djokovic wins Olympic gold in Paris (the one major missing from his CV); **Nadal loses final match at Davis Cup** |

---

## Era 7: 2025–present — the Alcaraz-Sinner era

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| 2025 | **Alcaraz wins 3 Grand Slams** (Australian, French, Wimbledon); finishes year-end #1 |
| 2025 | **Sinner wins 2 Grand Slams** (Australian, US); finishes year-end #2 |
| 2025 | Djokovic passes Federer for most Grand Slam titles (24 → 25 → ...) |
| 2025 | **Björn Borg quote (Marca, 2025):** *"I think Jannik is more aggressive, but if I have to choose, I'd say Carlos because of how he feels the ball."* |

Notable players:

- **Carlos Alcaraz (ESP)** — 6+ Grand Slam titles by age 22; 2025 year-end #1; "fire and ice" — combines modern physicality with old-school variety
- **Jannik Sinner (ITA)** — 4+ Grand Slam titles; first Italian man to win Wimbledon; relentless aggression

## WTA timeline highlights

| Era | Dominant players | Notable achievements |
|-----|------------------|----------------------|
| 1970s | King, Evert, Navratilova (amateur-pro crossover) | King founds WTA (1973); wins Battle of the Sexes |
| 1980s | Evert, Navratilova | Navratilova dominates (18 Grand Slam singles); 74-match win streak (1984) |
| 1990s | Graf, Seles, Sabatini | **Steffi Graf wins Golden Slam** (all 4 Slams + Olympic gold, 1988); 22 Grand Slam singles titles |
| 2000s | Williams sisters, Henin, Clijsters | **Serena Williams wins 23 Grand Slams**; Henin-Hardenne wins 7 |
| 2010s | Williams sisters, Halep, Osaka | Serena continues dominance; Halep wins 2 Slams; Osaka wins 4 (incl. 2 US Opens) |
| 2020s | Barty, Swiatek, Sabalenka, Gauff | **Ash Barty retires at #1 (2022)**; Swiatek wins 6 Grand Slams by age 24; Sabalenka dominates 2023–2025 |

## Rule changes that shaped the era

| Year | Change |
|------|--------|
| 1968 | Open Era begins; professionals admitted to all 4 Grand Slams |
| 1970 | Tiebreak introduced (12–12 at US Open); 6–6 elsewhere from 1971 |
| 1975 | Tiebreak adopted at Australian Open and French Open |
| 1977 | Wimbledon adopts tiebreak at 6–6 |
| 1990 | Lets on serves become standard |
| 2006 | Hawk-Eye challenge system introduced at US Open |
| 2018 | 25-second serve clock introduced at US Open |
| 2021 | Australian Open becomes first Grand Slam to use Hawk-Eye Live on all courts |
| 2022 | US Open replaces all line judges with Hawk-Eye Live |
| 2024 | ATP trials 3-second-after-point shot clock auto-start |
| 2025 | ATP makes shot-clock auto-start permanent; **Wimbledon ends 147-year line-judge tradition** |
