### ITF Tennis 10s (junior progression)

- Red ball (age 5–8) on 25% court with foam / low-compression ball
- Orange ball (age 8–10) on 50% court with low-compression ball
- Green ball (age 10–11) on full court with low-compression ball
- Yellow ball (age 11+) on full court with standard ITF-approved ball

### ITF Wheelchair tennis classification

| Dimension | Open Division | Quad Division |

## 7. tennisplayer.net (John Yandell)

**URL:** https://www.tennisplayer.net
**Editor:** John Yandell, author of *Visual Tennis*. Editorial board includes Dr. Brian Gordon (University of Western Australia) and Prof. Bruce Elliott (biomechanics, UWA).

**Key citable data points (Sampras-serve studies):**

- On a first serve at ~120 mph, Pete Sampras averaged **~2,700 rpm of spin** — up to twice the spin of Agassi/Chang at similar speeds. Ball makes 35–40 revolutions in ~0.75 s between serve and return.
- Second serves averaged 85 mph with **>4,600 rpm spin**; single fastest ball recorded **>5,300 rpm**, which makes 60–70 revolutions before reaching the returner.
- Modern pro shots routinely exceed 5,000 rpm; in one second of flight a ball can turn over 80+ times.
- **3D Sakurai/Reid/Elliott study** (7 elite male servers, 5 ATP-ranked, one Top 10): flat serves averaged 116 mph / ~1,200 rpm; slice 104 mph / ~2,200 rpm; kick 91 mph / ~3,200 rpm. **Total axis-of-rotation difference between serve types was less than 15°** — challenging the "purely flat / slice / kick" labels.

### Wikipedia tennis articles — key facts extracted

**Serve** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serve_(tennis)

- Four serve types — **flat, slice, kick (topspin/American twist/reverse kick), and underhand** — all legal in professional and amateur play.
- Underhand serve "was the standard serve method" in early tennis, "merely intended to start the game"; still used in children's tennis on 36 ft (11 m) courts.
- Record holders cited: **Roger Federer** — most aces in a Grand Slam final; **Andy Roddick** — fastest serve at a Grand Slam; **Serena Williams** — most aces at a Grand Slam.

**Grip** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grip_(tennis)

- **Continental grip (Bevel #2):** "The Shotgun" — well-suited to slice shots. Origin: European continent when grass courts produced lower bounces.
- **Eastern forehand (Bevel #3):** primarily for flatter groundstrokes.
- **Semi-Western (Bevel #4):** the modern forehand default.
- **Western forehand (Bevel #5):** extreme forehand grip. "Originally used by Rafael Nadal growing up."
- **3/4 Western variation** (popularized by Novak Djokovic): knuckle slightly on Semi-Western, heel pad more on the Western side.
- **Semi-Western backhand (Bevel #8):** index-finger base knuckle on bevel #8.
- **Two-handed backhand:** Continental bottom hand + left-handed Semi-Western Forehand top hand.
- "At professional levels, the continental grip is used to hit all serves."

**Tennis ball** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_ball

- ITF official diameter **6.54–6.86 cm (2.57–2.70 in)**.
- Mass **56.0–59.4 g (1.98–2.10 oz)**.
- Internal pressure "approximately two atmospheres" above sea-level ambient; contains ~12 psi (80 kPa; 0.8 atm) more nitrogen/oxygen than ambient air.
- Felt is a wool/nylon/cotton mix around a rubber edge.
- Bounce test: drop from 254 cm (100 in) onto concrete; acceptable bounce **135–147 cm (53–58 in)** at sea level, 20 °C (68 °F).
- Slow-ball color code: red → orange → green (matches ITF Tennis 10s progression).

**Grand Slam (tennis)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)

- **Calendar-year Grand Slam** = winning all four majors in one year. First achieved by **Don Budge in 1938**.
- The four majors: Australian Open (hard), French Open (clay), Wimbledon (grass), US Open (hard).
- The term "Grand Slam" was first applied by journalist John Kieran in 1933.
- **Career Grand Slam in both singles and doubles** achieved by only six players: one man — **Roy Emerson**; five women — **Margaret Court, Doris Hart, Shirley Fry Irvin, Martina Navratilova, Serena Williams**.
- **Career "boxed set"** (every Grand Slam title — singles, doubles, mixed) achieved by only three players: Margaret Court, Doris Hart, Martina Navratilova.
- **Golden Slam** = all four majors + Olympic gold; "Calendar-year Golden Slam" if in one year.

**Tennis court** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_court

- Court **78 ft (23.77 m) long**.
- Width **27 ft (8.23 m) singles**, **36 ft (10.97 m) doubles**.
- Service line **21 ft (6.40 m) from the net**.
- Recommended clear overrun space: **60 ft (18 m) wide × 120 ft (37 m) long**.
- Net **3 ft 6 in (1.07 m) high at the posts, 3 ft (0.914 m) high in the center**.

## 8. Rod Cross — Tennis Physics (Tennis Warehouse University Learning Center)

**Principal TWU articles by Rod Cross (URL → topic):**

- https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/doublependulum.php → "The Double Pendulum in Tennis" — foundational biomechanics model (Am. J. Phys. 79(5) 470–476, 2011).
- https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/videocuckoo.php → "Tennis Physicist — Cuckoo Or Genius?" — profile of Cross as TWU contributor; retired University of Sydney physics professor.
- https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/racquetweighting.php → "Tennis Racquet Customization" (Crawford Lindsey, with extensive Cross references) — double-pendulum experiments.
- https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/spinandlubev2.php → "String Lubrication and Movement in Spin" — experiments at 420 fps, balls fired at 49±1 mph / −3150±286 rpm / 28° from perpendicular.
- https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/padel/padelspin.php → "The Physics of Padel Spin Production" — applies Cross's grip/slide transition model (transition at ~45° from racket surface).

**External Cross papers of record:**

- Cross, R. (2011). "A double pendulum model of tennis strokes." Am. J. Phys. 79(5): 470–476.
- Cross, R. (2005). "A double pendulum swing experiment: In search of a better bat." Am. J. Phys. 73: 330–339.
- Cross, R. (2004). "Physics of overarm throwing." Am. J. Phys. 72: 305–312.
- Cross, R. "Measurement of the speed and bounce of tennis courts." Court-speed classification: COF >0.71 = high (clay), <0.55 = low (grass); COR >0.85 high, <0.78 low; surfaces with COR <0.70 are not recommended by the ITF.
- Cross, R. home page: https://physics.usyd.edu.au/~cross

**Key citable findings:**

- "Rod Cross has pioneered the [double pendulum] concept in tennis and baseball." (Lindsey, TWU)
- "Cross has shown that more force is automatically generated by swinging or throwing a more massive object, so all we can really do is control the energy that is applied to the swing."
- On racquet weighting: at very low swingweights (<200–250 kg·cm² RDC) you can get more ball speed for the same effort ("free lunch"), but at typical playing swingweights you get out what you put in.
- Each racket has a slide-grip transition angle; below this angle the ball slides throughout impact, above it the ball grips and achieves maximum spin.
- Co-authored book: Brody, H., Cross, R., Lindsey, C. (2002). *The Physics and Technology of Tennis.* Racquet Tech Publishing.

## 9. USPTA (United States Professional Tennis Association)

**URL hub:** https://uspta.com
**Authoritative role:** Largest association of tennis-teaching professionals in the world; publishes *ADDvantage* magazine and *USPTA Magazine*.

**ADDvantage / USPTA — "Exploring the Serve-and-Volley Paradigm Shift"**
*Author:* James Shaughnessy, MSS, CSCS, USPTA Master Professional

- "In the 1960s, four of the world's five top-ranked players — Rod Laver, Roy Emerson, John Newcombe and Arthur Ashe — used the serve-and-volley style (SVS) of play."
- Sampras serves from a platform stance with feet 26 in apart; his left foot lands 34 in inside the baseline 0.167 s after contact, requiring him to cover 15 ft 2 in to the service line in 1.33 s.
- Federer: feet 25 in apart, left foot lands 7.51 in inside the baseline at 0.229 s, covering 17.38 ft in 1.18 s.
- Shaughnessy distinguishes **horizontal** vs **vertical** serve techniques: horizontal servers (Sampras) have CoG at its highest at contact; vertical servers (Roddick, Federer) push downward, are airborne longer.
