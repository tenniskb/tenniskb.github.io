# ART-454: Attacking Backhand Drive

## Executive Summary
The attacking backhand drive is an offensive weapon used to finish points from the baseline. It combines power, spin, and precision to put the opponent on the defensive. Mastering the attacking drive requires excellent timing, body mechanics, and shot selection.

## When to Attack with the Backhand
The attacking backhand drive is appropriate when the player receives a short ball, a slow ball, or a ball that sits up in the strike zone. It is also effective when the player is inside the baseline and wants to take time away from the opponent.

The attacking drive should be used selectively, as it provides less margin than a standard drive. Players should choose moments when the risk is justified by the potential reward.

## Technique for the Attacking Drive
The attacking backhand drive uses the same mechanics as a standard drive, with a few adjustments. The player should move forward to meet the ball, taking it early to take time away from the opponent. The swing should be more aggressive, with a faster racket head speed and a more pronounced follow-through.

The contact point is typically further forward than for a standard drive, allowing the player to hit down on the ball and produce a penetrating shot. The body should rotate fully, generating maximum power.

## Shot Selection for the Attack
The attacking drive can be hit crosscourt or down the line, depending on the situation. Crosscourt is the safer option, as it provides a larger margin over the net and into the court. Down the line is riskier but can be more effective, as it changes the direction of the rally and catches the opponent by surprise.

Players should also consider the opponent's position. If the opponent is on the opposite side of the court, a crosscourt drive to the open court is effective. If the opponent is in the center, a down-the-line drive can catch them by surprise.

## The Inside-Out Backhand
The inside-out backhand is a specific attacking shot where the player runs around the backhand to hit a forehand. This shot is effective because it allows the player to use their stronger forehand to attack the opponent's backhand.

The inside-out backhand requires excellent footwork and timing. The player must run around the backhand, set up for the forehand, and hit the shot before the opponent can recover. This shot is risky but can be a powerful weapon when executed correctly.

## Common Attacking Drive Errors
One common error is attacking from too far back, resulting in a weak, defensive shot. Another is failing to move forward, resulting in a contact point that is too far back. Some players also attack too frequently, resulting in unforced errors.

Players who struggle with the attacking drive often benefit from drills that emphasize footwork and timing. Practicing the approach to the ball and the aggressive swing helps develop the technique needed for an effective attacking drive.

## Drills for the Attacking Drive
Several drills help develop the attacking drive. The "short ball drill" involves having a partner feed short balls, practicing moving forward and hitting an aggressive drive. The "attack drill" involves rallying with a partner, focusing on attacking any ball that comes within 3 meters of the baseline. The "inside-out drill" involves practicing running around the backhand to hit a forehand, developing the footwork needed for this shot.

Players should incorporate these drills into their regular practice routine, focusing on developing the technique needed for an effective attacking drive.

## Practical Application: "The Attack Drill"
Have a partner feed balls to your backhand, varying the depth. Practice attacking any ball that comes within 3 meters of the baseline, focusing on moving forward and hitting an aggressive drive. Count how many points you win with the attacking drive. Track progress over sessions to ensure the attacking drive is developing correctly.
