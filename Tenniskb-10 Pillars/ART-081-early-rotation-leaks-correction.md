# ART:081: Early Rotation Leaks and Their Correction

## Executive Summary
Early rotation leaks occur when the hips or trunk begin rotating before the loading phase is complete, dissipating the elastic energy that should be stored for the forward swing. This is one of the most common and most damaging timing faults in tennis, reducing power output by 20-40%. Understanding and correcting early rotation leaks is essential for maximizing stroke power.

## The Loading Phase Purpose

### Energy Storage
The loading phase stores elastic energy in:
- Lower-extremity muscles and tendons (knee flexors, calf muscles)
- Trunk rotator muscles and fascia (obliques, thoracolumbar fascia)
- Hip rotator muscles (gluteals, deep external rotators)

### The Critical Window
The loading phase must be completed before rotation begins:
- **Loading**: Lower extremities flex, trunk rotates away from target
- **Rotation**: Hips and trunk rotate toward target
- **Error**: Rotation begins before loading is complete

## Early Rotation Leaks

### Early Hip Rotation
- **Observation**: Hips begin rotating before leg drive is complete
- **Cause**: Rushing the stroke, poor leg-hip coordination
- **Result**: Ground reaction forces not fully utilized; elastic energy dissipated
- **Correction**: Pause-at-leg-drive drills; sequential timing exercises

### Early Trunk Rotation
- **Observation**: Trunk rotates before hips have completed their rotation
- **Cause**: Poor coordination, weak hip rotators, arm-dominant tendency
- **Result**: Hip-trunk separation not fully released; reduced elastic energy
- **Correction**: Hip-first rotation drills; core stability training

## Practical Application: "The Load-Before-Turn Drill"
Practice loading completely before initiating rotation. Stand in your ready position and perform a slow-motion groundstroke: first, load deeply (bend your knees, rotate your trunk back), feeling the stretch in your legs and trunk. Hold this loaded position for one full second. Now rotate: first your hips, then your trunk, then your shoulder, then your arm. This drill teaches your nervous system to complete the loading phase before beginning rotation.
