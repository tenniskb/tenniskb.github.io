# ART-320: The Power Position — Optimal Athletic Stance Before the Forward Swing

## Executive Summary

The power position is the athletic stance at the completion of the unit turn, from which the forward swing is launched. It is characterized by deep knee flexion, hip-shoulder separation, lateral trunk flexion, and the racquet positioned behind the body in the optimal launch orientation. The quality of the power position directly determines the potential energy available for the forward swing: a well-constructed power position contains all the elastic energy and ground force potential needed for a high-quality forehand, while a poorly constructed position limits the ceiling of shot quality regardless of the forward swing's execution.

## The Five Characteristics of the Power Position

The power position can be assessed through five key characteristics:

1. Knee flexion: Both knees are flexed to 110 to 135 degrees, with the rear knee typically slightly deeper than the front knee in closed and neutral stances. This flexion stores elastic energy in the quadriceps and positions the legs for explosive extension.

2. Hip-shoulder separation: The shoulders have rotated 90 to 120 degrees while the hips have rotated 45 to 60 degrees, creating 40 to 60 degrees of separation. This separation stores elastic energy in the trunk musculature.

3. Lateral trunk flexion: The trunk is flexed 15 to 25 degrees laterally over the rear hip, loading the contralateral oblique muscles and creating the C-curve spine alignment.

4. Racquet orientation: The racquet is positioned behind the body, with the face perpendicular to the target plane and the head above the hands (for a loop backswing) or at the same height as the hands (for a straight backswing). The wrist is cocked back 45 to 60 degrees, creating the lag angle.

5. Weight distribution: Weight is loaded on the rear foot (60-70% for closed/neutral stances, 50-60% for open stances), with the rear heel slightly off the ground and the rear foot angled to push into the court.

## The Stability-Mobility Balance

The power position must balance stability (the ability to generate ground force against a stable base) with mobility (the ability to rotate and accelerate freely). Too much stability (wide stance, deep flexion, locked joints) restricts rotation and reduces racquet head speed. Too much mobility (narrow stance, shallow flexion, loose joints) compromises ground force production and directional control.

The optimal balance is a stance that is wide enough to provide a stable base (feet at least shoulder-width apart) but narrow enough to allow full hip rotation, with knee flexion deep enough to store elastic energy but not so deep that extension is slow.

## Transitioning from Power Position to Forward Swing

The transition from the power position to the forward swing is not a deliberate "launch" but a natural release of the stored elastic energy. As the front hip begins rotating toward the target (triggered by the front leg driving into the ground), the hip-shoulder separation is reduced, the lateral flexion is released, and the racquet begins its forward acceleration.

This transition should feel effortless and explosive, like releasing a compressed spring. If the player must consciously "muscle" the forward swing, the power position was not optimally constructed (insufficient loading) or the transition is not properly sequenced (hip lead missing).

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shallow knee flexion at power position | Insufficient elastic energy storage | Low ground force; weak shots | Deepen flexion to 110-135 degrees in both knees |
| No hip-shoulder separation | No torso coil; no elastic energy | Arm-dominated swing | Restrict hip rotation; maximize shoulder rotation |
| Racquet face not perpendicular to target | Face angle errors at contact | Directional inconsistency | Check racquet orientation at power position |
| Wrist not cocked (no lag) | Missing wrist energy storage | Reduced racquet speed | Cock wrist 45-60 degrees at power position |
| Weight on front foot | No platform for rear-leg drive | Flat, weak shots | Shift weight to rear foot before forward swing |

## Practical Application: "The Power Position Hold"

Hold the power position for 3-5 seconds before each forward swing, consciously checking all five characteristics. Feel the elastic tension in your legs, trunk, and wrist. This deliberate hold ingrains the correct position and develops the proprioceptive awareness needed to self-assess the power position during normal-speed strokes. Over time, the hold duration decreases as the correct position becomes automatic.
