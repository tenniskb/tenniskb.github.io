# ART-468: Handling High Bouncing Backhand

## Executive Summary
High bouncing balls are a common challenge on the backhand side, particularly on clay courts and against opponents who hit with heavy topspin. Handling these balls requires proper positioning, body mechanics, and shot selection. Mastering this skill allows the player to neutralize the opponent's advantage and stay in the point.

## The Challenge of High Bouncing Balls
High bouncing balls are difficult to handle because they rise above the strike zone, forcing the player to hit up on the ball. This is particularly challenging on the backhand side, where the player has less leverage and control than on the forehand.

Players who struggle with high bouncing balls often hit weak, defensive shots that sit up for the opponent. Developing the ability to handle high balls is essential for success on clay courts and against heavy topspin opponents.

## Positioning for High Balls
The key to handling high bouncing balls is positioning. The player should move back to allow the ball to drop into the strike zone, rather than trying to hit it at the peak of its bounce. This requires quick adjustment steps and a commitment to moving backward.

Players who fail to move back often find themselves hitting the ball at shoulder height or above, resulting in weak, defensive shots. Developing the habit of moving back for high balls is essential for effective play.

## Technique for High Balls
The technique for high balls depends on the ball's height and the player's position. If the ball is moderately high, the player can hit a standard topspin drive, using a low-to-high swing path and a slightly closed racket face. If the ball is very high, the player may need to hit a slice or a defensive shot.

The player should bend the knees and get low, allowing the racket to swing up through the ball. The contact point should be in front of the body, but the player should be prepared to hit the ball at a higher contact point than usual.

## Body Mechanics for High Balls
The body mechanics for high balls require getting low and using the legs to drive upward. The player should bend the knees, lowering the center of gravity, and then extend the legs through the swing, adding lift to the shot.

The shoulders should rotate fully, generating maximum power. The non-dominant arm should be used for balance, extending backward during the take-back and moving across the body during the forward swing.

## Shot Selection for High Balls
The shot selection for high balls depends on the tactical situation. If the player is inside the baseline, an aggressive topspin drive may be appropriate. If the player is behind the baseline, a defensive slice or a high topspin drive may be more appropriate.

Players should also consider the opponent's position. If the opponent is at the net, a lob may be necessary to buy time. If the opponent is behind the baseline, a topspin drive to the open court may be effective.

## Common High Ball Errors
One common error is failing to move back, resulting in hitting the ball at the peak of its bounce. Another is using a flat swing path, resulting in a ball that sails long. Some players also fail to get low, resulting in a loss of power and control.

Players who struggle with high balls often benefit from drills that emphasize positioning and body mechanics. Practicing high balls with a focus on moving back and getting low helps develop the technique needed for effective play.

## Drills for High Balls
Several drills help develop the ability to handle high balls. The "high ball drill" involves having a partner feed high bouncing balls, practicing moving back and hitting a variety of shots. The "clay court drill" involves practicing on clay, where the ball bounces higher, developing the positioning and technique needed for high balls. The "topspin drill" involves practicing against heavy topspin, developing the ability to handle the high, heavy bounce.

Players should incorporate these drills into their regular practice routine, focusing on developing the technique needed for effective play against high bouncing balls.

## Practical Application: "The High Ball Practice"
Have a partner feed high bouncing balls to your backhand side. Practice moving back, getting low, and hitting a variety of shots based on the ball's height and position. Track how many shots you can hit in the court and how many produce a good result. This drill develops the positioning and technique needed for effective play against high bouncing balls.
