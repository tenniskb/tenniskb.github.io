# ART:098: Biomechanical Video Analysis for Tennis Stroke Optimization

## Executive Summary
Biomechanical video analysis is a powerful tool for identifying technical faults and optimizing stroke mechanics in tennis. By recording and analyzing strokes in slow motion, players and coaches can identify specific biomechanical errors—such as timing faults, insufficient rotation, or energy leaks—that are invisible at full speed. This article presents a practical framework for using video analysis to improve tennis stroke mechanics.

## Video Analysis Framework

### Recording
- Use a high-speed camera (120+ fps) for detailed analysis
- Record from multiple angles: front, side, rear
- Capture the full movement: preparation, loading, forward swing, contact, follow-through

### Analysis
- Review footage in slow motion (10-25% speed)
- Identify key positions: maximum loading, hip initiation, trunk initiation, shoulder initiation, contact
- Compare to elite player models
- Identify timing errors, range-of-motion limitations, and energy leaks

### Intervention
- Design targeted drills to address identified faults
- Use video feedback to reinforce correct mechanics
- Re-record to assess improvement

## Key Variables to Analyze

### Loading Phase
- Knee flexion depth
- Hip-shoulder separation angle
- Racket position

### Forward Swing
- Sequential timing of segmental activation
- Hip rotation velocity
- Trunk rotation velocity
- Shoulder rotation velocity
- Arm acceleration

### Contact
- Contact point height and distance from body
- Racket face angle
- Wrist stability
- Body balance

### Follow-Through
- Deceleration pattern
- Completion of rotation
- Recovery position

## Practical Application: "The Video Check"
Record your strokes monthly and analyze them using the framework above. Compare your mechanics to elite player models and identify specific areas for improvement. Even small technical corrections—identified through video analysis—can lead to significant improvements in power, consistency, and injury prevention.
