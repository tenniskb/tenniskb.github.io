# ART:061: Flat Hit Mechanics and Drive Stroke Production

## Executive Summary
Flat hits are generated by a linear (level) racket path through the ball with a square (perpendicular) face angle. This produces maximum ball speed with minimal spin, making flat shots the fastest but lowest-margin shots in tennis. Flat drives are used for winners, approach shots, and when time is limited. Understanding flat hit mechanics enables players to generate maximum pace when needed while recognizing the trade-off with margin and control.

## Racket Path for Flat Hits

### Linear Trajectory
- **Starting position**: Racket at approximately the same height as the ball
- **Ending position**: Racket at approximately the same height as the ball
- **Minimal vertical component**: The racket moves horizontally through the ball

### Face Angle
- **Square face**: The face is perpendicular to the direction of travel at contact
- **Minimal face rotation**: No brushing action, just direct drive through the ball

## Practical Application: "The Drive Through"
To hit a flat drive, focus on driving straight through the ball with a level racket path and square face. Imagine hitting through the ball in a straight line from take-back to follow-through. The flatter your racket path, the flatter and faster your shot. Use flat drives for winners and approach shots when you need maximum pace, but recognize that the margin over the net is lower than with spin shots.
