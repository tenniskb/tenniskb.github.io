# ART-0628: The Scissors Kick Overhead — Advanced Smash Technique

## Executive Summary

The scissors kick overhead is an advanced smash technique used when a deep lob forces the net player to jump backward to reach the ball at its peak. It involves a scissor-like leg movement — one leg drives upward while the other scissors back — that generates power from a fully airborne position. The scissors kick overhead is a spectacular but difficult shot that requires excellent coordination, timing, and confidence. It is the ultimate expression of the overhead smash.

## When to Use the Scissors Kick Overhead

The scissors kick overhead is used when: (1) the lob is so deep that you cannot reach it with a stationary overhead, (2) you are forced to move backward quickly, (3) the ball's peak is above your standing reach, and (4) you have the athleticism and confidence to execute an airborne shot. It is a last-resort shot — if you can reach the lob with a stationary overhead, use the simpler technique. The scissors kick is most commonly seen on deep lobs that land within 5 feet of the baseline.

## Technical Execution

The scissors kick overhead begins with a drop-step as you recognize the lob. Move backward with crossover steps to get behind the ball's trajectory. As you approach the contact point, push off the back foot (right foot for right-handed players) and drive the left knee upward. The body rotates in the air — the right leg scissors back as the left leg drives up. Contact occurs at the peak of the jump, with the racket extending above the head. Land on the left foot (for right-handed players) with knees bent to absorb the landing.

## The Kinetic Chain in the Air

The scissors kick overhead uses a modified kinetic chain: (1) the push-off leg (right leg) drives upward from the ground, (2) the torso rotates in the air, (3) the shoulder drives the arm forward, (4) the elbow leads the forearm, and (5) the wrist snaps through contact. Because the ground connection is lost during the jump, power comes from the push-off leg and the rotation of the body in the air. The jump height determines the contact height — higher jump = higher contact = more downward angle on the smash.

## Tracking the Ball While Airborne

Tracking the ball during a scissors kick overhead is the most difficult aspect. The non-dominant arm points up at the ball throughout the movement — this helps maintain visual contact while the body rotates. The head must remain still and focused on the ball. Do not look at the target until after contact — looking up early causes the body to lose rotation and the contact to be inconsistent.

## Common Scissors Kick Errors

Jumping too early (reaching the peak before the ball arrives). Jumping too late (ball passes before you can contact). Poor push-off (insufficient jump height). Losing sight of the ball during rotation (inconsistent contact). Landing poorly (knee injury risk). Over-hitting (trying for too much power from an unstable platform). Not committing fully (hesitation in the air).

## Tactical Application

The scissors kick overhead is primarily a defensive recovery shot — you are saving a point that would otherwise be lost. The goal is not necessarily a winner but a deep, well-placed smash that resets the point. Aim for the open court or the opponent's weaker side. If you cannot generate enough power for a winner, aim for depth and height to buy time to recover. In doubles, the scissors kick overhead is less common because partners can split lob coverage.

## The Safety Considerations

The scissors kick overhead carries injury risk: (1) landing on one foot with poor knee alignment can stress the knee, (2) rotating in the air can cause ankle sprains on uneven landings, (3) colliding with the net or fence if you jump too far backward. Practice the scissors kick on a forgiving surface (grass or hard court, not clay) and with proper footwear. Strengthen the legs and core to support the demands of the jump.

## Developing the Scissors Kick

Start by practicing the jump without a ball — push off one foot, drive the opposite knee up, rotate in the air, and land on the pushing-off foot. Once the movement is comfortable, add a coach tossing balls that you hit with the scissors kick. Gradually increase the difficulty — deeper lobs, faster movement, more pressure. The scissors kick overhead is a shot that develops over years, not weeks.

## Summary

The scissors kick overhead is the most advanced overhead technique, requiring a full jump and aerial rotation to reach deep lobs. It is a spectacular but difficult shot that should be used as a last resort when simpler overheads are not possible. Practice the movement without a ball first, then add progressively more challenging tosses. When executed correctly, the scissors kick overhead is one of tennis's most exciting shots.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Scissors kick misses | Poor ball tracking, inconsistent contact | Errors, lost points | Point non-dominant arm at ball, keep head still |
| Insufficient jump height | Weak push-off, poor timing | Cannot reach ball | Strengthen legs, practice timing of push-off |

| Lands awkwardly | Poor landing technique | Knee/ankle injury risk | Practice landing on one foot with bent knee |

## Practical Application: "Jump to the Ball"

The scissors kick overhead is about commitment. You must jump with full force, trusting that your body will rotate and your racket will find the ball. Hesitation in the air is fatal — once you leave the ground, commit fully to the shot.
