# ART-561: Return Against Big Serving: Strategies for Facing Power
## Executive Summary

Big servers present one of the toughest challenges in tennis. When facing opponents who serve at 120+ mph, standard returning techniques are often insufficient. This article provides specialized strategies for neutralizing power servers and competing effectively against them.

## Understanding the Big Server's Advantage

### The Physics of Big Serving
- A 130 mph serve reaches the returner in approximately 0.45 seconds
- The returner must react, move, and swing in under half a second
- At these speeds, anticipation becomes more important than reaction

### The Psychological Factor
- Big servers intimidate returners
- Fear of the big serve leads to tentative returning
- Intimidation creates a self-fulfilling prophecy

## Positioning Against Big Servers

### Stand Deep
- Start 8-12 feet behind the baseline
- This gives you more time to react
- Accept that you will hit more defensive returns
- Focus on getting the ball back in play

### Stand Closer to the Center
- Big servers often serve wide to pull you off court
- Standing closer to the center reduces the angle you must cover
- You may concede some width but gain time to react

### Adjust Based on the Server
- If the server serves predominantly wide, cheat slightly toward the sideline
- If the server serves predominantly to your backhand, cheat toward the backhand
- If the server serves predominantly body, stay in the center

## Technique Adjustments

### The Block Return
- Use a minimal backswing
- Let the racket act as a wall to deflect the ball
- Focus on getting the ball back in play
- The server's pace provides the power

### The Chip Return
- Use a short, chopping motion
- Chip the ball back with backspin
- The backspin keeps the ball low and short
- Forces the server to hit up on the ball

### The Half-Volley Return
- Meet the ball early, before it reaches you
- Use a half-volley to redirect the ball
- Reduces the time available for the serve to travel
- Requires excellent hand-eye coordination

## Tactical Strategies

### Accept That You Will Lose Some Points
- Big servers will hit aces — accept this
- Do not try to return every serve
- Focus on the serves you can reach

### Target the Server's Weaker Side
- Direct returns to the server's weaker stroke
- This neutralizes their ability to attack after serving
- Forces them to hit from their weaker side

### Extend the Rally
- Do not try to hit winners off big serves
- Focus on getting the ball back in play
- Extend the rally and wait for a better opportunity
- The longer the rally, the more the advantage shifts to you

### Return Deep
- Aim for the back third of the court
- Deep returns prevent the server from attacking
- Keep the server back and force them to hit from behind the baseline

## Returning Second Serves Against Big Servers

### Attack Every Second Serve
- Big servers often hit weaker second serves
- Move inside the baseline to return second serves
- Attack with a full swing
- Target the server's weaker side

### The Second Serve Return as Your Opportunity
- The second serve is your chance to take control of the point
- Be aggressive on every second serve you can reach
- Do not let the server off the hook with passive returns

## Mental Strategies

### Do Not Be Intimidated
- The big serve is just a shot — you can return it
- Focus on the process, not the server's reputation
- Remember that even the best servers miss serves

### Stay Positive
- Celebrate every successful return
- Do not dwell on aces or missed returns
- Focus on the next point

### Play the Percentages
- Accept that you will lose some service games
- Focus on breaking when the server is vulnerable
- One break may be enough to win the set

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Intimidated by big servers | Fear of the serve | Tentative returning | Mental training; exposure to big serves |
| Always late on big serves | Poor positioning | Mishits and errors | Move deeper; practice deep returning |
| Trying to hit winners off big serves | Over-aggression | Unforced errors | Focus on consistency; extend the rally |
| Cannot handle second serves | Not attacking | Missed break opportunities | Second serve attack drills; move inside baseline |
| Returns too short | Passive returning | Server attacks | Return deep; practice depth control |

## Practical Application: "The Deep Return Drill"

Stand 10 feet behind the baseline. Have a partner serve at match speed. Focus only on blocking the ball back deep crosscourt. Do not worry about winners — just get the ball back in play and deep. Track your success rate. Aim for 60%+ of returns landing in the court. This drill develops the skills needed for returning against big servers.
