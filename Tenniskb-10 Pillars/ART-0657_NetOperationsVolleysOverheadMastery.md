# ART-657: The Volley Against Big Servers — Surviving Power at the Net

## Executive Summary

When you are the net player in doubles facing a big server, you must handle returns of serve hit at extreme pace. Big servers (120+ mph) force returners to block or swipe at the ball, producing returns that arrive at the net player with tremendous speed and little warning. Surviving as the net player against big servers requires specialized techniques: the block volley, body volley, and the ability to redirect pace without a swing.

## The Challenge of the Big Serve Return

A big serve return: (1) arrives at the net player in under 300 milliseconds, (2) often has unpredictable direction (the returner has little control), (3) may be hit at the net player's body (a deliberate tactic), (4) is often hit with slice or flat pace (no time for topspin). The net player must react instinctively — there is no time for conscious technique.

## Positioning Against Big Servers

When your partner is serving against big hitters: (1) position slightly deeper at the net (6-9 feet) to gain reaction time, (2) hold the racket in front of your body at chest height (the "racket in front" position), (3) keep both hands on the racket for stability, (4) stay on the balls of your feet for quick movement. The "racket in front" position minimizes the distance the racket must travel to meet the return.

## The Block Volley Against Big Returns

Use the block volley for most big serve returns: (1) present a stable racket face in the ball's path, (2) absorb pace with a firm wrist, (3) redirect the ball by angling the face rather than swinging, (4) aim for the open court or the opponent's feet. The block volley is your primary weapon — do not try to hit winners on big serve returns. Focus on keeping the ball in play and redirecting pace.

## The Body Volley Against Big Returns

When the return comes directly at your body: (1) hold the racket in front of your chest, (2) step slightly to the side to create space (if time allows), (3) block the ball with a firm wrist, (4) redirect cross-court (the safest option). Do not try to do too much with a body volley — the goal is survival, not winners.

## Common Errors Against Big Servers

Swinging at the ball (no time for a swing). Holding the racket too low (cannot react in time). Ducking or turning away (natural reflex but results in misses). Trying for winners (over-aggression). Not communicating with your partner about coverage. These errors lead to easy points for the returning team.

## Tactical Application

Against big servers: (1) focus on survival — keep the ball in play, (2) redirect pace rather than adding your own, (3) aim for the open court or the opponent's feet, (4) communicate with your partner about who takes which shots, (5) accept that some returns will get through — do not get frustrated. The key is consistency over aggression.

## Summary

Facing big serve returns at the net requires the block volley, body volley, and the ability to redirect pace without a swing. Position deeper, hold the racket in front, and focus on survival over winners. With practice, you will become a reliable net player against even the biggest servers.

## Practical Application: "Block and Redirect"

Against big serve returns: (1) block the ball with a stable racket face, (2) redirect it to the open court, (3) do not try to hit winners. Survival is the goal — keep the ball in play and trust your partner to finish the point.
