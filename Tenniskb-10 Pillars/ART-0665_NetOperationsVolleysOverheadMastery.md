# ART-0665: The Volley Against the Inside-Out Forehand — Handling the Modern Power Shot

## Executive Summary

The inside-out forehand is the most common power shot in modern tennis — a forehand hit from the backhand corner that curves cross-court to the opponent's backhand. When you are at the net, the inside-out forehand is a primary passing shot threat because it is hit with heavy topspin and pace from a position that opens up the court. Handling the inside-out forehand at the net requires anticipation, positioning, and the ability to handle heavy topspin.

## The Inside-Out Forehand

The inside-out forehand: (1) is hit from the backhand corner (the opponent runs around their backhand), (2) curves cross-court with heavy topspin, (3) lands deep in the forehand corner, (4) is hit with maximum pace and spin. The inside-out forehand is the most dangerous passing shot against a net player because it combines pace, spin, and angle.

## The Challenge for Net Players

The inside-out forehand challenges net players because: (1) the heavy topspin causes the ball to kick high and wide, (2) the pace makes block volleys difficult, (3) the angle pulls the net player off the court, (4) the shot is hit from a position that opens up the court. The net player must adjust their positioning and technique to handle this powerful shot.

## Positioning Against the Inside-Out Forehand

Against the inside-out forehand: (1) position slightly to the cross-court side of the bisecting line (the inside-out forehand goes cross-court), (2) be prepared for the ball to kick wide (heavy topspin causes lateral movement), (3) use a firmer grip (6-7/10) to handle the heavy topspin, (4) be ready to use the block volley (the pace is too high for a punch volley). The key is to anticipate the cross-court direction and position accordingly.

## Volley Technique Against the Inside-Out Forehand

Against the inside-out forehand: (1) use the block volley (absorb the heavy pace), (2) close the racket face slightly (the heavy topspin causes the ball to jump), (3) aim for depth (the opponent is likely out of position after running around their backhand), (4) or redirect down-the-line (the opponent is out of position on the cross-court side). The key is to handle the heavy topspin and redirect the ball effectively.

## Tactical Application Against the Inside-Out Forehand

Against opponents who hit inside-out forehands: (1) approach to their backhand (the inside-out forehand is harder to hit from the backhand corner), (2) position slightly to the cross-court side, (3) use the block volley as your primary weapon, (4) be prepared for the ball to kick wide, (5) redirect down-the-line when the opponent is out of position. The key is to take away the inside-out forehand by approaching to the backhand.

## Common Errors Against the Inside-Out Forehand

Positioning too far down-the-line (the inside-out forehand goes cross-court). Using punch volleys instead of block volleys (the pace is too high). Not closing the racket face enough (the heavy topspin causes the ball to go long). Not anticipating the wide kick (the ball pulls you off the court).

## Summary

Handling the inside-out forehand at the net requires anticipation, positioning to the cross-court side, and the block volley. The heavy topspin and pace of the inside-out forehand make it one of the most dangerous passing shots, but with the right adjustments, you can neutralize it effectively.

## Practical Application: "Block, Close, Redirect"

Against the inside-out forehand: (1) block the ball with a stable racket face, (2) close the face slightly to handle the topspin, (3) redirect down-the-line or deep cross-court. The inside-out forehand is powerful, but with the right technique, you can neutralize it.
