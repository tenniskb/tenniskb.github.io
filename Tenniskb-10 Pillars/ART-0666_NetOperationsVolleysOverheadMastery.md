# ART-0666: The Volley Against the Inside-In Forehand — Down-the-Line Defense

## Executive Summary

The inside-in forehand is a forehand hit from the backhand corner that goes down-the-line instead of cross-court. It is less common than the inside-out forehand but equally dangerous because it targets the net player's forehand side with pace and angle. Handling the inside-in forehand requires quick lateral movement to the forehand side and a compact punch volley.

## The Inside-In Forehand

The inside-in forehand: (1) is hit from the backhand corner, (2) goes down-the-line to the opponent's forehand side, (3) is hit with pace and often slice or flat spin. The inside-in is used to surprise the net player who is positioned for the cross-court shot. It is particularly effective against net players who cheat to the cross-court side.

## Positioning Against the Inside-In

Against the inside-in: (1) do not cheat too far to the cross-court side — maintain a balanced position, (2) use the split step to prepare for movement in either direction, (3) read the opponent's racket face at contact (a closed face indicates down-the-line), (4) use the cross-step to move to the forehand side. The key is to stay balanced and react to the actual shot direction rather than anticipating.

## Volley Technique Against the Inside-In

Against the inside-in: (1) use the punch volley (the ball arrives with pace but not extreme pace), (2) contact the ball in front of the body, (3) redirect cross-court or down-the-line, (4) use a firm wrist to handle the pace. The key is quick lateral movement followed by a compact, decisive volley.

## Common Errors

Cheating too far cross-court (the inside-in exploits this). Not split stepping (slow reaction). Using a long backswing (late contact). Not reading the opponent's racket face (late recognition).

## Summary

The inside-in forehand is a surprise shot that targets the net player's forehand side. Stay balanced, read the opponent's racket face, and use the punch volley to handle the shot effectively.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cheats too far cross-court | Anticipating inside-out | Wide inside-in passing shot | Stay balanced, react to actual shot |
| Slow reaction to inside-in | No split step | Late volley | Split step before every shot |
| Long backswing on inside-in | No compact technique | Late contact | Shorten backswing, punch volley |
| Cannot read inside-in | Not watching opponent's racket | Late recognition | Focus on opponent's contact |

## Practical Application: "Stay Balanced, React Quick"

Against the inside-in: (1) do not over-anticipate cross-court, (2) stay balanced in the center, (3) react to the actual shot direction with quick lateral movement and a compact volley.
