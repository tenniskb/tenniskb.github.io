# ART-0696: The Volley Against the Return with Extreme Width — Handling Sideline Returns

## Executive Summary

Returns hit to the extreme sideline force the net player to stretch wide for a volley that is difficult to control and recover from. When you are the net player facing a sideline return, you must use the cross-step for maximum reach, contact the ball in front of your body, and use the block volley to handle the pace. Sideline returns are a standard tactic to stretch the net player and open the court for the next shot.

## The Sideline Return

The sideline return: (1) is hit to the extreme sideline, (2) forces the net player to stretch wide, (3) is hit with slice (to stay low) or topspin (to kick wide). At the net, the sideline return is one of the most challenging volley situations because the extreme lateral distance reduces control and recovery time.

## Technique for Handling Sideline Returns

Against sideline returns: (1) use the cross-step to move laterally, (2) contact the ball in front of your body, (3) use the block volley (absorb pace, redirect), (4) aim deep to the open court, (5) recover to the center with the crossover step. The key is quick lateral movement and a compact block volley.

## Common Errors

Not using the cross-step (the shuffle step cannot cover enough ground). Reaching instead of moving the feet. Breaking the wrist on the wide volley. Not recovering to the center after the shot. Trying for a winner from a wide position (errors).

## Summary

Sideline returns demand the cross-step, block volley, and quick recovery. Move laterally, block the ball, and recover to the center.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cannot reach sideline returns | Using shuffle instead of cross-step | Missed volleys | Drill cross-step for lateral movement |
| Wide volley lacks control | Reaching instead of moving feet | Erratic direction | Move feet first, then contact |
| No recovery after wide volley | Standing and watching | Court left open | Practice crossover recovery steps |
| Tries for winner from wide position | Over-aggression | Errors | Focus on control, not winners |

## Practical Application: "Cross-Step, Block, Recover"

Against sideline returns: (1) cross-step to reach the ball, (2) block the volley, (3) crossover step to recover. Quick lateral movement and recovery are the keys.
