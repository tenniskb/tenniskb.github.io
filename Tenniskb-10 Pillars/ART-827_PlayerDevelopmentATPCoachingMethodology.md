# ART-827: Biomechanics of Tennis Strokes

## Executive Summary

Tennis biomechanics applies the principles of physics and human movement science to understand and optimize stroke production. The kinetic chain—the sequential activation of body segments from the ground up—is the foundation of all tennis strokes, with energy generated by the legs and trunk and transferred through the arm to the racket. Understanding biomechanics allows coaches to optimize power production, improve efficiency, and reduce injury risk by identifying mechanical flaws that increase joint loading beyond safe thresholds.

## The Kinetic Chain in Tennis

### Principle of Sequential Segment Activation

The kinetic chain operates on the principle that energy is generated by the largest, most powerful body segments first, then transferred sequentially to smaller, faster segments:

1. **Ground Reaction Forces:** Legs push against the ground, generating force that travels upward
2. **Hip Rotation:** The hips rotate toward the target, transferring energy to the trunk
3. **Trunk Rotation:** The shoulders rotate, further accelerating the energy transfer
4. **Arm Acceleration:** The arm whips forward, adding speed to the racket head
5. **Wrist Snap:** The wrist flexes at contact, providing the final increment of racket head speed

### The Kinetic Chain Ratio

Research indicates the approximate contribution of each segment to racket head speed:

| Segment | Contribution to Racket Head Speed |
| :--- | :--- |
| Legs and Hip Drive | 40-50% |
| Trunk Rotation | 20-30% |
| Shoulder Internal Rotation | 15-20% |
| Forearm Pronation and Wrist | 10-15% |

Players who rely excessively on the arm (rather than the legs and trunk) generate less power and place higher loads on the shoulder and elbow.

## Serve Biomechanics

### Phase 1: Wind-Up and Loading

- **Knee Flexion:** 90-110 degrees of knee bend; deeper flexion stores more elastic energy
- **Hip-Shoulder Separation:** 45-60 degrees of separation between hip and shoulder rotation (the X-factor)
- **Shoulder External Rotation:** 160-180 degrees of external rotation in the cocking phase (the "loaded spring")
- **Scapular Retraction:** The shoulder blade retracts to maximize shoulder external rotation

### Phase 2: Drive and Explosion

- **Leg Extension:** Knee extension velocities of 200-400 degrees per second; vertical ground reaction forces of 1.5-2.5 times body weight
- **Trunk Rotation:** Hip rotation precedes shoulder rotation by 30-50 milliseconds
- **Shoulder Internal Rotation:** Peak angular velocities of 2,000-4,000 degrees per second (the fastest human movement)
- **Forearm Pronation:** 1,000-2,000 degrees per second
- **Wrist Flexion:** 1,500-2,500 degrees per second

### Phase 3: Contact and Follow-Through

- **Contact Height:** Maximum reach (toes + arm + racket); elite males reach 9-10 feet
- **Elbow Extension:** Near-full extension at contact for maximum reach
- **Deceleration Phase:** The arm decelerates rapidly after contact; the rotator cuff and scapular muscles absorb the deceleration forces

### Joint Loading During the Serve

| Joint | Primary Load | Injury Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shoulder | Compression, shear, and distraction forces | Rotator cuff tendinopathy, labral tears |
| Elbow | Valgus stress, extension force | Medial epicondylitis, UCL strain |
| Lower Back | Compression, shear, rotation | Disc herniation, spondylolysis |
| Knee | Compression, shear during drive | Patellar tendinopathy, meniscal stress |

## Forehand Biomechanics

### The Modern Forehand Kinetic Chain

1. **Leg Drive:** Push off the ground, extending the back leg and rotating the hips
2. **Hip Rotation:** Hips rotate toward the target, initiating the kinetic chain
3. **Trunk Rotation:** Shoulders follow hip rotation, creating the whip effect
4. **Arm Acceleration:** The arm extends and the forearm pronates
5. **Contact:** Maximum racket head speed at contact (70-90+ mph)
6. **Follow-Through:** Arm continues across the body; deceleration controlled by trunk and shoulder muscles

### Comparison: Eastern vs. Semi-Western Forehand

| Element | Eastern | Semi-Western |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Grip | Bevel 2 | Bevel 4 |
| Swing Path | More horizontal | More vertical (loop) |
| Spin Generation | Moderate topspin | Heavy topspin |
| Contact Height | Waist to chest | Waist to shoulder |
| Joint Loading | Lower shoulder stress | Higher shoulder stress |
| Low Ball Handling | Better | Requires adjustment |

### Joint Loading During the Forehand

| Joint | Primary Load | Common Injuries |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shoulder | Distraction, rotation | Rotator cuff tendinopathy |
| Elbow | Valgus stress, wrist extension | Lateral epicondylitis |
| Wrist | Extension, radial deviation | Tendinopathy, TFCC tears |
| Lower Back | Rotation, extension | Muscle strains, disc issues |

## Backhand Biomechanics

### Two-Handed Backhand

The two-handed backhand involves both arms in the stroke, creating unique biomechanical demands:

- **Dominant Arm (bottom):** Provides racket face stability and drives through contact
- **Non-Dominant Arm (top):** Controls the backswing and adds power
- **Trunk Rotation:** Both shoulders rotate, with the non-dominant shoulder leading the backswing
- **Joint Loading:** Lower shoulder stress than one-handed backhand due to two-arm support

### One-Handed Backhand

The one-handed backhand places higher demands on individual arm strength:

- **Trunk Rotation:** Greater hip-shoulder separation required for power
- **Arm Acceleration:** The single arm must generate all racket head speed
- **Joint Loading:** Higher shoulder and elbow stress than two-handed backhand
- **Deceleration:** The rotator cuff must absorb all deceleration forces

## Injury Mechanisms in Tennis

### Overuse Injury Mechanisms

Overuse injuries result from repetitive loading without adequate recovery:

- **Rotator Cuff Tendinopathy:** Repetitive overhead loading during serves and overheads
- **Lateral Epicondylitis:** Repetitive wrist extension during backhands and serves
- **Patellar Tendinopathy:** Repetitive jumping and landing during serves
- **Disc Herniation:** Repetitive rotation and compression during serves

### Acute Injury Mechanisms

Acute injuries result from sudden, high-force events:

- **Ankle Sprains:** Sudden inversion during lateral movements
- **Muscle Strains:** Sudden eccentric loading during sprinting or lunging
- **ACL Tears:** Sudden deceleration with rotation

## Biomechanical Screening for Injury Risk

### Shoulder Screening

| Assessment | What to Measure | Risk Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Glenohumeral Internal Rotation | Degrees of internal rotation | GIRD > 18 degrees vs. non-dominant |
| Glenohumeral External Rotation | Degrees of external rotation | Total arc < 180 degrees |
| Scapular Dyskinesis | Scapular positioning and movement | Asymmetry or winging |
| Rotator Cuff Strength | Internal/external rotation strength | Ratio < 66% external/internal |

### Lower Extremity Screening

| Assessment | What to Measure | Risk Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Single-Leg Squat | Knee valgus, trunk lean | Knee valgus > 10 degrees |
| Ankle Dorsiflexion | Degrees of dorsiflexion | < 30 degrees |
| Hamstring Flexibility | Degrees of hip flexion | < 70 degrees |
| Quadriceps Strength | Isokinetic strength ratio | H:Q ratio < 60% |

## Practical Application: "The Biomechanical Serve Checklist"

Use a biomechanical checklist for serve analysis:

**Loading Phase:**
- [ ] Knee flexion 90+ degrees
- [ ] Hip-shoulder separation 45+ degrees
- [ ] Shoulder external rotation 160+ degrees
- [ ] Racket below ball height (lag position)
- [ ] Weight loaded on back foot

**Drive Phase:**
- [ ] Leg extension initiates the motion
- [ ] Hip rotation precedes shoulder rotation
- [ ] Trunk uncoiling transfers energy to the arm
- [ ] Shoulder internal rotation is rapid and explosive

**Contact Phase:**
- [ ] Contact at maximum reach height
- [ ] Elbow near-full extension
- [ ] Forearm pronation adds spin and velocity
- [ ] Wrist snap at contact

**Follow-Through:**
- [ ] Racket decelerates across the body
- [ ] Front foot lands inside the court
- [ ] Body remains balanced
