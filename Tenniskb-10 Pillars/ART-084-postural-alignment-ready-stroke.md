# ART:084: Posturalural Alignment in Tennis Ready and Stroke Positions

## Executive Summary
Optimal postural alignment is the foundation of efficient tennis movement and stroke production. Proper alignment in the ready position and during strokes ensures that forces are transmitted efficiently through the kinetic chain, reduces injury risk, and enables the player to move quickly in any direction. This article presents the key postural principles for tennis and common deviations that compromise performance.

## Ready Position Alignment

### Key Elements
- **Feet**: Shoulder-width apart, weight on balls of feet
- **Knees**: Slightly flexed, tracking over toes
- **Hips**: Level, neutral pelvis
- **Trunk**: Slightly forward lean, neutral spine
- **Shoulders**: Relaxed, slightly forward
- **Head**: Up, eyes on the ball

### Common Deviations
- **Flat-footed stance**: Reduces reactivity and ground connection
- **Straight legs**: Reduces loading capacity and force production
- **Excessive forward lean**: Shifts center of mass too far forward
- **Hunched shoulders**: Restricts shoulder mobility and breathing

## Stroke Position Alignment

### Loading Phase
- **Knee flexion**: 60-90 degrees for optimal force production
- **Pelvis**: Stable, level
- **Trunk**: Rotated away from target, storing elastic energy
- **Shoulders**: Rotated further than hips (hip-shoulder separation)

### Forward Swing
- **Leg drive**: Full extension of ankles, knees, hips
- **Hip rotation**: Leading the kinetic chain
- **Trunk rotation**: Following hips, adding momentum
- **Arm acceleration**: Extending through contact

### Contact
- **Stable base**: Wide enough for balance
- **Optimal contact point**: In front of body, at waist-to-shoulder height
- **Firm wrist**: Stable at contact for energy transfer

## Practical Application: "The Posture Check"
Before each point, perform a quick posture check: feet shoulder-width, knees bent, weight forward, trunk tall, shoulders relaxed. This simple check ensures you're in the optimal ready position to move and stroke effectively.
