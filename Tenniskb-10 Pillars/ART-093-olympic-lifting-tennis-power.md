# ART:093: Olympic Lifting for Tennis Power Development

## Executive Summary
Olympic lifts—the clean, jerk, and snatch—develop the explosive triple-extension power (ankle, knee, hip) that is the foundation of tennis stroke production. These lifts train the body to generate maximum force in minimum time, a quality that directly translates to serve velocity, groundstroke power, and first-step quickness. While the full Olympic lifts require technical proficiency, their variations (hang cleans, power cleans, hang snatches) can be safely incorporated into tennis training programs.

## Olympic Lifts for Tennis

### Hang Power Clean
- **Movement**: Lift the bar from mid-thigh to the shoulders in one explosive motion
- **Triple extension**: Ankle, knee, and hip extend simultaneously
- **Transfer to tennis**: Mimics the leg drive of the serve and groundstrokes

### Hang Power Snatch
- **Movement**: Lift the bar from mid-thigh to overhead in one explosive motion
- **Triple extension**: Ankle, knee, and hip extend simultaneously
- **Transfer to tennis**: Mimics the serve's upward and forward movement

### Push Press
- **Movement**: Use leg drive to press the bar overhead
- **Triple extension**: Ankle, knee, and hip extend to drive the bar
- **Transfer to tennis**: Mimics the serve's upward drive

## Practical Application: "The Olympic Routine"
Perform Olympic lift variations 2 times per week: (1) 3 sets of 3 hang power cleans, (2) 3 sets of 3 hang power snatches, (3) 3 sets of 3 push presses. Focus on maximum intentional speed during the triple extension phase. The explosive power developed through these lifts directly transfers to your serve and groundstroke power.
