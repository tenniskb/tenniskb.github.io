# ART:051: Racket Head Speed vs. Ball Speed Relationship

## Executive Summary
Ball exit speed is determined by racket head speed, the coefficient of restitution, and the effective mass of the racket. The relationship is approximately linear: a 10% increase in racket speed produces roughly a 10% increase in ball speed, assuming contact at the sweet spot. Understanding this relationship enables players to optimize their racket speed through proper kinetic chain mechanics while recognizing that equipment choices can also influence the conversion efficiency.

## The Racket Speed to Ball Speed Equation

### Simplified Formula
v_ball ≈ v_racket × (1 + COR) × (m_racket_effective / (m_racket_effective + m_ball))

### Typical Conversion Ratios
- **Serve**: Ball speed ≈ 1.5-1.8 × racket head speed
- **Groundstroke**: Ball speed ≈ 1.4-1.6 × racket head speed
- **Volley**: Ball speed ≈ 1.1-1.3 × racket head speed

### Efficiency Factors
- **COR**: Higher COR = higher conversion ratio
- **Effective mass**: Higher effective mass = higher conversion ratio
- **Contact location**: Sweet spot = highest conversion ratio

## Practical Application: "The Speed Multiplier"
Think of your racket as a speed multiplier. If you swing at 30 m/s and the multiplier is 1.5, your ball exits at 45 m/s (100 mph). To increase ball speed, either increase racket speed (through better mechanics) or increase the multiplier (through better equipment). The most effective approach is to improve both—better mechanics AND better equipment.
