# ART:100: Wearable Technology for Tennis Biomechanics Monitoring

## Executive Summary
Wearable technology—including inertial measurement units (IMUs), GPS trackers, and heart rate monitors—provides real-time biomechanical data that can transform tennis training and match analysis. These devices measure movement patterns, stroke mechanics, workload, and physiological responses during actual play, providing insights that were previously available only in laboratory settings. This article presents the current state of wearable technology for tennis biomechanics and its practical applications.

## Types of Wearable Technology

### Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)
- **Components**: Accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers
- **Placement**: On the racket, wrist, trunk, or hip
- **Data**: Acceleration, angular velocity, orientation
- **Applications**: Stroke classification, racket speed, movement analysis

### GPS Trackers
- **Placement**: Upper back (in a vest)
- **Data**: Position, speed, distance, acceleration/deceleration
- **Applications**: Movement analysis, workload quantification

### Heart Rate Monitors
- **Placement**: Chest strap or wrist-based
- **Data**: Heart rate, heart rate variability
- **Applications**: Physiological load monitoring, recovery assessment

### Pressure-Sensing Insoles
- **Placement**: Inside the shoe
- **Data**: Plantar pressure distribution, center of pressure trajectory
- **Applications**: Footwork analysis, balance assessment

## Practical Applications

### Stroke Analysis
- IMUs on the racket measure racket head speed, spin rate, and swing path
- Data can be analyzed to identify technical faults
- Real-time feedback enables immediate correction

### Workload Monitoring
- GPS and IMUs quantify the total movement load of training and matches
- Data can be used to optimize training load and prevent overtraining
- Long-term tracking reveals fitness improvements

### Injury Prevention
- Wearable data can identify movement patterns that increase injury risk
- Fatigue detection through changes in movement patterns
- Early warning systems for potential injuries

## Practical Application: "The Wearable Routine"
Incorporate wearable technology into your training: (1) Use an IMU on your racket to track your racket head speed during practice sessions. (2) Use a GPS tracker to monitor your movement load during matches. (3) Use pressure-sensing insoles to analyze your footwork patterns. The data from these devices provides objective feedback that can guide your training and accelerate your development. Wearable technology democratizes access to biomechanical data that was once available only in elite laboratories.
