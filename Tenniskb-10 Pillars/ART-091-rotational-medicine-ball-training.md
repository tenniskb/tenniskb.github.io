# ART:091: Rotational Medicine Ball Training for Tennis

## Executive Summary
Medicine ball training is one of the most effective methods for developing rotational power in tennis. The medicine ball's weight forces the player to use the entire kinetic chain to generate force, while its size allows for sport-specific movement patterns. Rotational medicine ball exercises develop the coordination, timing, and power that directly transfer to groundstroke and serve performance.

## Key Medicine Ball Exercises

### Rotational Throws
- **Standing rotational throw**: Throw the ball diagonally across the body, emphasizing hip-trunk separation
- **Split-stance rotational throw**: Throw from a tennis stance, mimicking stroke mechanics
- **Kneeling rotational throw**: Isolate the trunk rotation component

### Overhead Throws
- **Overhead slam**: Develop whole-body power from ground through arm
- **Overhead backward throw**: Develop posterior chain power
- **Overhead rotational throw**: Combine rotation with overhead power

### Chest Passes
- **Rotational chest pass**: Throw the ball while rotating the trunk
- **Step-and-throw**: Develop weight transfer and rotation integration

## Practical Application: "The Medicine Ball Routine"
Perform a medicine ball routine 2-3 times per week: (1) 10 standing rotational throws each side, (2) 10 split-stance rotational throws each side, (3) 10 overhead slams, (4) 10 rotational chest passes each side. This 10-minute routine develops the rotational power that directly transfers to your tennis strokes.
