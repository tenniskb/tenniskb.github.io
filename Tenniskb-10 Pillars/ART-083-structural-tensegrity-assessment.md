# ART:083: Structural Tensegrity Assessment for Tennis Players

## Executive Summary
Structural tensegrity assessment evaluates the balance between the body's compression elements (bones) and tension elements (muscles, fascia, tendons). In a healthy, high-performing tennis player, this balance ensures efficient force transmission, structural integrity during movement, and resilience to injury. This article presents a practical framework for assessing structural tensegrity in tennis players.

## Tensegrity Assessment Framework

### Compression Element Assessment
- **Bone density**: Adequate for the demands of tennis
- **Joint alignment**: Proper alignment under load
- **Structural integrity**: No stress fractures or degenerative changes

### Tension Element Assessment
- **Muscle strength**: Balanced between agonist and antagonist
- **Fascial integrity**: No restrictions or adhesions
- **Tendon health**: No tendinopathy or degeneration
- **Ligamentous integrity**: No excessive laxity or tightness

### Integration Assessment
- **Postural alignment**: Optimal alignment in static and dynamic positions
- **Movement quality**: Efficient, coordinated movement patterns
- **Force transmission**: Effective transfer through the kinetic chain

## Practical Application: "The Tensegrity Screen"
Perform a simple tensegrity screen by evaluating: (1) Can the player maintain optimal posture under load? (2) Are forces transmitted efficiently through the kinetic chain? (3) Is there any pain or dysfunction during movement? Any "no" answers indicate a tensegrity imbalance that needs to be addressed.
