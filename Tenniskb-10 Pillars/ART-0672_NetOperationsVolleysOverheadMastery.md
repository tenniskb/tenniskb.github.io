# ART-0672: The Volley Against the Body Serve — Handling Jammer Returns

## Executive Summary

The body serve is directed at the returner's body, jamming them and forcing a weak return. When you are the net player in doubles facing a body serve return, the ball is often hit directly at your body, requiring a block volley with minimal swing. Handling body serve returns requires the "racket in front" position and the ability to block and redirect without swinging.

## The Body Serve Return

The body serve return: (1) is hit with minimal swing (the returner is jammed), (2) often comes directly at the net player's body, (3) has moderate pace. At the net, the body serve return is difficult because there is no room to extend the racket and the ball arrives at the body.

## Volley Technique Against Body Serve Returns

Against body serve returns: (1) hold the racket in front of your body at chest height, (2) use the block volley (no swing), (3) step slightly to the side to create space (if time allows), (4) redirect cross-court (the safest option), (5) use a firm wrist. The key is to block and redirect without swinging.

## Common Errors

Ducking or turning away (natural reflex but misses). Swinging at the ball (no time, no space). Holding the racket too low (slow reaction). Not stepping to the side to create space.

## Summary

Body serve returns demand the block volley: hold the racket in front, block the ball, redirect cross-court. The "racket in front" position is essential.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Swings at body returns | No block technique | Late contact | Use block volley, no swing |
| Ducking from body returns | Fear reflex | Missed volleys | Face the ball, block with firm wrist |
| Racket not in front | Holding racket too low | Slow reaction | Maintain "racket in front" position |
| Cannot create space | Not stepping to side | Jammed volley | Step slightly to side before blocking |

## Practical Application: "Racket in Front, Block and Redirect"

Against body serve returns: (1) hold the racket in front, (2) block the ball, (3) redirect cross-court. The block volley is your primary weapon.
