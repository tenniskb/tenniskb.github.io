# ART-515: Serve Placement Strategy: Wide, Body, and T Targeting
## Executive Summary

Serve placement is as important as serve speed and spin. The three primary placement targets — wide, body, and T — each create different tactical advantages depending on the returner's positioning, strengths, and the match situation. This article provides a strategic framework for serve placement decisions.

## The Three Primary Placement Targets

### Wide Serve
The wide serve lands near the sideline of the service box, pulling the returner off the court.

**Advantages:**
- Opens up the court for the next shot
- Forces the returner to cover maximum distance
- Can neutralize a strong forehand or backhand

**Disadvantages:**
- Smallest margin for error (near the sideline)
- Requires precise mechanics
- Returner may anticipate and start moving early

**Best used when:**
- Returner is hugging the center mark
- You want to open up the court for a forehand approach
- Playing on clay where the ball slows down after the wide bounce

### Body Serve
The body serve targets the returner's torso, jamming them and preventing a full swing.

**Advantages:**
- Forces the returner to hit with restricted arm extension
- Effective against players with long swings who need space
- Difficult to generate power on the return

**Disadvantages:**
- May hit the returner (let or replay)
- Requires precise direction control
- Can be effective only if the returner does not move

**Best used when:**
- Returner has a long, looping swing
- You want to neutralize a strong returner
- Serving at body limits return angles

### T Serve (Down the Middle)
The T serve lands near the center of the service box, splitting the court.

**Advantages:**
- Shortest distance to the net (lowest net height at center)
- Narrows the return angle
- Forces the returner to hit from the middle of the court

**Disadvantages:**
- Does not pull the returner off the court
- Gives the returner time to set up
- Less effective if returner anticipates the serve

**Best used when:**
- Returner is positioned to cover the wide serve
- You want to reduce the return angle
- Playing on fast surfaces where the serve skids through

## Court Geometry and Serve Angles

The effectiveness of each placement depends on the geometry of the court:

### Deuce Court Geometry
- Wide serve: Pulls the returner to their forehand side (for right-handed returners)
- Body serve: Jams the returner's forehand side
- T serve: Splits the court, reducing backhand return angles

### Ad Court Geometry
- Wide serve: Pulls the returner to their backhand side (for right-handed returners)
- Body serve: Jams the returner's backhand side
- T serve: Splits the court, reducing forehand return angles

## Placement by Surface

### Fast Surfaces (Grass, Fast Hard Courts)
- Wide serves are more effective because the ball stays low and skids
- T serves are effective because the returner has less time
- Body serves are less effective because the fast pace gives the returner less time to react

### Slow Surfaces (Clay, Slow Hard Courts)
- Wide serves are less effective because the slow surface allows the returner to cover more ground
- Body serves are more effective because the returner has more time to react but less pace to handle
- T serves are effective for reducing return angles

## Placement Patterns

### Pattern 1: Wide-T-Wide
Alternate between wide and T serves to keep the returner guessing. After two wide serves, the returner begins cheating toward the sideline, opening up the T.

### Pattern 2: Body-Wide
Hit a body serve to freeze the returner, then follow with a wide serve to pull them off court. The body serve prevents the returner from leaning toward the sideline.

### Pattern 3: Same Side Twice
Hit two serves to the same side to establish a pattern, then break the pattern with a surprise placement.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wide serves consistently go long | Over-rotation or excessive spin | Errors wide | Target practice with cones; reduce spin on wide serves |
| Body serves miss into the net | Closed racket face at contact | Weak serves into net | Racket face angle adjustment; target practice |
| T serves lack pace | Overly conservative serve placement | Easy returns for partner | Speed drills targeting T; commit fully to placement |
| Returner consistently covers wide serve | Predictable serve placement | Wide serves ineffective | Pattern variation; mix up placement |
| Cannot control placement direction | Inconsistent toss or racket path | Serves spray in multiple directions | Toss consistency drills; placement-specific practice |

## Practical Application: "The Target Rotation"

Place three targets in the service box: one wide, one at the body, and one at the T. Hit 10 serves to each target in random order, calling out your target before each serve. This drill develops the ability to place serves precisely on demand, a skill that is far more valuable in matches than simply serving hard. Track your success rate for each placement and identify which targets need the most work.
