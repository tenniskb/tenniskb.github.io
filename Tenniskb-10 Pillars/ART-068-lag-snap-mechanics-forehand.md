# ART:068: Lag and Snap Mechanics in the Forehand

## Executive Summary
The lag position—where the racket head trails behind the hand during the forward swing—is a critical element of the modern forehand. This lag creates a whip-like acceleration as the racket head catches up to and then passes the hand, producing the final burst of racket head speed. The "snap" is the moment when the racket head accelerates from behind the hand to in front of it, generating the highest angular velocities. Understanding lag and snap mechanics enables players to develop the modern, powerful forehand that dominates today's game.

## The Lag Position

### Definition
The lag position occurs when:
- The racket head is behind the hand relative to the direction of swing
- The wrist is extended (bent backward)
- The racket is "trailing" behind the arm

### How Lag Creates Power
- **The lag stores elastic energy** in the wrist and forearm muscles
- **The lag creates a longer acceleration path** for the racket head
- **The lag allows the trunk and shoulder to accelerate the arm before the racket head catches up**

### The Snap
- **The snap occurs when the racket head catches up to the hand**
- **The wrist moves from extended to neutral or flexed**
- **The racket head accelerates rapidly, producing the highest angular velocities**
- **The snap is felt as a "crack" of speed at the end of the swing**

## Practical Application: "The Lag and Snap"
To develop the lag and snap, practice the following drill: Start with your racket in the lag position (wrist extended, racket head behind your hand). Now swing forward, letting the racket head lag behind your hand for as long as possible. As your arm reaches full extension, allow the racket head to snap through, catching up to your hand and then passing it. The feeling should be of the racket head "whipping" through the ball. Practice this slowly at first, then gradually increase speed. The lag and snap are what give the modern forehand its explosive power.
