# ART-0685: The Volley Against the Return with Deception — Handling Disguised Shots

## Executive Summary

Deceptive returns are a challenge for net players because the returner hides the direction, pace, or spin of the shot until the last possible moment. When you are the net player facing deceptive returns, you must stay balanced, avoid anticipating, and react to the actual shot rather than the fake. Deceptive returns are used by advanced players to exploit net players who commit early.

## The Deceptive Return

The deceptive return: (1) hides direction, pace, or spin until late, (2) uses body language or racket preparation to mislead, (3) forces the net player to guess. At the net, the deceptive return is frustrating because you cannot rely on early reads — you must wait for the actual shot.

## Technique for Handling Deceptive Returns

Against deceptive returns: (1) stay balanced and do not commit early, (2) wait for the ball to be hit before moving, (3) use the split step to prepare for any direction, (4) react to the actual shot, not the fake, (5) use the block volley for unexpected shots. The key is patience — do not commit until you are sure of the direction.

## Common Errors

Committing early (the deception works). Not split stepping (poor reaction). Anticipating based on body language (the returner uses this against you). Getting frustrated (loss of focus).

## Summary

Deceptive returns demand patience and balance. Stay centered, wait for the actual shot, and react with the split step and block volley.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Commits early on deceptive returns | Anticipating | Wrong direction | Wait for the actual shot |
| No split step on deceptive returns | Poor preparation | Late reaction | Split step before every return |
| Frustrated by deception | Poor mental approach | Loss of focus | Stay calm, accept deception |
| Reads fake instead of real shot | Poor observation | Wrong reaction | Focus on the ball, not the body |

## Practical Application: "Stay Balanced, Wait, React"

Against deceptive returns: (1) stay balanced, (2) wait for the actual shot, (3) react with the split step and block volley. Patience is your greatest weapon against deception.
