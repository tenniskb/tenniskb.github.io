# ART-0260: Nutrition, Hydration, and Cognitive Performance in Tennis

## Executive Summary
Nutrition and hydration directly affect cognitive function in tennis. Dehydration of even 2% body mass impairs attention, working memory, and decision-making. Blood glucose depletion reduces prefrontal cortex function, leading to poor tactical processing. Understanding the cognitive effects of nutrition and hydration allows players to maintain mental sharpness through long matches and tournaments.

## Hydration and Cognitive Function

### The Effects of Dehydration
Dehydration impairs cognitive function in a dose-dependent manner:
- 1-2% dehydration: reduced attention, increased fatigue
- 2-3% dehydration: impaired working memory, slower processing
- 3-4% dehydration: significant cognitive decline, increased perceived effort
- >4% dehydration: severe impairment, risk of heat illness

### Hydration Strategy
- Drink 5-7 ml per kg of body weight 2-4 hours before play
- Drink 150-250 ml every 15-20 minutes during play
- Include electrolytes for matches lasting more than 1 hour
- Monitor urine color as a hydration indicator

## Nutrition and Cognitive Function

### Blood Glucose and the Brain
The brain consumes approximately 20% of the body's glucose. During long matches, blood glucose levels can decline, impairing prefrontal cortex function. This manifests as reduced tactical processing, slower decision-making, and increased perceived effort.

### Pre-Match Nutrition
- Eat a balanced meal 2-4 hours before play
- Include complex carbohydrates for sustained energy
- Include moderate protein for satiety
- Avoid high-fat and high-fiber foods that slow digestion

### During-Match Nutrition
- Consume 30-60 grams of carbohydrates per hour for matches lasting more than 1 hour
- Use easily digestible sources: bananas, energy gels, sports drinks
- Practice your nutrition strategy in training to avoid gastrointestinal issues

### Post-Match Nutrition
- Consume carbohydrates and protein within 30 minutes after play
- Rehydrate with fluids and electrolytes
- Eat a balanced meal within 2 hours

## Caffeine and Cognitive Performance

### Benefits
Caffeine improves attention, reaction time, and perceived exertion. It blocks adenosine receptors, reducing the sensation of fatigue.

### Risks
Caffeine can increase anxiety, disrupt sleep, and cause gastrointestinal distress. Individual responses vary widely.

### Strategy
- Use caffeine strategically for important matches
- Test your response in practice before using in competition
- Avoid caffeine late in the day to protect sleep

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| Cognitive decline in long matches | Dehydration or glucose depletion | Poor decisions | Hydration and nutrition strategy |
| Fatigue increases in heat | Fluid loss | Performance decline | Pre-cooling and hydration |
| Energy crashes between meals | Poor nutrition | Reduced intensity | Pre-match meal timing |
| Caffeine causes anxiety | Overstimulation | Performance decline | Dose adjustment and testing |

## Practical Application: "The Fuel Check"
Before and during matches, perform a regular "fuel check": (1) assess your hydration status (urine color, thirst), (2) assess your energy level (focus, alertness), and (3) assess your perceived effort (is the match feeling harder than it should?). If any of these indicate a problem, take immediate action: drink fluids, consume carbohydrates, or adjust your effort level. This simple check prevents the cognitive decline that occurs when players ignore their nutritional needs until performance has already suffered.
