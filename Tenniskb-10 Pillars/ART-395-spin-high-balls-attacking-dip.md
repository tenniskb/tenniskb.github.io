# ART-395: Spin on High Balls — Attacking with Dip

## Executive Summary

High-bouncing balls (above shoulder height) present an opportunity to attack with heavy topspin, using the high contact point to generate a steep downward swing path that produces both pace and spin. The resulting ball dips sharply after clearing the net, landing deep in the court with a high bounce that pushes the opponent back. Elite players use high balls as attacking opportunities, generating 2500 to 3500 RPM of topspin combined with aggressive pace to produce a devastating dipping shot.

## The Opportunity of High Balls

High-bouncing balls provide three advantages:

1. High contact point: The player can hit downward or level through the ball, generating pace without the net clearance concerns of lower contact points.

2. More time to set up: The high ball's longer flight time allows the player to position optimally.

3. Attacking position: The high ball is often a sign that the opponent is in a defensive position, creating an opportunity to attack.

## The Mechanics of Attacking High Balls

Attacking high balls with topspin involves:

1. A moderate-to-steep swing path (15 to 30 degrees above horizontal): The high contact point allows a steeper path than a standard rally ball, generating more topspin while still maintaining forward drive.

2. A slightly closed face (5 to 10 degrees): The closed face at the high contact point generates heavy topspin.

3. Aggressive trunk rotation: The trunk rotates aggressively to generate maximum racquet head speed.

4. A high-to-moderate follow-through: The follow-through is slightly lower than the standard topspin forehand, reflecting the more aggressive, downward-oriented swing.

## The Dip Effect

The dip effect is particularly pronounced on high-ball forehands because:

1. The high contact point allows a higher trajectory, giving the ball more time to dip.

2. The heavy topspin creates a strong Magnus force, pulling the ball downward.

3. The combination of pace and spin produces a ball that dips sharply and lands deep in the court.

## Training for High Ball Spin

Training involves:

1. High-ball feeding drills: Have a partner feed balls that bounce above shoulder height, practicing the attacking topspin technique.

2. Spin measurement: Use a spin measurement device to track your spin rate on high balls, ensuring you are generating 2500+ RPM.

3. Tactical drills: Practice using high-ball forehands as attacking shots, following up with approach shots or finishing shots.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flat shots on high balls | Missing the opportunity to generate spin | Less effective attacking shots | Add topspin; steepen swing path |
| Insufficient spin on high balls | Balls sailing long | Long errors | Increase topspin; steepen swing path |
| No pace on high balls | Slow shots; easy to handle | Missed opportunities | Increase racquet head speed; add pace |
| Cannot attack high balls | Passive play; lost opportunities | Predictable shot selection | Develop attacking technique; practice confidence |
| Poor timing on high balls | Mishits; off-balance shots | Unforced errors | Practice timing drills; develop consistency |

## Practical Application: "The High Ball Attack"

Practice attacking high balls by having a partner feed heavy topspin balls that bounce above shoulder height. Focus on the moderate-to-steep swing path, the slightly closed face, and the aggressive trunk rotation. Use the high contact point to generate both pace and spin, creating a dipping ball that lands deep in the court and pushes your opponent back.
