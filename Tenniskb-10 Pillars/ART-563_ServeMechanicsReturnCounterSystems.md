# ART-563: Return Mental Approach: Aggression, Patience, and Shot Selection
## Executive Summary

The mental approach to returning serve is just as important as the physical technique. Knowing when to be aggressive, when to be patient, and which shots to choose can make the difference between holding and breaking serve. This article provides a framework for the mental side of returning.

## The Aggression-Patience Spectrum

### Aggressive Returning
**When to be aggressive:**
- Returning second serves
- When you are behind in the game
- Against weaker servers
- When you are feeling confident

**Characteristics:**
- Step inside the baseline
- Use a full swing
- Target the server's weaker side
- Aim to take control of the point

**Risks:**
- More errors if the serve is better than expected
- Vulnerable to fast first serves
- Can lead to frustration if returns miss

### Patient Returning
**When to be patient:**
- Returning fast first serves
- When you are ahead in the game
- Against big servers
- When you are struggling with timing

**Characteristics:**
- Stay at or behind the baseline
- Use a controlled swing
- Focus on getting the ball in play
- Extend the rally

**Risks:**
- Gives the server more time to set up
- Allows the server to dictate play
- Can lead to passive, defensive tennis

## Shot Selection Decisions

### The Decision-Making Process
1. **Assess the serve:** How fast? How much spin? Where is it going?
2. **Assess your position:** Are you on-balance or off-balance? Inside or behind the baseline?
3. **Assess the situation:** What is the score? How is the match going?
4. **Choose the shot:** Block, drive, chip, or lob?

### Shot Selection by Serve Quality

#### Weak Serve (slow, short, sitting up)
- **Shot:** Aggressive drive
- **Target:** Server's weaker side
- **Goal:** Take control of the point

#### Moderate Serve (medium pace, decent placement)
- **Shot:** Controlled drive or block
- **Target:** Crosscourt or server's weaker side
- **Goal:** Neutralize the serve and extend the rally

#### Strong Serve (fast, good placement)
- **Shot:** Block or chip
- **Target:** Crosscourt for safety
- **Goal:** Get the ball back in play

### Shot Selection by Match Situation

#### When You Are Ahead
- Be patient; do not take unnecessary risks
- Focus on consistency
- Extend the rally and let the server make errors

#### When You Are Behind
- Be aggressive; take calculated risks
- Attack second serves
- Look for opportunities to break

#### In Crucial Games
- Balance aggression and patience
- Focus on the process, not the score
- Trust your preparation and instincts

## The Mental Framework for Returning

### Pre-Point Routine
Before each return:
1. **Assess the situation:** What is the score? What is the server doing?
2. **Decide on a plan:** Aggressive or patient? Target the backhand or forehand?
3. **Commit to the plan:** Once you decide, execute with confidence

### During the Point
- Focus on the ball, not the server
- React to the serve; do not overthink
- Trust your instincts and training
- Stay relaxed and loose

### After the Point
- **If you won the point:** Celebrate briefly; focus on the next point
- **If you lost the point:** Let it go; do not dwell on mistakes
- **If you made an error:** Acknowledge it, learn from it, move on

## Common Mental Faults

### Overthinking
- Analyzing too much before the serve
- Trying to predict exactly where the serve will go
- Fix: Trust your instincts; react to what you see

### Fear of Failure
- Afraid of missing returns
- Plays tentatively to avoid errors
- Fix: Accept that errors happen; focus on the process

### Trying to Do Too Much
- Attempting winners off every return
- Taking unnecessary risks
- Fix: Choose the right shot for the situation

### Lack of Patience
- Trying to end the point too quickly
- Not extending the rally
- Fix: Be patient; wait for the right opportunity

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Too passive on second serves | Not attacking | Missed break opportunities | Aggressive return drills; second serve attack practice |
| Over-swinging on first serves | Trying to do too much | Errors on big serves | Block return drills; controlled return practice |
| Cannot decide on a shot | Indecision | Rushed returns | Shot selection drills; decision-making practice |
| Tense under pressure | Anxiety | Poor return quality | Mental training; relaxation exercises |
| No pre-point routine | No preparation | Disorganized returning | Routine development; pre-point planning |

## Practical Application: "The Return Decision Drill"

Have a partner serve while you call out your intended return before hitting:
- "Block crosscourt" for fast serves
- "Drive crosscourt" for moderate serves
- "Attack down the line" for weak second serves

This drill develops the decision-making process that leads to optimal shot selection. By verbalizing your decision, you become more aware of your thought process and can identify areas for improvement.
