# ART-0279: Anxiety and Attention in Tennis

## Executive Summary
Anxiety narrows attention, reducing the player's ability to process peripheral information and maintain broad, flexible focus. This attentional narrowing leads to missed cues, poor tactical awareness, and predictable shot selection. Understanding the relationship between anxiety and attention allows players to maintain attentional control under pressure.

## How Anxiety Affects Attention

### Attentional Narrowing
Anxiety causes attention to contract to a smaller set of cues. A player who normally processes the ball, the opponent, and the court may focus only on the ball when anxious. This narrowing reduces the information available for tactical decision-making.

### Hypervigilance
Anxiety can cause hypervigilance: a state of heightened sensitivity to potential threats. In tennis, this manifests as constantly scanning for problems (e.g., the opponent's strengths, the score, the crowd) rather than focusing on the task.

### Attentional Bias
Anxiety creates an attentional bias toward threat-related information. An anxious player may focus on their opponent's strengths rather than their own, or on the consequences of losing rather than the process of playing.

### Reduced Attentional Control
Anxiety consumes working memory resources, reducing the brain's capacity to direct and maintain attention. This makes it harder to stay focused and easier to be distracted.

## Maintaining Attentional Control Under Anxiety

### External Focus
Direct attention to external cues (the ball, the target, the opponent) rather than internal cues (anxiety, tension, self-doubt). External focus prevents the self-focus that anxiety produces.

### Routines
Use pre-shot and between-point routines to structure attention. Routines provide a clear sequence of attentional targets, preventing anxiety from capturing attention.

### Breathing Techniques
Deep, slow breathing activates the parasympathetic nervous system and reduces anxiety. This restores attentional control by reducing the physiological arousal that narrows attention.

### Mindfulness
Mindfulness training improves the ability to notice when attention has wandered and to return it to the task. This skill is particularly valuable for maintaining focus under anxiety.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| Misses peripheral cues | Attentional narrowing | Surprise shots | External focus training |
| Scans for threats | Hypervigilance | Distraction | Routines and breathing |
| Focuses on negative outcomes | Attentional bias | Increased anxiety | Cognitive reframing |
| Cannot maintain focus | Reduced attentional control | Performance decline | Mindfulness training |

## Practical Application: "The Attention Anchor"
When you notice anxiety narrowing your attention, use an attention anchor: choose a specific external cue (e.g., the ball, the net, the baseline) and focus on it intensely for 3-5 seconds. This focused attention on a single external cue interrupts the attentional narrowing that anxiety produces and restores broad, flexible focus. Practice this anchor in training under pressure until it becomes an automatic response to anxiety, allowing you to maintain the attentional control that underpins high-level performance.
