# ART:071: Kinetic Leak Audit Methodology

## Executive Summary
A kinetic leak audit is a systematic assessment of the tennis player's kinetic chain to identify points where energy is lost during force transmission. By observing the player's movement patterns and using objective testing (force plates, motion capture, video analysis), practitioners can pinpoint specific kinetic leaks and design targeted interventions. This article presents a comprehensive audit methodology that can be applied in both clinical and coaching settings.

## The Audit Framework

### Phase 1: Observation
Watch the player perform each stroke from multiple angles:
- **Front view**: Assess symmetry and alignment
- **Side view**: Assess loading depth and timing
- **Rear view**: Assess hip-trunk separation and rotation
- **Top view** (if available): Assess rotational patterns

### Phase 2: Segmental Analysis
Analyze each kinetic chain segment:
1. **Ground connection**: Is the player pushing effectively off the ground?
2. **Leg drive**: Are the legs extending fully and forcefully?
3. **Hip rotation**: Are the hips rotating fully and at the right time?
4. **Trunk rotation**: Is the trunk rotating fully and sequentially?
5. **Shoulder rotation**: Is the shoulder rotating and accelerating the arm?
6. **Arm acceleration**: Is the arm extending and accelerating the racket?
7. **Racket delivery**: Is the racket contacting the ball at the sweet spot?

### Phase 3: Timing Analysis
Assess the timing of segmental activation:
- Are segments activating in the proximal-to-distal sequence?
- Is each segment reaching peak velocity before the next segment begins?
- Are there any timing errors (simultaneous activation, reversed sequence)?

### Phase 4: Objective Testing
Use objective measures to confirm observations:
- **Force plate testing**: Measure ground reaction forces and asymmetries
- **Motion capture**: Measure segmental angular velocities and timing
- **Video analysis**: Slow-motion review of movement patterns
- **Strength testing**: Assess individual segment strength

## Practical Application: "The Audit Process"
Perform a kinetic leak audit on yourself or your players by following the four-phase framework. Start with observation, then analyze each segment, then assess timing, and finally confirm with objective testing. Document your findings and design targeted interventions to address each identified leak. Regular audits (every 4-6 weeks) allow you to track progress and ensure that interventions are effective.
