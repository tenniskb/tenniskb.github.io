# ART:066: Racket Angular Velocity and Linear Speed Conversion

## Executive Summary
The angular velocity of the racket (measured in degrees per second) converts to linear ball speed (measured in meters per second) at contact through the relationship v = ω × r, where ω is the angular velocity and r is the radius (distance from the axis of rotation to the contact point). This conversion explains why longer arms, extended contact points, and higher angular velocities all contribute to greater ball speed. Understanding this relationship enables players to optimize their mechanics for maximum ball speed.

## The Conversion Formula

### Linear Velocity from Angular Velocity
v_racket_head = ω × r

Where:
- v_racket_head = linear velocity of the racket head (m/s)
- ω = angular velocity of the racket (rad/s)
- r = distance from the axis of rotation to the racket head (m)

### Factors Affecting Conversion
- **Higher angular velocity → higher linear velocity**
- **Longer radius (extended arm) → higher linear velocity**
- **Contact at the tip → highest linear velocity** (but lowest effective mass)

## Practical Application: "The Lever Effect"
Your arm and racket form a lever: the axis of rotation is your shoulder, the radius is your arm length plus racket length, and the linear velocity at the racket head is the product of angular velocity and radius. To maximize ball speed, maximize both angular velocity (through kinetic chain mechanics) and radius (through full arm extension at contact). The lever effect explains why taller players with longer arms have a natural advantage in generating ball speed—their longer radius converts the same angular velocity into higher linear velocity.
