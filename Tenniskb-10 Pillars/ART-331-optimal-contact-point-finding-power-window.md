# ART-331: Optimal Contact Point — Finding the Power Window

## Executive Summary

The power window is the specific contact point location where the forehand produces maximum racquet head speed for a given level of effort. This window is unique to each player, determined by their height, arm length, flexibility, and stroke mechanics, and is typically located 0.5 to 0.7 meters in front of the front hip and 0.7 to 0.9 meters above the court. Finding and consistently reproducing the power window is one of the most impactful technical improvements a player can make, as it increases ball speed by 5 to 15 percent without any increase in physical exertion.

## Defining the Power Window

The power window is not a single point but a small volume in space—approximately 0.2m forward by 0.15m lateral by 0.2m vertical—where the player's racquet head reaches peak velocity with the arm at optimal extension. Within this window, the kinetic chain's energy transfer is maximized, and the racquet's velocity vector is most directly aligned with the intended target.

The power window can be identified by hitting a series of forehands at varying contact points and measuring the resulting ball speeds (using a radar gun or ball-mark analysis). The contact point that produces the highest ball speed is the center of the power window.

## Factors That Determine the Power Window

Five primary factors determine the location of an individual player's power window:

1. Height and arm length: Taller players with longer arms typically have a power window further from the body (0.6 to 0.8m in front) because their longer levers produce maximum velocity at greater extension.

2. Trunk rotation velocity: Players with faster trunk rotation can contact the ball slightly further in front, as the racquet head is still accelerating at greater distances.

3. Shoulder flexibility: Greater shoulder external rotation allows the racquet to be positioned further behind the body, creating a longer acceleration path and a more forward power window.

4. Leg strength: Stronger legs produce higher ground forces, allowing the player to maintain stability at more forward contact points.

5. Grip type: Western grip users typically have a higher power window (0.8 to 1.0m) than eastern grip users (0.6 to 0.8m) because the closed face angle naturally contacts the ball at a higher point.

## Finding Your Power Window

To find your power window, perform the following drill:

1. Stand in your normal forehand position and mark the ground at 0.4m, 0.5m, 0.6m, and 0.7m in front of your front hip.

2. Have a partner feed balls at a consistent height and pace, and practice contacting each ball at each of the four marked distances.

3. Measure the ball speed (or have a coach assess the ball's pace) for each contact distance.

4. The distance that produces the highest ball speed is the forward center of your power window.

Repeat this process at varying heights to determine the vertical center of your window.

## Reproducing the Power Window

Once identified, the power window must be reproduced consistently through footwork and timing. The key is to develop the motor program that positions the body so the ball naturally arrives in the power window. This requires:

1. Footwork patterns that consistently deliver the body to the correct position relative to the ball.

2. Timing patterns that ensure the forward swing peaks when the ball is in the power window.

3. Proprioceptive awareness of where the contact point is relative to the body during each shot.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Never identified personal power window | Suboptimal contact point; wasted energy | Lower performance ceiling | Perform the power window identification drill |
| Contact point varies within the power window | Inconsistent power output | Variable shot quality | Develop footwork and timing consistency |
| Power window changes as player develops | Outdated contact point habits | Stagnant performance | Re-assess power window periodically |
| Power window too narrow (very precise) | Difficult to reproduce consistently | Frustration; frequent errors | Expand footwork tolerance; accept functional range |
| Power window does not match biomechanics | Forcing an unnatural position | Injury risk; reduced quality | Re-design window based on current body mechanics |

## Practical Application: "The Power Window Drill"

Spend 15 minutes of each practice session hitting foreballs at your identified power window. Use a visual reference on the court to mark the window's location. Focus on the feeling of maximum power with minimum effort—the sensation that the ball is "jumping" off the racquet. This feeling is the feedback signal that you are in the power window.
