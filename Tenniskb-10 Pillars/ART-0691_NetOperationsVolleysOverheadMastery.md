# ART-0691: The Volley Against the Return with High Height — Handling Shoulder-to-Head Returns

## Executive Summary

Returns hit at shoulder-to-head height are challenging for net players because the high contact point reduces the downward angle available on the volley. When you are the net player facing a high return, you must let the ball drop slightly, use a closed racket face, and punch downward to bring the ball into the court. High returns are common against servers who hit flat, low serves that are returned at height.

## The High Return

The high return: (1) is hit at shoulder-to-head height, (2) is common against flat serves (the returner takes the ball at height), (3) is hit with moderate-to-high pace. At the net, the high return forces contact above the ideal volley height, requiring adjustments to standard volley technique.

## Technique for Handling High Returns

Against high returns: (1) let the ball drop slightly to a comfortable height, (2) close the racket face more than usual, (3) use a downward punch volley, (4) aim deep to the baseline, (5) use a firm wrist. The key is to close the face and punch downward to control the high-bouncing ball.

## Common Errors

Not letting the ball drop (contact too high). Not closing the face enough (ball goes long). Using a flat punch (the ball jumps off the racket). Not anticipating the high bounce.

## Summary

High returns demand a closed racket face and downward punch volley. Let the ball drop, close the face, and punch downward.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Volley goes long | Face too open, not handling high ball | Ball past baseline | Close face more, punch downward |
| Contact too high | Not letting ball drop | Weak volley | Let ball drop to comfortable height |
| Cannot handle high bounce | Not anticipating | Erratic contact | Close face, anticipate high bounce |
| Volley lacks downward angle | Flat punch | Easy return | Use downward punch volley |

## Practical Application: "Let It Drop, Close the Face, Punch Down"

Against high returns: (1) let the ball drop, (2) close the racket face, (3) punch downward. The high ball is manageable with the right technique.
