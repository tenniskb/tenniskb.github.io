# ART-817: Nutrition for Tennis Performance

## Executive Summary

Nutrition plays a critical role in tennis performance, recovery, and long-term health. The sport's intermittent high-intensity demands require adequate glycogen stores for energy, protein for muscle repair, and micronutrients for optimal physiological function. Effective tennis nutrition involves daily macronutrient planning, strategic hydration protocols, and tournament-specific nutritional strategies that fuel performance while minimizing gastrointestinal distress. Players who optimize their nutritional intake gain competitive advantages in energy availability, recovery speed, and mental clarity.

## Macronutrient Requirements

### Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are the primary fuel source for tennis's high-intensity efforts:

- **Training Days:** 5-8 g per kg body weight per day
- **Match Days:** 7-10 g per kg body weight per day
- **Recovery Days:** 3-5 g per kg body weight per day

**Sources:** Whole grains, rice, pasta, bread, fruits, vegetables, potatoes, oats

**Timing:** Distribute across meals and snacks; emphasize pre-match (3-4 hours before), during-match (if applicable), and post-match (within 30 minutes) intake.

### Protein

Protein supports muscle repair, immune function, and recovery:

- **Daily Intake:** 1.4-2.0 g per kg body weight per day
- **Distribution:** 20-40 g per meal, 4-5 meals per day
- **Sources:** Lean meats, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, protein supplements

**Key Timing:** Post-match protein within 30 minutes; protein before sleep for overnight recovery.

### Fats

Fats provide essential fatty acids, support hormone production, and aid in vitamin absorption:

- **Daily Intake:** 0.8-1.2 g per kg body weight per day
- **Focus:** Unsaturated fats (omega-3s, olive oil, nuts, avocados)
- **Limit:** Saturated fats (fried foods, processed meats, full-fat dairy)

| Macronutrient | Training Day | Match Day | Recovery Day |

## Hydration Strategies

### Daily Hydration

- **Baseline:** 35-40 mL per kg body weight per day (2.5-3.0 L for 70 kg player)
- **Additional:** 500 mL per hour of training or competition
- **Monitor:** Urine color (pale yellow indicates adequate hydration)

### Pre-Match Hydration

- **3-4 Hours Before:** 500-600 mL of water or electrolyte drink
- **1-2 Hours Before:** 250-300 mL of water
- **15 Minutes Before:** 150-200 mL of water

### During-Match Hydration

- **Changeovers:** 150-250 mL of water or electrolyte drink every changeover
- **Hot Conditions:** Increase to 200-300 mL per changeover
- **Electrolytes:** Include sodium (300-600 mg per liter) for sessions over 60 minutes

### Post-Match Rehydration

- **Target:** 150% of body weight lost during the match
- **Method:** Weigh before and after; replace each kg lost with 1.5 L of fluid
- **Include:** Sodium to enhance fluid retention

### Hydration Benchmarks

| Hydration Status | Urine Color | Body Weight Change | Performance Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Well-Hydrated | Pale yellow | < 1% loss | No impact |
| Mild Dehydration | Yellow | 1-2% loss | 5-10% performance decline |
| Moderate Dehydration | Dark yellow | 2-4% loss | 10-20% performance decline |
| Severe Dehydration | Amber/orange | > 4% loss | > 20% performance decline, health risk |

## Tournament Nutrition

### Pre-Match Meal (3-4 Hours Before)

**Goal:** Top off glycogen stores without causing GI distress.

**Composition:**
- Moderate-high carbohydrates (1-2 g per kg body weight)
- Moderate protein (15-25 g)
- Low fat and low fiber (to minimize GI distress)

**Examples:**
- White rice with grilled chicken and steamed vegetables
- Pasta with tomato sauce and lean protein
- Bagel with peanut butter and banana

### Pre-Match Snack (1-2 Hours Before)

**Goal:** Maintain blood glucose without overeating.

**Examples:**
- Banana with a small amount of nut butter
- Sports drink and a granola bar
- Toast with honey
- Energy bar with moderate carbs and low fat

### During Match (if applicable)

**Goal:** Maintain blood glucose and hydration.

**Options:**
- Sports drink (6-8% carbohydrate solution)
- Energy gels (20-30 g carbs per gel, taken with water)
- Banana (easy to digest, provides potassium)
- Dried fruit (dates, raisins for quick energy)

### Post-Match Recovery Meal (Within 30 Minutes)

**Goal:** Begin glycogen replenishment and muscle repair.

**Composition:**
- 3:1 or 4:1 carbohydrate-to-protein ratio
- 1.0-1.2 g per kg body weight of carbohydrates
- 20-40 g of protein

**Examples:**
- Chocolate milk (natural 3:1 ratio)
- Protein shake with banana and oats
- Turkey sandwich on white bread
- Rice bowl with grilled chicken

## Micronutrient Considerations

### Iron

- **Role:** Oxygen transport, energy production
- **Sources:** Red meat, spinach, fortified cereals, lentils
- **Risk:** Female athletes and vegetarians at higher risk of deficiency
- **Monitor:** Annual blood tests (ferritin levels)

### Calcium and Vitamin D

- **Role:** Bone health, muscle function, immune support
- **Sources:** Dairy, fortified plant milks, sunlight exposure (vitamin D)
- **Risk:** Indoor training, limited sun exposure, dairy-free diets
- **Monitor:** Bone density scans for at-risk players

### Magnesium

- **Role:** Muscle function, energy production, sleep quality
- **Sources:** Nuts, seeds, leafy greens, whole grains
- **Risk:** Heavy sweating depletes magnesium

### B Vitamins

- **Role:** Energy metabolism, red blood cell production
- **Sources:** Whole grains, meat, eggs, dairy
- **Risk:** Restrictive diets (vegan, low-carb)

## Supplementation

### Evidence-Based Supplements

| Supplement | Evidence | Dose | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Creatine Monohydrate | Strong | 3-5 g/day | Power, strength, recovery |
| Caffeine | Moderate-Strong | 2-6 mg/kg | Alertness, performance |
| Beta-Alanine | Moderate | 3-6 g/day | Buffering capacity |
| Vitamin D | Moderate | 1,000-4,000 IU/day | Bone health, immunity |
| Omega-3 Fatty Acids | Moderate | 1-3 g EPA/DHA | Anti-inflammatory, recovery |
| Whey Protein | Strong | 20-40 g/post-exercise | Muscle repair, recovery |

### Supplements with Limited Evidence

- BCAAs (if protein intake is adequate, BCAAs offer minimal benefit)
- Glutamine (limited performance benefit)
- Testosterone boosters (ineffective and potentially harmful)
- Fat burners (risky, may contain banned substances)

### Anti-Doping Considerations

- All supplements should be third-party tested (NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport)
- Players are responsible for what they consume
- Check all supplements against WADA prohibited list
- When in doubt, do not take it

## Practical Application: "The Match Day Nutrition Plan"

Structure match day nutrition with precise timing:

**Wake Up:**
- Drink 500 mL water immediately

**Breakfast (4 hours before match):**
- 80 g oats with banana and honey
- 2 scrambled eggs
- 300 mL orange juice
- 500 mL water

**Pre-Match Snack (90 minutes before):**
- Energy bar (200 calories, 30 g carbs)
- 300 mL sports drink

**Warm-Up:**
- 200 mL water with electrolytes

**During Match:**
- 200 mL sports drink at each changeover
- Half a banana at the set break (if applicable)

**Post-Match (within 30 minutes):**
- 500 mL chocolate milk
- Protein shake with banana

**Post-Match Meal (within 2 hours):**
- Grilled chicken, rice, vegetables
- Continued hydration

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Energy crash during matches | Insufficient pre-match carbohydrates, poor during-match fueling | Fatigue, reduced performance | Increase pre-match carbs; introduce during-match fueling |
| Slow recovery between days | Inadequate post-match nutrition, poor sleep | Cumulative fatigue, reduced training quality | Implement post-match recovery protocol; sleep optimization |
| Frequent muscle cramps | Dehydration, electrolyte imbalance, poor conditioning | Cramping, time loss | Hydration protocol; electrolyte supplementation; conditioning improvement |
| Weight loss during tournament | Insufficient caloric intake, high energy expenditure | Fatigue, performance decline | Increase caloric intake; portable high-energy snacks; meal planning |
