# ART-870: Elbow and Wrist Health in Tennis

## Executive Summary

The elbow and wrist are critical yet vulnerable joints in tennis, subjected to repetitive loading during serves, groundstrokes, and volleys. Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow) is the most common tennis-related condition, affecting up to 50% of recreational players and a significant proportion of professionals. Wrist injuries, including tendinopathy and TFCC tears, are also prevalent due to the complex wrist motions required for stroke production. Effective elbow and wrist health management involves a comprehensive prehabilitation program that addresses forearm strength, flexibility, and technique optimization to prevent and manage these common conditions.

## Elbow Biomechanics in Tennis

### Forces on the Elbow

During tennis strokes, the elbow experiences:
- **Valgus stress:** During the serve's acceleration phase
- **Extension force:** During groundstroke follow-through
- **Wrist extension force:** During backhand strokes
- **Forearm rotation:** During serves and topspin groundstrokes

### Common Elbow Injuries

| Injury | Cause | Symptoms |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lateral Epicondylitis (Tennis Elbow) | Repetitive wrist extension, backhand strokes | Pain on outer elbow, weak grip |
| Medial Epicondylitis (Golfer's Elbow) | Repetitive forearm pronation, serving | Pain on inner elbow |
| UCL Strain | Valgus stress during serving | Inner elbow pain, instability |
| Olecranon Stress Fracture | Repetitive extension force | Pain at tip of elbow |

## Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis)

### Causes

- Repetitive wrist extension during backhand strokes
- Gripping the racket too tightly
- Poor backhand technique (arming the ball)
- Insufficient forearm extensor strength
- Excessive string tension
- Racket too heavy or grip too small

### Symptoms

- Pain on the outer aspect of the elbow
- Weakness in grip strength
- Pain worsens with wrist extension and gripping
- Tenderness over the lateral epicondyle

### Prevention and Management

**Eccentric Wrist Extension:**
- Hold a light dumbbell (1-3 kg), palm down
- Use the other hand to lift the weight into wrist extension
- Slowly lower the weight (eccentric phase) over 3 seconds
- 3 sets of 15 reps, daily

**Wrist Extension Strengthening:**
- Hold a dumbbell, palm down
- Lift the weight into wrist extension
- 3 sets of 15 reps

**Reverse Wrist Curl:**
- Hold a dumbbell, palm up
- Lift the weight into wrist flexion
- 3 sets of 15 reps

**Forearm Extensor Stretch:**
- Extend the arm, palm down
- Push the hand down with the other hand
- Hold 30 seconds, 3 reps each arm

## Wrist Biomechanics in Tennis

### Wrist Motions in Tennis

| Stroke | Primary Wrist Motion |
| :--- | :--- |
| Serve | Extension and radial deviation (cocking), flexion and ulnar deviation (contact) |
| Forehand | Extension (backswing), flexion (contact), ulnar deviation (follow-through) |
| Backhand | Extension (both one-handed and two-handed) |
| Volley | Firm wrist, minimal motion |

### Common Wrist Injuries

| Injury | Cause | Symptoms |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wrist Extensor Tendinopathy | Repetitive wrist extension | Pain on outer wrist |
| Wrist Flexor Tendinopathy | Repetitive wrist flexion | Pain on inner wrist |
| TFCC Tear | Repetitive rotation and loading | Pain on outer wrist, clicking |
| De Quervain's Tenosynovitis | Repetitive thumb motion | Pain at base of thumb |

## Forearm Strengthening Program

### Extensor Strengthening

**Eccentric Wrist Extension:**
- 3 sets of 15 reps, daily
- Essential for tennis elbow prevention

**Wrist Extension (Concentric):**
- 3 sets of 15 reps
- Builds extensor strength

**Finger Extensions (Rubber Band):**
- Place a rubber band around the fingers
- Spread the fingers against the resistance
- 3 sets of 15 reps

### Flexor Strengthening

**Wrist Curl:**
- Hold a dumbbell, palm up
- Lift the weight into wrist flexion
- 3 sets of 15 reps

**Ball Squeeze:**
- Squeeze a tennis ball or stress ball
- Hold 5 seconds, 15 reps
- Builds grip strength

### Rotational Strengthening

**Forearm Pronation:**
- Hold a dumbbell or hammer
- Rotate the forearm to palm-down position
- 3 sets of 15 reps

**Forearm Supination:**
- Rotate the forearm to palm-up position
- 3 sets of 15 reps

## Equipment Considerations for Elbow and Wrist Health

### Racket Selection

| Factor | Recommendation | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Weight | Moderate (270-310g unstrung) | Too heavy increases joint loading |
| Balance | Head-light or even-balance | Reduces shock transmission |
| Grip Size | Correct fit (not too small) | Reduces gripping force needed |
| String Type | Softer strings (multifilament, natural gut) | Reduces vibration |
| String Tension | Lower end of recommended range | Reduces shock |

### Vibration Dampeners

- Reduce vibration transmission to the elbow and wrist
- Minimal effect on performance
- Recommended for all players

### Grips

- Use overgrips to maintain correct grip size
- Replace grips regularly (worn grips require tighter gripping)
- Consider cushioned grips for added comfort

## Practical Application: "The Elbow and Wrist Health Routine"

**Pre-Training/Match (5 minutes):**
- Wrist circles: 30 seconds each direction
- Forearm stretches: 30 seconds each position
- Ball squeezes: 10 reps
- Eccentric wrist extensions: 1 set of 10

**Post-Training/Match (10 minutes):**
- Forearm stretches: 30 seconds each position
- Eccentric wrist extensions: 2 sets of 15
- Wrist curls: 2 sets of 15
- Ice application if any discomfort

**Daily Maintenance:**
- Eccentric wrist extensions: 3 sets of 15
- Forearm stretches: 30 seconds each position
- Ball squeezes: 3 sets of 10

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Outer elbow pain (tennis elbow) | Weak forearm extensors, poor technique | Lateral epicondylitis | Eccentric wrist extension; technique review; equipment adjustment |
| Inner elbow pain (golfer's elbow) | Weak forearm flexors, excessive pronation | Medial epicondylitis | Forearm flexor strengthening; technique review; equipment adjustment |
| Wrist pain during backhand | Weak wrist extensors, excessive wrist use | Wrist tendinopathy | Extensor strengthening; technique review (less wrist, more body rotation) |
| Weak grip strength | Insufficient forearm strength | Reduced stroke control, racket stability | Ball squeezes; wrist curls; grip strength training |
| Clicking or catching in wrist | TFCC injury, ligament damage | Pain, instability | Medical assessment; wrist support; technique modification |
