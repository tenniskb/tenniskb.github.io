# ART-554: Returning the Slice Serve: Handling Lateral Movement
## Executive Summary

The slice serve curves laterally in flight and stays low after the bounce, making it one of the more challenging serves to return. This article covers the techniques for handling the lateral movement and low bounce of slice serves, along with tactical options for returning them effectively.

## The Challenge of Returning Slice Serves

### Lateral Curve
- Slice serves curve 2-6 feet laterally in flight
- The curve continues after the bounce, making the ball angle away from the returner
- Returners must adjust their positioning to account for the curve

### Low Bounce
- Slice serves stay lower than flat or kick serves after bouncing
- The returner must stay low and hit up on the ball
- The low bounce makes it difficult to attack aggressively

### Wide Angle
- Slice serves are often hit wide to pull the returner off the court
- The wide angle opens up the court for the server's next shot
- Returners must decide whether to block the ball back or attempt a more aggressive return

## Positioning for Slice Serves

### Adjust for the Curve
- Start slightly wider than normal to account for the lateral curve
- Be ready to move further to the side as the ball curves
- Watch the ball closely to judge the amount of curve

### Stay Low
- The low bounce requires a lower stance
- Bend your knees more than for flat or kick serves
- Hit the ball at knee to waist height

### Adjust for Court Surface
- **Grass:** Slice serves stay very low; stay extra low
- **Hard court:** Moderate low bounce; normal low stance
- **Clay:** The ball slows down and bounces slightly higher; can stand slightly taller

## Returning Wide Slice Serves

### The Block Return
For wide slice serves that pull you off the court:
1. Use a short, compact swing
2. Block the ball back crosscourt
3. Focus on getting the ball back in play
4. Recover to the center of the court

### The Drive Return
For wide slice serves that are slower:
1. Take a full swing
2. Drive the ball crosscourt or down the line
3. Aim deep to keep the server back
4. Take control of the point

### The Lob Return
For wide slice serves when you are off-balance:
1. Lob the ball over the server's head
2. Use the lob to recover your position
3. The lob buys time and neutralizes the serve

## Returning Body Slice Serves

### The Jam Block
When the slice serve jams your body:
1. Use a very compact swing
2. Block the ball back at the server or crosscourt
3. The server's pace does most of the work
4. Focus on getting the ball back in play

### The Forehand Jam
If the body serve jams your forehand:
1. Turn your upper body to create space
2. Use a short forehand swing
3. Aim crosscourt or at the server's feet

### The Backhand Jam
If the body serve jams your backhand:
1. Use a compact backhand block
2. Aim down the line or crosscourt
3. The shorter backhand swing is easier to execute when jammed

## Returning T Slice Serves

### The T Slice Advantage
T slice serves are the easiest slice serves to return because:
- They do not pull you off the court
- You have more time to set up
- You can use a full swing

### Returning the T Slice
1. Use a normal groundstroke swing
2. Step into the return
3. Drive the ball crosscourt or down the line
4. Take control of the point

## Tactical Considerations

### Crosscourt vs. Down the Line
- **Crosscourt:** Safer; more margin for error; keeps the ball in play
- **Down the line:** More aggressive; can catch the server off-guard; riskier

### Neutralizing vs. Attacking
- **Neutralize:** Block the ball back crosscourt; focus on getting the ball in play
- **Attack:** Drive the ball aggressively; take control of the point

The decision depends on:
- The quality of the serve (harder serves = more neutralizing)
- Your position (off-balance = more neutralizing)
- The match situation (behind = more attacking)

## Common Faults

### Reaching Too Far
Overextending to reach wide slice serves:
- Loss of balance
- Weak returns
- Difficulty recovering
- Fix: Stay closer to the center; use a shorter swing

### Not Staying Low
Standing too tall for the low bounce:
- Hitting into the net
- Mishits
- Fix: Lower your stance; bend your knees more

### Over-Swinging
Trying to hit a big return on a wide slice serve:
- Errors
- Loss of control
- Fix: Use a shorter swing; focus on getting the ball back in play

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cannot handle wide slice serves | Poor lateral movement | Pulled off court | Wide return drills; lateral movement exercises |
| Returns sail long on slice serves | Over-swinging | Errors long | Shorten swing; use blocking technique |
| Cannot handle low bounce | Not staying low | Returns into net | Low stance drills; bend knees more |
| Body serve jams you | Poor body serve return | Weak returns | Body serve blocking drills; compact swing |
| Difficulty judging slice curve | Poor anticipation | Misjudged positioning | Anticipation drills; watch the ball closely |

## Practical Application: "The Cone Return"

Place a cone 2 feet outside the singles sideline at the service line. Have a partner hit slice serves wide, aiming to curve the ball past the cone. Practice returning these serves, focusing on:
1. Starting wide enough to account for the curve
2. Staying low to handle the low bounce
3. Blocking the ball back crosscourt
4. Recovering to the center after the return

This drill develops the specific skills needed for returning wide slice serves.
