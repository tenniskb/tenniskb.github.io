# ART:056: Racket Vibration and Damping Effects

## Executive Summary
When the ball contacts the racket, the string bed vibrates at its fundamental frequency (typically 100-200 Hz). These vibrations are transmitted to the hand and arm, contributing to discomfort and potentially to overuse injuries such as tennis elbow. Understanding racket vibration and damping enables players to select equipment and techniques that minimize harmful vibrations while maintaining feedback and feel.

## Vibration Frequency and Amplitude

### Fundamental Frequency
- **Typical range**: 100-200 Hz
- **Factors affecting frequency**: String tension, string material, racket stiffness
- **Higher tension = higher frequency**
- **Lower tension = lower frequency**

### Vibration Amplitude
- **Depends on**: Impact location, ball speed, racket stiffness
- **Sweet spot hits**: Minimal vibration
- **Off-center hits**: Maximum vibration

## Vibration and Injury

### Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis)
- **Cause**: Repetitive vibration transmitted to the extensor tendons
- **Risk factors**: Off-center hits, stiff rackets, high string tension
- **Prevention**: Proper equipment, good technique, vibration dampeners

### Vibration White Finger
- **Cause**: Prolonged exposure to high-frequency vibration
- **Symptoms**: Numbness, tingling, loss of grip strength
- **Rare in tennis** but possible with high-vibration rackets

## Damping Technologies

### Built-in Dampening
- **Vibration dampeners**: Small rubber devices placed in the strings
- **Effect**: Reduce vibration amplitude by 10-30%
- **Trade-off**: Slightly reduced feel and feedback

### Handle Dampening
- **Grip materials**: Softer grips absorb more vibration
- **Overgrips**: Additional dampening layer
- **Effect**: Reduces vibration transmission to the hand

## Practical Application: "The Vibration Test"
Hold your racket by the handle and tap the strings with a hard object. Listen to the sound and feel the vibration. A dull thud indicates good damping; a high-pitched ping indicates more vibration. Choose equipment and techniques that minimize excessive vibration while maintaining the feedback you need for good stroke production.
