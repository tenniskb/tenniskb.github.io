# ART-553: Returning the Flat Serve: Reaction Time and Blocking
## Executive Summary

The flat serve is the fastest and most challenging serve to return. With less spin to help predict the bounce and maximum speed to contend with, returning flat serves requires specialized techniques. This article covers reaction time management, blocking strategies, and how to handle flat serves of varying speeds.

## The Challenge of Returning Flat Serves

### Speed Demands
- Elite flat serves travel 120-145 mph
- At these speeds, the ball reaches the returner in approximately 0.4-0.5 seconds
- The returner must react, move, and swing in under half a second

### Minimal Spin
- Flat serves have minimal spin (500-1500 RPM)
- The bounce is predictable but fast
- No Magnus effect to help the returner judge the trajectory
- The ball stays low and skids through the court

### Low Trajectory
- Flat serves have a flatter trajectory than spin serves
- The ball stays lower after the bounce
- Returners must stay low to handle the low bounce

## Reaction Time Strategies

### Anticipation
The most effective way to handle fast flat serves is to anticipate:
- **Read the toss:** A toss in front suggests a flat serve; a toss over the head suggests a kick serve
- **Read the server's body:** A more aggressive body motion suggests a flat serve
- **Read the pattern:** If the server has been hitting flat serves, expect another one

### Pre-Movement
Take a small step forward as the server begins their motion:
- This pre-loads the legs for forward movement
- It reduces the distance you must cover
- It takes time away from the serve

### Split Step Timing
The split step must be perfectly timed:
- Too early: You land and must reload
- Too late: You are flat-footed when the serve is struck
- Just right: You land and immediately push off

## Blocking Technique

### What Is Blocking?
Blocking is a short, compact return technique used for fast serves:
- Minimal backswing
- The racket acts as a "wall" to deflect the ball back
- Power comes from the server's pace, not the returner's swing

### Block Execution
1. Short backswing (racket comes back only to the body)
2. Firm wrist and forearm at contact
3. The racket face is open slightly to control the bounce
4. Minimal follow-through (just enough to guide the ball)

### When to Block
- Serves above 110 mph
- Serves that are body-high or above
- When you are caught off-balance
- When you need to neutralize a big server

### When Not to Block
- Serves below 90 mph (you have time for a full swing)
- Serves that are short or soft
- When you are well-positioned and balanced
- When you want to attack the return

## Driving the Flat Serve Return

### When to Drive
- When the serve is below 90 mph
- When you are well-positioned and balanced
- When you want to take control of the point
- When the serve is short and sitting up

### Drive Execution
1. Full backswing (normal groundstroke motion)
2. Step into the return with the drive step
3. Transfer weight forward through contact
4. Full follow-through in the direction of the target

### Target Selection
- **Crosscourt:** Safest option; most margin for error
- **Down the line:** More aggressive; requires precise timing
- **At the server's feet:** Effective if the server follows the serve to net

## Returning Flat Serves to Different Targets

### Wide Flat Serve
- Use a block or a short swing
- Aim crosscourt to neutralize the serve
- Do not try to do too much with a wide flat serve

### Body Flat Serve
- Use a block with minimal backswing
- Direct the ball back at the server or crosscourt
- The body serve jams you, so keep the swing compact

### T Flat Serve
- You have more time because the serve is down the middle
- Use a full swing to drive the return
- Aim crosscourt or down the line based on your positioning

## Common Faults

### Swinging Too Hard
Trying to match the server's pace with an aggressive swing:
- Results in errors
- The server's pace is enough — just redirect the ball
- Use a shorter, more controlled swing

### Standing Too Far Back
If you stand too far behind the baseline:
- The ball drops below the strike zone
- You must hit up on every return
- You lose the ability to take the ball early

### Not Staying Low
Flat serves stay low after the bounce:
- If you stand upright, you will hit the ball into the net
- Stay low with bent knees throughout the return
- Hit the ball at or below waist height

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Late on fast flat serves | Poor reaction time | Mishits and errors | Anticipation drills; pre-movement practice |
| Cannot block effectively | Incorrect blocking technique | Errors on big serves | Blocking drills with fast serves |
| Returns sail long | Swinging too hard | Unforced errors | Shorten swing; use blocking technique |
| Cannot handle body serves | Poor body serve return | Weak returns on body serves | Body serve blocking drills; compact swing practice |
| Stands too deep | Poor positioning | Difficult returns | Move closer to baseline; practice returning from inside |

## Practical Application: "The Blocking Wall"

Stand at the service line with a partner on the other side. Have the partner feed balls at you at increasing speeds, focusing on blocking them back with minimal backswing. Start at moderate speed and gradually increase. This drill develops the blocking technique needed for returning fast flat serves. The goal is to develop a "wall" with your racket that deflects the ball back without a big swing.
