# ART:077: Knee Leak Patterns and Their Correction

## Executive Summary
The knee is a critical link in the lower-extremity segment of the tennis kinetic chain. Knee leaks—including insufficient flexion during loading, valgus collapse during leg drive, and timing errors—can reduce force production and increase injury risk. This article presents the most common knee leak patterns and provides targeted corrective strategies.

## Common Knee Leak Patterns

### Insufficient Knee Flexion
- **Observation**: The knees bend less than 60° during loading
- **Cause**: Poor technique, weak quadriceps, fear of loading
- **Result**: Reduced elastic energy storage, lower force production
- **Correction**: Loading depth drills, quadriceps strengthening, technique correction

### Valgus Collapse
- **Observation**: The knees collapse inward (valgus) during leg drive and landing
- **Cause**: Weak hip abductors (gluteus medius), poor ankle mobility, weak quadriceps
- **Result**: Energy loss at the knee, increased ACL and meniscus injury risk
- **Correction**: Hip abductor strengthening, ankle mobility exercises, movement retraining

### Timing Errors
- **Observation**: The knee extends too early or too late relative to the loading phase
- **Cause**: Poor coordination, insufficient practice
- **Result**: Reduced force production, wasted energy
- **Correction**: Timing drills, sequential activation exercises

## Practical Application: "The Knee Check"
During loading, ensure your knees bend sufficiently (at least 60°) and track over your toes (no valgus collapse). If your knees are too straight, work on loading depth drills. If your knees collapse inward, work on hip abductor strength and ankle mobility. If your knee timing is off, work on sequential activation drills. The knee is the power generator of the lower body—proper knee mechanics are essential for powerful stroke production.
