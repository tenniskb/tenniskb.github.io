# ART:079: Core Leak Patterns and Their Correction

## Executive Summary
The core is the central link in the kinetic chain, connecting the lower and upper body. Core leaks—including insufficient rotation, poor stability, and timing disruptions—can compromise the entire chain. This article presents the most common core leak patterns and provides targeted corrective strategies.

## Common Core Leak Patterns

### Insufficient Core Rotation
- **Observation**: The trunk rotates less than optimal range during strokes
- **Cause**: Tight trunk muscles, weak rotators, poor mobility
- **Result**: Reduced power transfer from lower to upper body
- **Correction**: Trunk mobility exercises, rotational strength training

### Poor Core Stability
- **Observation**: The trunk collapses or wobbles during stroke production
- **Cause**: Weak deep stabilizers (transversus abdominis, multifidus), poor motor control
- **Result**: Energy loss at the core, compensatory movements
- **Correction**: Core stability training (planks, bird-dogs, dead bugs)

### Timing Disruptions
- **Observation**: The core rotates out of sequence with the hips and shoulders
- **Cause**: Poor coordination, insufficient practice
- **Result**: Disrupted kinetic chain, reduced power
- **Correction**: Timing drills, video feedback, sequential activation exercises

### Core Dominance Without Stability
- **Observation**: The player attempts to generate power from the core without adequate stability
- **Cause**: Misunderstanding of core function, weak stabilizers
- **Result**: Inefficient power generation, potential injury
- **Correction**: Balance stability and mobility training, technique correction

## Practical Application: "The Core Check"
During strokes, focus on feeling your core rotate fully and in sequence with your hips and shoulders. If your rotation is limited, work on trunk mobility. If your core feels unstable, work on deep stabilizer training. If your timing is off, work on sequential activation drills. The core is the power transfer center—a strong, stable, well-timed core ensures efficient force transmission from ground to racket.
