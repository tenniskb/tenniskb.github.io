# ART-447: Recovery and Preparation

## Executive Summary
Recovery and preparation are often overlooked but essential components of the backhand. The ability to quickly return to the ready position after a shot and prepare for the next ball determines the player's court coverage and consistency. Developing efficient recovery and preparation is a hallmark of an advanced player.

## The Ready Position
The ready position is the stance the player assumes between shots. It is a balanced, athletic position with the feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and weight on the balls of the feet. The racket is held in front of the body, with both hands on the grip (for a two-handed backhand) or the dominant hand on the grip and the non-dominant hand at the throat (for a one-handed backhand).

The ready position should be active, not passive. The player should be bouncing lightly on the feet, ready to move in any direction. This active stance allows quick reaction to the opponent's shot.

## The Split Step
The split step is a small hop that the player performs just before the opponent hits the ball. It loads the leg muscles and positions the feet for quick movement in any direction. The split step is the first step in preparation and is essential for court coverage.

Players who fail to split step are often slow to react to the opponent's shot. Developing the habit of splitting on every shot is essential for effective preparation.

## Recovery After the Backhand
After hitting the backhand, the player should immediately recover to the ready position. The recovery step should be quick and efficient, returning the player to a balanced stance. The recovery is particularly important after an aggressive shot, as the player may be off-balance or out of position.

Players who fail to recover after a shot often find themselves out of position for the next ball. Practicing the recovery step after each shot helps develop the habit of moving immediately.

## Preparation for the Next Shot
Preparation begins as the player recovers. The player should assess the opponent's position, the ball's trajectory, and their own court position to determine the appropriate response. This assessment should happen quickly, allowing the player to move to the ball and prepare the backhand.

Players who fail to prepare often find themselves rushing the swing, resulting in poor technique and inconsistent shots. Developing the habit of preparing for each shot is essential for a consistent backhand.

## Footwork Patterns for Recovery
Several footwork patterns are used for recovery. The "recovery step" involves taking a step back toward the center of the court after a shot. The "adjustment step" involves taking small steps to fine-tune the position. The "split step" involves hopping into the ready position before the opponent's shot.

Players should practice these footwork patterns during drills, developing the ability to move efficiently and recover quickly.

## The Role of the Non-Dominant Hand
On the one-handed backhand, the non-dominant hand plays a crucial role in recovery. After the shot, the non-dominant hand moves to the throat of the racket, allowing the dominant hand to relax and the player to prepare for the next shot. This transition should be quick and automatic.

Players who fail to use the non-dominant hand for recovery often struggle with grip changes and preparation. Developing the habit of moving the non-dominant hand to the throat after the shot is essential for efficient recovery.

## Common Recovery and Preparation Errors
One common error is failing to recover after a shot, resulting in being out of position for the next ball. Another is failing to split step, resulting in slow reaction. Some players also fail to prepare for the next shot, resulting in rushed swings and poor technique.

Players who struggle with recovery and preparation often benefit from drills that emphasize footwork and readiness. Practicing the split step, recovery step, and preparation helps develop the habits needed for effective court coverage.

## Drills for Recovery and Preparation
Several drills help develop recovery and preparation. The "shadow drill" involves practicing the backhand swing and then recovering to the ready position, focusing on speed and balance. The "footwork drill" involves practicing the split step, recovery step, and adjustment step, developing the movement patterns needed for court coverage. The "rally drill" involves rallying with a partner, focusing on recovering and preparing for each shot.

Players should incorporate these drills into their regular practice routine, focusing on developing efficient recovery and preparation.

| Out of position for next shot | Failing to recover after the shot | Weak, defensive shots | Practice recovery step; drill immediate movement |

## Practical Application: "The Recovery Habit"
During practice, after every backhand shot, immediately recover to the ready position and split step. Count how many times you are in the ready position before the opponent hits the ball. Track progress over sessions to ensure recovery and preparation are becoming automatic.
