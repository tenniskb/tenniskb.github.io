# ART:078: Ankle Leak Patterns and Their Correction

## Executive Summary
The ankle is the first link in the kinetic chain, where ground reaction forces enter the body. Ankle leaks—including insufficient plantarflexion, instability, and mobility restrictions—can reduce force production and compromise the entire chain above. This article presents the most common ankle leak patterns and provides targeted corrective strategies.

## Common Ankle Leak Patterns

### Insufficient Plantarflexion
- **Observation**: The ankle does not push off forcefully during leg drive
- **Cause**: Weak calf muscles (gastrocnemius, soleus), limited dorsiflexion mobility
- **Result**: Reduced vertical ground reaction force, lower power output
- **Correction**: Calf strengthening (raises, plyometrics), dorsiflexion mobility work

### Ankle Instability
- **Observation**: The ankle wobbles or rolls during ground contact
- **Cause**: Weak peroneals, poor proprioception, previous sprains
- **Result**: Energy loss at the ankle, increased sprain risk
- **Correction**: Balance training, peroneal strengthening, proprioceptive exercises

### Limited Dorsiflexion Mobility
- **Observation**: The ankle cannot dorsiflex sufficiently for deep knee flexion
- **Cause**: Tight calf muscles, joint restrictions
- **Result**: Compensatory movement patterns, reduced loading depth
- **Correction**: Calf stretching, joint mobilization, mobility drills

### Foot Pronation/Supination Issues
- **Observation**: The foot rolls excessively inward (pronation) or outward (supination)
- **Cause**: Weak foot intrinsics, poor footwear, structural issues
- **Result**: Altered force transmission, reduced stability
- **Correction**: Foot strengthening, orthotic assessment, footwear modification

## Practical Application: "The Ankle Check"
During loading and leg drive, ensure your ankles are stable and pushing off forcefully. If your push-off is weak, work on calf strength and plyometrics. If your ankles feel unstable, work on balance and peroneal strength. If you can't flex your knees deeply, work on dorsiflexion mobility. The ankle is where the chain begins—a strong, stable ankle sets the foundation for everything above.
