# ART-448: Backhand on the Rise

## Executive Summary
Hitting the backhand on the rise is an aggressive technique that involves meeting the ball as it is ascending after the bounce. This takes time away from the opponent and creates offensive opportunities. Mastering this technique requires precise timing, compact swing mechanics, and the ability to handle the ball's upward momentum.

## The Tactical Advantage of Hitting on the Rise
Hitting on the rise provides several tactical advantages. It takes time away from the opponent, reducing their ability to recover and prepare. It allows the player to hit the ball at a higher contact point, making it easier to hit down on the ball and produce a penetrating shot. It also disrupts the opponent's rhythm, as they must adjust to a faster-paced rally.

Players who master hitting on the rise can turn defensive situations into offensive ones, putting pressure on the opponent and creating opportunities to finish points.

## When to Hit on the Rise
Hitting on the rise is appropriate when the player is inside the baseline and facing a ball that is not too high or too fast. It is particularly effective against opponents who hit with heavy topspin, as the ball rises quickly and can be taken early. It is also useful against opponents who prefer to rally from behind the baseline, as it takes away their time.

Players should be cautious about hitting on the rise when the ball is too high or too fast, as this can lead to errors. Developing the judgment to know when to hit on the rise and when to let the ball drop is essential.

## Technique for Hitting on the Rise
Hitting on the rise requires a compact swing and precise timing. The player must move forward to meet the ball, taking it early in its ascent. The contact point is typically further forward than for a standard drive, often at chest to shoulder height.

The swing path is still low to high, but the racket must be accelerated quickly to meet the rising ball. The wrist lag and release are particularly important, as they add the speed needed to handle the ball's upward momentum.

## The Role of Footwork
Footwork is essential for hitting on the rise. The player must move forward aggressively to meet the ball early, taking it on the rise rather than waiting for it to drop. This requires quick adjustment steps and a commitment to moving forward.

Players who are hesitant to move forward often miss the opportunity to hit on the rise, allowing the ball to drop and losing the tactical advantage. Developing the habit of moving forward to take the ball early is essential.

## Common Errors When Hitting on the Rise
One common error is mistiming the swing, resulting in contacting the ball too early or too late. Another is using too large a backswing, resulting in a slow swing that cannot handle the rising ball. Some players also fail to move forward, resulting in a contact point that is too far back.

Players who struggle with hitting on the rise often benefit from drills that emphasize timing and compact swings. Practicing with a partner who feeds balls at different heights helps develop the timing needed for this technique.

## Drills for Hitting on the Rise
Several drills help develop the ability to hit on the rise. The "early contact drill" involves having a partner feed balls and practicing taking them as early as possible. The "rise drill" involves practicing hitting balls that are rising through the strike zone, focusing on timing and compact swings. The "pressure drill" involves rallying with a partner, focusing on taking the ball on the rise to create offense.

Players should incorporate these drills into their regular practice routine, focusing on developing the timing and technique needed for hitting on the rise.

## Practical Application: "The Early Take Drill"
Have a partner feed balls to your backhand from inside the baseline. Practice taking each ball as early as possible, focusing on moving forward and using a compact swing. Count how many shots you can hit on the rise that land in the court. Track progress over sessions to ensure the technique is developing.
