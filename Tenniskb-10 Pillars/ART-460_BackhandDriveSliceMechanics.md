# ART-460: Short Backhand Slice Approach

## Executive Summary
The short backhand slice approach is a transitional shot that allows the player to approach the net behind a low, skidding ball. It uses a Continental grip, a high-to-low swing path, and a slightly open racket face to produce backspin. Mastering this shot adds a powerful transitional weapon to the player's arsenal.

## The Purpose of the Short Slice Approach
The short backhand slice approach is used when the player is inside the baseline and wants to move forward to the net. The slice stays low and skids, forcing the opponent to hit up on the ball, which sets up the player for a volley or overhead.

The short slice approach is particularly effective on low-bouncing surfaces, such as grass, where the ball stays low and is difficult to attack. It is also useful against opponents who prefer pace, as the slice takes away their preferred rhythm.

## Technique for the Short Slice Approach
The short backhand slice approach uses a Continental grip, a high-to-low swing path, and a slightly open racket face. The player should take a short backswing, meeting the ball in front of the body with a firm wrist. The swing is more of a block than a full swing, with the racket descending through the ball.

The contact point is typically at waist to chest height, and the follow-through continues downward and forward, finishing around waist height. The player should move forward as the shot is hit, maintaining the momentum needed for the net approach.

## Body Position for the Short Slice Approach
The body position for the short slice approach reflects the forward momentum. The stance is typically open or neutral, with the weight transferring slightly forward through the swing. The player should be moving forward as the shot is hit, maintaining the momentum needed for the net approach.

Players should practice the body position for the short slice approach, ensuring that the rotation and weight transfer are appropriate for the shot. This develops the consistency needed for an effective approach.

## Tactical Considerations for the Short Slice Approach
The short slice approach should be tactical, with the player considering the opponent's position, the type of ball, and the court surface. In general, the player should aim for a low, skidding slice that lands in the service box, forcing the opponent to hit up on the ball.

Players should also look for opportunities to vary the approach, hitting a drive or a flat shot to keep the opponent guessing. However, they should be cautious about varying the approach too much, as this can lead to errors.

## Common Short Slice Approach Errors
One common error is hitting the slice too high, resulting in a ball that sits up for the opponent. Another is failing to move forward, resulting in a loss of momentum. Some players also fail to produce enough backspin, resulting in a ball that does not skid.

Players who struggle with the short slice approach often benefit from drills that emphasize the low trajectory and backspin. Practicing the shot with a focus on these elements helps develop the technique needed for an effective approach.

## Drills for the Short Slice Approach
Several drills help develop the short slice approach. The "approach drill" involves having a partner feed short balls, practicing hitting a short slice and moving forward to the net. The "low trajectory drill" involves practicing the low, skidding bounce, focusing on keeping the ball below net height. The "backspin drill" involves practicing the heavy backspin, focusing on making the ball skid after the bounce.

Players should incorporate these drills into their regular practice routine, focusing on developing the technique needed for an effective short slice approach.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix
| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Slice too high | Racket face too open or swing path too steep | Easy for opponent to attack | Reduce open face; shallow descent |
| Slice lacks backspin | Swing speed too slow or contact too far back | Ball does not skid | Accelerate through contact; move contact forward |
| Cannot approach | Not moving forward after the shot | Caught in no-man's land | Practice transition drill; focus on forward movement |
| Slice lands too deep | Swing speed too slow or follow-through too short | Ball dies at opponent's feet | Increase speed; extend follow-through |
| Slice too low | Racket face too closed or descent too steep | Predictable, easy to attack | Open face more; steepen descent |

## Practical Application: "The Approach Slice"
Have a partner feed short balls to your backhand side. Practice hitting short slice approaches and immediately moving forward to the net. Focus on producing a low, skidding slice that lands in the service box and forces the opponent to hit up on the ball. Track progress over sessions to ensure the approach is developing correctly.
