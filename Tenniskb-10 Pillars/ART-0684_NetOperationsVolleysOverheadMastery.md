# ART-0684: The Volley Against the Return with Extreme Pace — Handling Maximum Power

## Executive Summary

Returns hit with maximum pace are the most challenging for net players because the ball arrives in under 300 milliseconds, leaving minimal time for reaction. When you are the net player facing an extreme-pace return, you must use the block volley — absorb pace, redirect without swinging, and aim for the open court. The extreme-pace return is the ultimate test of the net player's reflexes and block volley technique.

## The Extreme-Pace Return

The extreme-pace return: (1) is hit with maximum power, (2) arrives at the net in under 300 milliseconds, (3) is hit with minimal spin (flat, fast). At the net, the extreme-pace return gives you almost no time to react — you must rely on the block volley and pure reflexes.

## Technique for Handling Extreme-Pace Returns

Against extreme-pace returns: (1) hold the racket in front of your body at chest height, (2) use the block volley (no swing — just absorb and redirect), (3) use a firm wrist (7-8/10 grip pressure), (4) redirect cross-court or down-the-middle, (5) aim for depth. The key is to absorb pace and redirect without adding your own swing.

## Common Errors

Swinging at the ball (no time for a swing). Holding the racket too low (slow reaction). Using a soft grip (the ball deflects the racket). Not using the block volley. Trying for winners (errors).

## Summary

Extreme-pace returns demand the block volley: hold the racket in front, absorb pace, redirect without swinging. The block volley is your only option against maximum power.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Swings at extreme-pace returns | No block technique | Late contact | Use block volley, no swing |
| Racket not in front | Holding racket too low | Slow reaction | Maintain "racket in front" position |
| Wrist breaks on extreme pace | Grip too soft | Erratic direction | Firm wrist, grip pressure 7-8/10 |
| Tries for winners on pace | Over-aggression | Errors | Focus on redirection, not winners |

## Practical Application: "Racket in Front, Block, Redirect"

Against extreme-pace returns: (1) hold the racket in front, (2) block the ball, (3) redirect to safety. The block volley is your only option against maximum power.
