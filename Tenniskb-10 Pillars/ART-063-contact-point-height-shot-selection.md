# ART:063: Contact Point Height and Its Effect on Shot Selection

## Executive Summary
The height at which the ball is contacted relative to the body is a critical determinant of shot selection and effectiveness. Contact above the waist enables aggressive, downward shots; contact at the waist produces neutral, flat shots; contact below the waist requires upward force production and limits aggressive options. Elite players adjust their positioning to contact the ball at optimal heights, maximizing their tactical possibilities.

## Contact Height Zones

### Shoulder Height and Above (Strike Zone 1)
- **Options**: Aggressive flat drives, downward angles, maximum power
- **Advantage**: Can hit down on the ball, creating sharp angles
- **Best for**: Serve returns, approach shots, offensive groundstrokes

### Waist to Shoulder Height (Strike Zone 2)
- **Options**: All shot types—flat, topspin, slice
- **Advantage**: Maximum versatility and power
- **Best for**: Standard groundstrokes from the baseline

### Below Waist (Strike Zone 3)
- **Options**: Defensive shots, heavy topspin, slices
- **Disadvantage**: Must lift the ball, limiting pace and angles
- **Best for**: Defensive shots, returns of high balls

## Practical Application: "The Strike Zone"
Aim to contact the ball in your optimal strike zone—between waist and shoulder height—where you have maximum shot options and power. If the ball is too high, take a step back to let it drop into your strike zone. If the ball is too low, bend your knees to lower your body rather than reaching down with your arm. Maintaining contact in your strike zone is key to consistent, powerful stroke production.
