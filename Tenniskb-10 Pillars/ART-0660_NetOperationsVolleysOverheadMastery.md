# ART-660: The Volley Against the Pusher — Beating Defensive Players at the Net

## Executive Summary

The "pusher" is a defensive player who relies on consistency, soft shots, and retrieving everything rather than hitting winners. Pushers are frustrating for net players because they do not hit the hard passing shots that net players are trained to volley — instead, they hit soft, floating balls that require a different approach. Beating pushers at the net requires patience, varied shot selection, and the ability to handle soft, low-bouncing balls.

## The Pusher's Game

A pusher: (1) hits soft, floating groundstrokes with minimal pace, (2) rarely hits winners — relies on opponents' errors, (3) lobs frequently (the defensive lob is a primary weapon), (4) moves well and retrieves everything, (5) frustrates opponents into over-hitting. The pusher's goal is to make you miss, not to hit winners themselves.

## The Challenge for Net Players

Pushers challenge net players because: (1) their soft shots require you to generate your own pace (the ball does not bounce off the racket with authority), (2) their frequent lobs force you to retreat from the net, (3) their consistency means you must hit multiple volleys to win the point, (4) their soft, low-bouncing balls are difficult to volley with power. The net player must adjust their technique to handle these soft, floating balls.

## Volley Adjustments Against Pushers

Against pushers: (1) generate your own pace on volleys — the ball will not come off the racket with authority, (2) use more punch volleys (the punch adds pace that the soft ball lacks), (3) use drop volleys frequently (pushers are often deep, making drop volleys effective), (4) use the lob volley to exploit their deep positioning, (5) be prepared for multiple volleys — pushers retrieve everything.

## Tactical Application Against Pushers

Against pushers: (1) approach the net frequently — pushers struggle to hit aggressive passing shots, (2) use varied shot selection (mix punch volleys, drop volleys, and lob volleys), (3) be patient — you may need to hit 3-4 volleys to win the point, (4) target their feet on volleys (pushers struggle with low balls), (5) do not over-hit — pushers thrive on opponents' errors. The key is consistency and patience — do not try to hit winners on every volley.

## Common Errors Against Pushers

Over-hitting (trying for winners on every volley). Frustration (pushers are designed to frustrate). Not varying shot selection (becomes predictable). Not using drop volleys enough (missed opportunities). Not being patient enough to hit multiple volleys.

## Summary

Beating pushers at the net requires patience, varied shot selection, and the ability to generate your own pace on volleys. Use punch volleys for pace, drop volleys for angles, and lob volleys to exploit their deep positioning. The key is consistency — do not let the pusher frustrate you into errors.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Over-hits against pushers | Impatience, trying for winners | Errors | Focus on consistency, not winners |
| Cannot generate pace on volleys | Relying on opponent's pace | Weak volleys | Use punch volley, generate own pace |
| Frustrated by pusher's retrieving | Poor mental approach | Loss of focus | Be patient, accept multiple volleys |
| Predictable shot selection | Using same volley every time | Pusher adjusts | Mix punch, drop, and lob volleys |
| Drop volleys too deep | Insufficient touch | Attacking ball for pusher | Practice drop volley touch |

## Practical Application: "Punch, Drop, Lob — Repeat"

Against pushers: (1) punch volleys to generate pace, (2) drop volleys to bring them in, (3) lob volleys to push them back. Vary your shot selection and be patient — the pusher wants you to make errors. Do not give them the satisfaction.
