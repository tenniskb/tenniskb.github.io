# ART-0677: The Volley Against the Return Overhead — Handling Lobs Over the Net Player

## Executive Summary

Lobs hit over the net player are a standard defensive tactic in doubles — the returner lobs the ball over the net player's head, forcing a retreat and an overhead. When you are the net player facing a lob over your head, you must use the drop-step, retreat efficiently, and hit a reliable overhead smash. The lob over the net player is the most common way to neutralize an aggressive net player.

## The Lob Over the Net Player

The lob over the net player: (1) is hit high and deep, (2) lands near the baseline, (3) forces the net player to retreat for an overhead. At the net, the lob over your head is a defensive shot by the returner — their goal is to push you back and buy time to recover.

## Technique for Handling Lobs Over the Net Player

Against lobs over the net player: (1) drop-step immediately, (2) retreat with crossover steps, (3) track the ball with your non-dominant arm, (4) position yourself under the ball, (5) hit a full overhead smash. The key is early recognition and efficient backward movement.

## Common Errors

Not drop-stepping (slow start on retreat). Not tracking the ball (poor overhead contact). Rushing the overhead (errors). Not communicating with your partner (confusion about who takes the overhead).

## Summary

Lobs over the net player demand the overhead smash: drop-step, retreat, track the ball, and smash. Practice the overhead until it is automatic.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Slow retreat on lob | No drop-step | Late overhead | Drop-step immediately |
| Misses overhead | Poor tracking | Errors | Point non-dominant arm at ball |
| Rushes overhead | Impatience | Errors | Take your time, get in position |
| No communication | Confusion | Both/none take overhead | Call "mine/yours" for lobs |

## Practical Application: "Drop-Step, Track, Smash"

Against lobs over the net player: (1) drop-step, (2) track the ball with your non-dominant arm, (3) smash with a full overhead motion. Early recognition and efficient retreat are the keys.
