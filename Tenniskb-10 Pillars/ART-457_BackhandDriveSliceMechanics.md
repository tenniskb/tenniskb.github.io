# ART-457: Backhand Crosscourt Patterns

## Executive Summary
The crosscourt backhand is the safest and most common shot in tennis, providing the largest margin over the net and into the court. Crosscourt patterns are used to control rallies, move the opponent, and create opportunities to attack. Mastering crosscourt play is essential for a consistent, effective backhand game.

## The Geometry of the Crosscourt Shot
The crosscourt shot travels diagonally across the court, from one corner to the opposite corner. This diagonal trajectory provides the largest margin over the net and into the court, as the ball travels over the lower part of the net and has a longer distance to land in the court.

The crosscourt shot is also easier to control, as the diagonal trajectory allows the player to hit with more pace while still keeping the ball in the court. This makes it the default shot for most rally situations.

## Shot Selection for Crosscourt Patterns
The crosscourt backhand can be hit with a variety of spins and trajectories. The default is a topspin drive, which provides margin and heavy bounce. The slice can be used to change the pace and approach the net. The flat drive is effective for finishing points when inside the baseline.

Players should vary the crosscourt shot based on the tactical situation, using topspin for consistency, slice for variety, and flat drives for offense.

## Crosscourt Patterns for Rally Control
Several crosscourt patterns are used to control rallies. The "crosscourt rally" involves hitting crosscourt to the open court, the safest shot with the largest margin. The "crosscourt angle" involves hitting crosscourt with a sharper angle, sending the ball closer to the sideline and forcing the opponent to cover more ground. The "crosscourt depth" involves hitting crosscourt with more depth, pushing the opponent back and preventing them from attacking.

Players should practice these patterns during drills, developing the ability to execute them under pressure.

## Crosscourt Patterns for Creating Opportunities
Crosscourt patterns can also be used to create opportunities to attack. The player can hit a series of deep crosscourt shots to push the opponent back, then look for a shorter ball to attack down the line or inside-out. The player can also use the crosscourt angle to open up the court, creating space for the next shot.

Players should practice these patterns during drills, developing the ability to construct points and create opportunities.

## Common Crosscourt Errors
One common error is hitting the ball too short, allowing the opponent to attack. Another is failing to vary the shot, making the rally predictable. Some players also fail to use the crosscourt angle, resulting in a passive rally that allows the opponent to control the point.

Players who struggle with crosscourt patterns often benefit from drills that emphasize depth, angle, and variation. Practicing the patterns with a focus on constructing the point helps develop the tactical awareness needed for success.

## Drills for Crosscourt Patterns
Several drills help develop crosscourt patterns. The "crosscourt rally" involves rallying with a partner, focusing on consistency and depth. The "angle drill" involves practicing hitting crosscourt with a sharper angle, focusing on placing the ball near the sideline. The "variation drill" involves practicing varying the crosscourt shot, using topspin, slice, and flat drives.

Players should incorporate these drills into their regular practice routine, focusing on developing the skills needed for effective crosscourt play.

## Practical Application: "The Crosscourt Practice"
Have a partner rally with you from the baseline. Practice hitting crosscourt with a variety of spins and trajectories, focusing on controlling the rally and creating opportunities. Track how many points you win with crosscourt patterns. This drill develops the tactical awareness needed for effective crosscourt play.
