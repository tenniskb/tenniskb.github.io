# ART:076: Wrist Leak Patterns and Their Correction

## Executive Summary
The wrist is the final link in the tennis kinetic chain, and its stability at contact is essential for efficient energy transfer to the ball. Wrist leaks—including insufficient stability, excessive movement, and compensatory patterns—can reduce ball speed, compromise directional accuracy, and increase injury risk. This article presents the most common wrist leak patterns and provides targeted corrective strategies.

## Common Wrist Leak Patterns

### Insufficient Wrist Stability
- **Observation**: The wrist collapses or wobbles at contact
- **Cause**: Weak grip strength, poor wrist stabilization, fatigue
- **Result**: Energy loss at the wrist, reduced ball speed and accuracy
- **Correction**: Grip strengthening, wrist stability exercises, technique correction

### Excessive Wrist Movement
- **Observation**: The wrist moves too much during the swing, reducing control
- **Cause**: Over-reliance on wrist snap, poor proximal segment contribution
- **Result**: Inconsistent contact, directional errors
- **Correction**: Technique correction, proximal segment emphasis, wrist stability training

### Wrist Dominance
- **Observation**: The wrist compensates for weak proximal segments by overworking
- **Cause**: Arm-dominant technique, weak trunk rotation
- **Result**: Wrist overuse, injury risk, reduced power
- **Correction**: Core strengthening, technique correction, proximal segment emphasis

## Practical Application: "The Wrist Check"
At contact, your wrist should be firm and stable—not floppy or collapsing. If your wrist is unstable, work on grip strength and wrist stability exercises. If your wrist is moving too much, focus on using your proximal segments (legs, hips, trunk, shoulder) to generate power rather than relying on your wrist. The wrist's job is to deliver the energy generated by the rest of the chain, not to generate energy itself.
