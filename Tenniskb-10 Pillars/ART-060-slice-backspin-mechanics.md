# ART:060: Slice and Backspin Mechanics

## Executive Summary
Slice and backspin are generated by a high-to-low racket path combined with an open face angle. The racket brushes down the back of the ball, creating backward rotation that causes the ball to float in flight and skid low after the bounce. Slice is used defensively (to buy time), offensively (to approach the net), and as a variation (to disrupt the opponent's rhythm). Understanding slice mechanics enables players to use this versatile shot effectively in all phases of play.

## Racket Path for Slice

### High-to-Low Trajectory
- **Starting position**: Racket above the ball
- **Ending position**: Racket below the ball
- **Vertical component**: The greater the vertical component, the more backspin

### Optimal High-to-Low Angle
- **Moderate slice**: 20-35° high-to-low
- **Heavy slice**: 35-55° high-to-low
- **Drop shot**: 55-80° high-to-low (minimal forward component)

## Face Angle for Slice

### Open Face
- **Slice requires an open face** (pointing upward) at contact
- **The open face, combined with the high-to-low path, creates the downward brushing action**
- **Face angle**: 10-20° open for moderate slice, 20-35° open for heavy slice

## Practical Application: "The Chop"
The slice is essentially a chopping motion: the racket comes down on the ball from high to low, with an open face. Practice this by holding your racket at shoulder height and chopping down through the ball, brushing the back of the ball with an open face. The more you chop down, the more backspin you generate. Use slice to buy time when defensive, to approach the net, or to hit a low-bouncing ball that's difficult for the opponent to attack.
