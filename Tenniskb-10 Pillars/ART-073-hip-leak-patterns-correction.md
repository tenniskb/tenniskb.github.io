# ART:073: Hip Leak Patterns and Their Correction

## Executive Summary
The hip joint is a critical link in the tennis kinetic chain, connecting the leg drive to the trunk rotation. Hip leaks—including insufficient rotation, early rotation, late rotation, and hip instability—are among the most common causes of power loss in tennis. This article presents the most common hip leak patterns and provides targeted corrective strategies for each.

## Common Hip Leak Patterns

### Insufficient Hip Rotation
- **Observation**: The hips rotate less than 30° during groundstrokes
- **Cause**: Tight hip muscles, poor mobility, weak hip rotators
- **Result**: Reduced momentum for trunk rotation, lower power output
- **Correction**: Hip mobility exercises, hip rotator strengthening, rotation drills

### Early Hip Rotation
- **Observation**: The hips begin rotating before the leg drive is complete
- **Cause**: Rushing the stroke, poor coordination
- **Result**: Reduced ground reaction force contribution, wasted energy
- **Correction**: Pause-at-leg-drive drills, sequential timing exercises

### Late Hip Rotation
- **Observation**: The hips begin rotating after the trunk has started
- **Cause**: Poor hip-trunk coordination, weak hip rotators
- **Result**: Disrupted kinetic chain sequence, reduced power
- **Correction**: Hip-initiation drills, coordination exercises

### Hip Instability
- **Observation**: The pelvis drops or tilts during rotation
- **Cause**: Weak hip stabilizers (gluteus medius), poor core control
- **Result**: Energy loss at the hip, compensatory movements
- **Correction**: Hip stabilizer strengthening, core stability training

## Practical Application: "The Hip Check"
During groundstrokes, focus on feeling your hips rotate fully and at the right time. If you feel your hips are "stuck" or not rotating enough, work on hip mobility and rotator strength. If you feel your hips are rotating too early or too late, work on timing drills. If you feel your pelvis dropping or tilting during rotation, work on hip stabilizer strengthening. The hip is the power link—fix hip leaks and your entire kinetic chain becomes more powerful.
