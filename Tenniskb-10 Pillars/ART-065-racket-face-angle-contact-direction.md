# ART:065: Racket Face Angle at Contact and Directional Control

## Executive Summary
The racket face angle at the moment of ball contact is the primary determinant of ball direction. A face angled to the right sends the ball right; a face angled left sends the ball left; a face angled up sends the ball up; a face angled down sends the ball down. Elite players control the face angle through grip, wrist position, and forearm rotation, achieving consistent directional accuracy even under pressure. Understanding face angle control is essential for developing reliable shot placement.

## Face Angle Determinants

### Grip
- **The grip determines the default face angle**
- **Eastern grip**: Slightly open face
- **Semi-western grip**: More closed face
- **Western grip**: Most closed face
- **Adjusting grip changes the default face angle**

### Wrist Position
- **Wrist extension**: Opens the face
- **Wrist flexion**: Closes the face
- **Wrist neutrality**: Square face

### Forearm Rotation
- **Supination**: Opens the face
- **Pronation**: Closes the face
- **The forearm rotation at contact fine-tunes the face angle**

## Practical Application: "The Face Control"
To control direction, control the face angle at contact. For a cross-court shot, angle the face slightly toward the target. For a down-the-line shot, angle the face slightly toward the target. Practice by aiming at specific targets and adjusting your face angle until you can consistently hit your target. The key is to maintain a stable face through contact—any wobbling or inconsistency in face angle leads to directional errors.
