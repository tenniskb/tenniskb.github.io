# ART-0676: The Volley Against the Return at the Feet — Handling Low Returns

## Executive Summary

Returns hit at the net player's feet are among the most difficult to handle because the ball arrives low, forcing a half-volley or a low volley with minimal reaction time. When you are the net player facing a return at your feet, you must get low quickly, use a firm wrist, and redirect the ball to safety. The return at the feet is a deliberate tactic used to neutralize the net player.

## The Return at the Feet

The return at the feet: (1) is hit low and short, landing near the net player's feet, (2) forces a low volley or half-volley, (3) is difficult to hit aggressively. At the net, the return at the feet is one of the most challenging situations because the low bounce forces extreme knee bend and a compact swing.

## Volley Technique Against Returns at the Feet

Against returns at the feet: (1) bend your knees deeply to get low, (2) use the half-volley technique (contact immediately after the bounce), (3) use a firm wrist to resist the ball's pace, (4) open the racket face slightly to lift the ball over the net, (5) aim for depth. The key is to get low and keep the ball in play.

## Common Errors

Standing upright (cannot reach the low ball). Breaking the wrist (loss of control). Swinging too hard (errors). Not getting low enough (frame shots). Not using the half-volley technique.

## Summary

Returns at the feet demand the half-volley: get low, open the face, redirect to safety. The goal is to keep the ball in play, not to hit a winner.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cannot reach low returns | Not getting low enough | Missed volleys | Bend knees deeply |
| Half-volley goes long | Face too open | Ball past baseline | Close face slightly, firm wrist |
| Half-volley into net | Face too closed, no lift | Net violation | Open face slightly, add lift |
| Wrist breaks on half-volley | Grip too soft | Erratic direction | Firm wrist, increase grip pressure |

## Practical Application: "Get Low, Open Up, Push Through"

Against returns at the feet: (1) get low with deep knee bend, (2) open the racket face slightly, (3) push through the ball with a firm wrist. Keep the ball in play.
