# ART:095: Reactive Strength Development for Tennis

## Executive Summary
Reactive strength—the ability to rapidly transition from eccentric (lengthening) to concentric (shortening) muscle actions—is essential for tennis. It enables the explosive first step after a split-step, the powerful leg drive of a serve, and the rapid directional changes required for court coverage. Reactive strength is developed through plyometric training that emphasizes minimal ground contact time and maximum explosive effort.

## Key Reactive Strength Exercises

### Depth Jumps
- Drop from a box (30-60 cm) and immediately jump vertically
- Emphasize minimal ground contact time (<200 ms)
- Develops the stretch-shortening cycle for the lower extremities

### Bounding
- Alternate single-leg jumps with emphasis on distance and height
- Develops unilateral reactive strength
- Mimics the single-leg push-off of tennis movement

### Reactive Hops
- Hop on one foot repeatedly with minimal ground contact
- Develops ankle and foot reactive strength
- Mimics the repetitive ground contacts of tennis movement

### Split-Step to Jump
- Perform a split-step and immediately jump vertically
- Develops the reactive transition from landing to takeoff
- Mimics the split-step to first step transition in tennis

## Practical Application: "The Contact Time Challenge"
Time your ground contact during plyometric exercises using a stopwatch or contact mat. Aim for contact times of less than 200 milliseconds. If your contact times are longer, you are not utilizing the stretch-shortening cycle effectively. Focus on "bouncing" off the ground rather than "pushing" off—the difference is the reactive strength that the stretch-shortening cycle provides.
