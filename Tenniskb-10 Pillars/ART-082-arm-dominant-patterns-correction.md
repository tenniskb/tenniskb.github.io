# ART:082: Arm-Dominant Patterns and Their Correction

## Executive Summary
Arm-dominant stroke patterns—where the arm generates most of the racket head speed rather than the full kinetic chain—are the most common power-limiting fault in tennis. These patterns reduce ball speed by 30-50%, increase injury risk to the shoulder and elbow, and cause inconsistency under pressure. Correcting arm-dominant patterns requires rebuilding the stroke from the ground up, emphasizing proximal-to-distal sequencing.

## Identifying Arm-Dominant Patterns

### Observations
- Minimal leg drive during strokes
- Insufficient hip and trunk rotation
- Excessive shoulder and arm effort
- Short, punching swing paths
- Inconsistent ball speed and direction
- Fatigue in the arm and shoulder during play

### Consequences
- **Power loss**: The arm alone cannot generate the racket speed of the full kinetic chain
- **Injury risk**: The shoulder and elbow absorb forces they are not designed to handle
- **Inconsistency**: Arm-dominated strokes are more variable than full-chain strokes

## Corrective Strategies

### Ground-Up Rebuild
- Start with leg drive drills without a racket
- Add hip rotation drills
- Add trunk rotation drills
- Add shoulder rotation drills
- Add arm acceleration drills
- Finally, integrate all segments with a racket

### Resistance Training
- Use resistance bands to force proximal segment activation
- The resistance cannot be overcome by the arm alone
- This trains the legs, hips, and trunk to contribute

### Video Feedback
- Record strokes and compare to elite models
- Identify where the arm is overworking
- Make corrections based on feedback

## Practical Application: "The 70/30 Rule"
In an optimal stroke, the lower body and trunk generate approximately 70% of the racket head speed, and the arm generates approximately 30%. If your arm is generating more than 30%, you are arm-dominant. Focus on increasing the contribution of your legs, hips, and trunk until the ratio approaches 70/30.
