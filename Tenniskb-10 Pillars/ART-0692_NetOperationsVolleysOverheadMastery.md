# ART-0692: The Volley Against the Return with Low Height — Handling Knee-to-Ankle Returns

## Executive Summary

Returns hit at knee-to-ankle height are among the most challenging for net players because the low contact point forces extreme knee bend and an open racket face. When you are the net player facing a low return, you must get very low, open the racket face, and use the low volley technique to lift the ball over the net. Low returns are a common tactic to neutralize aggressive net players.

## The Low Return

The low return: (1) is hit at knee-to-ankle height, (2) forces extreme knee bend from the net player, (3) is hit with slice backspin that keeps the ball low. At the net, the low return is one of the most difficult volley situations because the low bounce forces contact below net height.

## Technique for Handling Low Returns

Against low returns: (1) bend your knees deeply to get very low, (2) open the racket face more than usual, (3) use the low volley technique (absorb pace, lift over the net), (4) aim for depth, (5) use a firm wrist. The key is getting very low and opening the face to lift the ball over the net.

## Common Errors

Standing upright (cannot reach the low ball). Closing the face (ball goes into the net). Not using the low volley technique. Not getting low enough (frame shots).

## Summary

Low returns demand the low volley technique: deep knee bend, open racket face, lift over the net. Get low, open up, and lift.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Volley into net | Standing upright, face closed | Net violation | Bend knees deeply, open face |
| Cannot reach low returns | Poor knee bend | Missed volleys | Get lower, bend knees deeper |
| Wrist breaks on low returns | Grip too soft | Erratic direction | Firm wrist, increase grip pressure |
| Volley pops up | Face too open, no forward drive | Attacking ball for opponent | Reduce face angle, add forward drive |

## Practical Application: "Get Very Low, Open Up, Lift Over"

Against low returns: (1) get very low, (2) open the racket face, (3) lift the ball over the net. The lower you get, the better your low volley.
