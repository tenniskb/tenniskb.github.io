# ART:069: Follow-Through Mechanics and Deceleration Patterns

## Executive Summary
The follow-through is not merely a cosmetic finish—it serves critical biomechanical functions including energy dissipation, arm protection, and stroke quality feedback. After ball contact, the racket and arm must decelerate from peak speed to zero, and the follow-through extends this deceleration period to reduce peak forces on the arm. A proper follow-through also provides visual and proprioceptive feedback on stroke quality, enabling players to self-correct and refine their technique.

## Functions of the Follow-Through

### Energy Dissipation
- After ball contact, the racket and arm still have significant kinetic energy
- The follow-through extends the deceleration period, reducing peak forces
- A longer deceleration period = lower peak forces = reduced injury risk

### Arm Protection
- The muscles of the arm must absorb the kinetic energy of the racket
- A proper follow-through distributes this absorption across multiple muscles and joints
- An abrupt stop after contact concentrates forces on specific tissues, increasing injury risk

### Stroke Feedback
- The path of the follow-through reflects the path of the racket through contact
- A high follow-through indicates a low-to-high path (topspin)
- A low follow-through indicates a high-to-low path (slice)
- An across-body follow-through indicates a flat or slightly topspin path

## Practical Application: "The Finish"
Don't cut your follow-through short—let it extend naturally after contact. A full, relaxed follow-through protects your arm and provides feedback on your stroke quality. If your follow-through feels forced or abrupt, you may be decelerating too quickly, which increases injury risk. Practice by performing groundstrokes and focusing on a smooth, relaxed follow-through that extends naturally to its finish position.
