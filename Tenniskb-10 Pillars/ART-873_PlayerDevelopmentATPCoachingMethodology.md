# ART-873: Hip Mobility and Stability for Tennis

## Executive Summary

The hip joint is central to tennis performance, serving as the primary link in the kinetic chain between the legs and trunk. Hip mobility allows full range of motion for stroke production and movement, while hip stability ensures efficient force transfer and injury prevention. Tennis players frequently develop hip flexor tightness from the ready position, gluteal inhibition from prolonged sitting, and hip joint restrictions from repetitive rotational movements. A comprehensive hip program addresses mobility restrictions, activates dormant gluteal muscles, and develops the rotational stability essential for powerful, efficient stroke production.

## Hip Biomechanics in Tennis

### Hip Range of Motion Demands

| Movement | Requirement | Consequence of Restriction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Internal Rotation | 35+ degrees | Reduced serve and groundstroke power |
| External Rotation | 45+ degrees | Limited backhand rotation, compensatory lumbar rotation |
| Flexion | 120+ degrees | Reduced court coverage, low ready position difficulty |
| Extension | 20+ degrees | Reduced serve leg drive, compensatory lumbar hyperextension |
| Abduction | 40+ degrees | Limited lateral movement, reduced court coverage |

### Common Hip Issues in Tennis

| Issue | Cause | Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hip Flexor Tightness | Prolonged ready position, sitting | Reduced hip extension, compensatory lumbar extension |
| Gluteal Inhibition | Prolonged sitting, poor activation | Reduced power, compensatory hamstring/lower back use |
| Hip Joint Restrictions | Repetitive rotation, capsular tightness | Reduced stroke power, compensatory movement |
| Labral Tears | Repetitive rotation with loading | Pain, clicking, reduced range of motion |
| Hip Bursitis | Repetitive friction, overuse | Pain on outer hip, especially on serve |

## Hip Mobility Exercises

### Hip Flexor Mobility

**Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch:**
- Kneel on one knee, front foot flat
- Push hips forward, squeezing the glute of the kneeling leg
- 30 seconds, 3 reps each side

**Couch Stretch:**
- Kneel with one foot behind you, shin against a wall or couch
- Squeeze the glute of the kneeling leg
- 30 seconds, 3 reps each side

**Hip Flexor CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations):**
- Slowly rotate the hip through its full range of motion
- 5 rotations each direction, each leg

### Hip Internal and External Rotation

**90/90 Hip Stretch:**
- Sit with one leg in front (90 degrees) and one to the side (90 degrees)
- Lean forward over the front leg (stretches internal rotation)
- Lean back over the back leg (stretches external rotation)
- 30 seconds each position

**Seated Hip Rotation:**
- Sit with legs extended
- Rotate each leg internally and externally
- 10 reps each direction, each leg

### Hip Flexion and Extension

**Deep Squat Hold:**
- Feet shoulder-width apart, squat as deep as possible
- Hold at the bottom, maintaining an upright torso
- 3 sets of 30 seconds

**Hip Hinge (Romanian Deadlift):**
- Hold a barbell or dumbbells
- Hinge at the hips, keeping the back straight
- 3 sets of 10 reps

## Gluteal Activation and Strengthening

### Glute Activation

**Glute Bridge:**
- Lie on back, feet flat
- Lift hips, squeezing the glutes at the top
- 3 sets of 15 reps

**Single-Leg Glute Bridge:**
- Same as above but on one leg
- 3 sets of 12 reps per leg

**Clamshell:**
- Lie on the side, knees bent
- Open the top knee, keeping feet together
- 3 sets of 15 reps per side

### Glute Strengthening

**Lateral Band Walk:**
- Place a resistance band around the ankles
- Walk laterally, maintaining tension
- 3 sets of 15 steps per direction

**Single-Leg Squat:**
- Stand on one leg, lower into a squat
- Maintain knee alignment
- 3 sets of 10 reps per leg

**Step-Up:**
- Step onto a box or bench with one leg
- Drive through the heel, squeezing the glute
- 3 sets of 10 reps per leg

## Rotational Hip Exercises

**Cable Woodchop:**
- Stand sideways to a cable machine
- Pull the cable across the body with rotation from the hips
- 3 sets of 8 reps per side

**Medicine Ball Rotational Throw:**
- Stand perpendicular to a wall
- Rotate the hips and throw the ball
- 3 sets of 6 reps per side

**Rotational Lunge:**
- Lunge forward while rotating the trunk
- 3 sets of 8 reps per side

## Practical Application: "The Hip Mobility and Activation Routine"

**Pre-Training/Match (10 minutes):**
- Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch: 30 seconds each side
- 90/90 hip stretch: 30 seconds each position
- Glute bridge: 2 x 15
- Clamshell: 2 x 12 each side
- Lateral band walk: 2 x 12 steps each direction

**Dedicated Session (20 minutes, 2-3x/week):**
- Full hip mobility routine: 10 minutes
- Glute activation: 5 minutes
- Rotational exercises: 5 minutes

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Limited hip internal rotation | Capsular tightness, poor mobility | Reduced serve and groundstroke power | 90/90 stretch; hip CARs; rotational mobility |
| Hip flexor tightness | Prolonged ready position, sitting | Reduced hip extension, lower back compensation | Hip flexor stretch; couch stretch; hip flexor mobility |
| Gluteal inhibition | Prolonged sitting, poor activation | Reduced power, hamstring/lower back compensation | Glute activation exercises; glute bridge; clamshell |
| Hip pain during rotation | Labral tear, impingement | Pain, reduced range of motion | Medical assessment; hip mobility; technique modification |
| Limited lateral movement | Hip abductor weakness, poor mobility | Reduced court coverage | Lateral band walk; hip abductor strengthening; mobility work |
