# ART-579: Return Tactical Systems — Chip-and-Charge, Drive, and Block

## Executive Summary

Elite returners use three primary tactical systems: chip-and-charge, drive, and block. Each system has specific applications based on the serve quality, court surface, and match situation.

## System 1: Chip-and-Charge

### What Is Chip-and-Charge?
Chip-and-charge involves chipping the return (short, with backspin) and immediately rushing the net to volley the server's reply.

### When to Use
- Against big servers who stay back
- On fast surfaces where the ball stays low
- When you want to take time away from the server

### Execution
1. Chip the return with backspin
2. Rush the net to the service line
3. Split step as the server hits their reply
4. Volley to the open court

## System 2: Drive

### What Is Drive?
Drive involves hitting a full-swing return with power and depth, aiming to put the server on the defensive.

### When to Use
- Against slow or weak second serves
- When you are well-positioned and balanced
- When you want to take control of the point

### Execution
1. Step into the return with the drive step
2. Use a full groundstroke swing
3. Aim deep to the back third of the court
4. Target the server's weaker side

## System 3: Block

### What Is Block?
Block involves using a short, compact swing to deflect the ball back, relying on the server's pace for power.

### When to Use
- Against fast first serves (110+ mph)
- When you are off-balance
- When you need to neutralize a big server

### Execution
1. Use a minimal backswing
2. Keep the wrist firm at contact
3. Aim crosscourt for safety

## Choosing the Right System

### By Serve Type
- Fast first serve: Block
- Slow first serve: Drive
- Second serve: Drive or chip-and-charge

### By Surface
- Fast surfaces: Chip-and-charge; Block
- Medium surfaces: All systems
- Slow surfaces: Drive; Block

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Always blocking returns | No variation | Predictable returning | Drive practice; chip-and-charge drills |
| Always driving returns | Over-aggression | Errors on big serves | Block practice; controlled returning |
| Never coming to net | No chip-and-charge | Missed finishing opportunities | Chip-and-charge drills; closing practice |

## Practical Application: "The System Drill"

Practice all three systems in sequence: Block 10 fast first serves, Drive 10 second serves, Chip-and-charge 10 second serves. This develops the ability to switch between systems based on the situation.
