# ART-871: Knee and Ankle Stability Training for Tennis

## Executive Summary

The knee and ankle are the primary weight-bearing joints in tennis, subjected to repetitive loading during serving, directional changes, and court movement. Knee injuries (patellar tendinopathy, meniscal tears, patellofemoral pain) and ankle injuries (lateral sprains, Achilles tendinopathy) are among the most common tennis-related conditions. Effective knee and ankle stability training involves strengthening the muscles supporting these joints, developing proprioception, and practicing safe movement patterns that reduce injury risk.

## Knee Biomechanics in Tennis

During tennis movements, the knee experiences compression forces during jumping and landing, shear forces during directional changes, valgus stress during lateral movements, and rotational forces during pivoting. Common knee injuries include patellar tendinopathy from repetitive jumping, meniscal tears from twisting, and patellofemoral pain from poor patellar tracking.

## Ankle Biomechanics in Tennis

The ankle experiences inversion stress during lateral movements, dorsiflexion during low ready positions, plantarflexion during push-off, and rotation during pivoting. Lateral ankle sprains are the most common acute tennis injury, while Achilles tendinopathy develops from repetitive push-off.

## Knee Stability Exercises

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Single-Leg Squat | 3 x 10 per leg | Quad strength and knee control |
| Nordic Hamstring Curl | 3 x 8 | Eccentric hamstring strength |
| Lateral Band Walk | 3 x 15 steps each direction | Glute medius and knee stability |
| Terminal Knee Extension | 3 x 15 per leg | VMO strength |
| Single-Leg Glute Bridge | 3 x 12 per leg | Hip and knee stability |
| Step-Down | 3 x 10 per leg | Quad control and landing mechanics |

## Ankle Stability Exercises

| Exercise | Sets x Reps | Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Single-Leg Balance (Eyes Open) | 3 x 30 sec per leg | Static balance |
| Single-Leg Balance (Eyes Closed) | 3 x 20 sec per leg | Proprioception |
| Single-Leg Balance (Unstable Surface) | 3 x 30 sec per leg | Advanced proprioception |
| Calf Raise (Straight Knee) | 3 x 15 | Gastrocnemius strength |
| Calf Raise (Bent Knee) | 3 x 15 | Soleus strength |
| Eccentric Calf Lowering | 3 x 10 per foot | Achilles tendon resilience |
| Star Excursion Balance | 3 x 5 reaches each direction | Dynamic balance and reach |

## Practical Application: "The 15-Minute Knee and Ankle Prehab Session"

Perform 3-4 times per week:

- Single-leg squat: 2 x 8 each leg
- Nordic hamstring curl: 2 x 6
- Lateral band walk: 2 x 12 steps each direction
- Single-leg balance: 2 x 30 seconds each leg
- Calf raise: 2 x 12
- Eccentric calf lowering: 2 x 8 each foot

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Knee pain during serving | Weak quads, poor landing mechanics | Patellar tendinopathy | Quad strengthening; eccentric squat program; landing mechanics |
| Ankle sprains | Poor proprioception, weak stabilizers | Recurrent sprains | Balance training; ankle strengthening; proprioceptive exercises |
| Knee valgus during movement | Weak glute medius, poor control | Knee injuries | Lateral band walks; single-leg exercises; movement retraining |
| Achilles pain | Tight calves, insufficient eccentric strength | Achilles tendinopathy | Eccentric calf lowering; calf stretching; gradual load increase |
