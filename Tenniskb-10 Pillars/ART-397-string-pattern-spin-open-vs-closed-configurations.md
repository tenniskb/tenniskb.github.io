# ART-397: String Pattern and Spin — Open vs. Closed Configurations

## Executive Summary

String pattern—the number of main and cross strings on the racquet—significantly affects spin production in the forehand. Open string patterns (fewer strings, e.g., 16x19) allow the mains to slide and snap back during contact, creating a "trampoline effect" that increases spin. Closed string patterns (more strings, e.g., 18x20) provide more control but less spin potential. Understanding the trade-offs between open and closed patterns allows players to select the optimal setup for their playing style.

## Open String Patterns

Open string patterns have fewer strings, with larger gaps between them. Common open patterns include 16x19 and 16x18.

Advantages of open patterns:
1. More string movement: The mains can slide and snap back during contact, increasing the "trampoline effect" and spin production.
2. More spin: Open patterns produce 10 to 20 percent more spin than closed patterns for the same swing mechanics.
3. More power: The increased string deflection produces higher ball speeds.

Disadvantages of open patterns:
1. Less control: The increased string deflection reduces directional precision.
2. Faster string wear: The sliding motion causes the strings to break more quickly.
3. Less durability: Open patterns have a shorter string life.

## Closed String Patterns

Closed string patterns have more strings, with smaller gaps between them. Common closed patterns include 18x20 and 18x18.

Advantages of closed patterns:
1. More control: The reduced string deflection provides better directional precision.
2. More durability: The strings wear more slowly and last longer.
3. More consistent: The consistent string bed provides a more predictable response.

Disadvantages of closed patterns:
1. Less spin: The reduced string movement produces less spin than open patterns.
2. Less power: The reduced string deflection produces lower ball speeds.
3. Stiffer feel: The denser string bed transmits more vibration to the arm.

## The Spin Production Mechanism

The spin production mechanism in open patterns is called the "snap-back" effect:

1. During contact, the mains deflect inward and slide sideways.
2. As the ball rebounds, the mains snap back to their original position.
3. This snap-back motion adds an upward component to the ball's velocity, increasing spin.

Closed patterns have less snap-back because the strings are held more tightly by the additional cross strings.

## Selecting the Right Pattern

The choice of string pattern depends on:

1. Playing style: Aggressive topspin players benefit from open patterns. Control-oriented players benefit from closed patterns.

2. Surface: Clay-court players benefit from open patterns (more spin). Grass-court players benefit from closed patterns (more control).

3. Strength: Strong players who generate their own power benefit from closed patterns (more control). Players who rely on the racquet for power benefit from open patterns (more power).

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Low spin despite good mechanics | String pattern not optimized | Flat shots | Switch to open pattern; 16x19 |
| Strings break too quickly | Open pattern durability issue | Frequent restringing | Switch to closed pattern; or use more durable strings |
| Poor control with open pattern | Too much string deflection | Directional errors | Switch to closed pattern; or increase tension |
| Stiff feel with closed pattern | Too little string deflection | Arm vibration | Switch to open pattern; or decrease tension |
| No awareness of pattern effect | Cannot optimize equipment | Suboptimal spin production | Experiment with different patterns; assess results |

## Practical Application: "The Pattern Test"

Test different string patterns by having your current racquet strung with both an open pattern (if possible) or by testing a racquet with a different pattern. Use a spin measurement device to compare the spin rates. Select the pattern that produces the highest spin rate while maintaining acceptable control and durability.
