# ART-823: Practice Session Design and Structure

## Executive Summary

Effective practice session design maximizes training time by structuring sessions to balance technical work, tactical development, physical conditioning, and mental preparation. A well-designed session progresses from warm-up through skill acquisition, skill application, and competitive play, with clear objectives and measurable outcomes for each phase. The coach must manage intensity, variety, and engagement to maintain high-quality repetitions while preventing boredom and physical overload.

## The Practice Session Framework

### Session Components

A complete practice session includes five phases:

1. **Warm-Up (10-15% of session):** Physical activation, dynamic mobility, neural preparation
2. **Skill Acquisition (25-30% of session):** Technical development, new skill introduction
3. **Skill Application (25-30% of session):** Tactical application, game-like scenarios
4. **Competitive Play (20-25% of session):** Point play, match simulation, competition
5. **Cool-Down (5-10% of session):** Physical recovery, review, goal setting

### Session Duration Guidelines

| Level | Session Duration | Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Beginner (Ages 5-8) | 45-60 minutes | Fun, basic skills, movement |
| Intermediate (Ages 9-12) | 60-90 minutes | Technical development, tactics |
| Advanced (Ages 13-16) | 90-120 minutes | Technical refinement, match play |
| Professional | 120-180 minutes | Tactical mastery, physical peak |

## Warm-Up Phase Design

### General Warm-Up (5 minutes)

- Light jogging, skipping, or cycling
- Goal: Raise core temperature by 1-2 degrees C
- Intensity: 50-60% of maximum effort

### Dynamic Mobility (5 minutes)

- Arm circles (shoulder preparation)
- Leg swings (hip preparation)
- Thoracic rotations (spine preparation)
- Lunges with rotation (hip and trunk preparation)
- Ankle circles and calf raises (ankle preparation)

### Neural Preparation (5 minutes)

- Reaction drills (mirror drills, ball drops)
- Quick-foot ladder or agility patterns
- Progressive hitting (easy rally to full-court rally)

## Skill Acquisition Phase Design

### Technical Development

**Principles:**
- Isolate the specific technical element being developed
- Provide clear demonstration and explanation
- Use appropriate feedback frequency (frequent for beginners, intermittent for advanced)
- Progress from simple to complex

**Structure:**
1. **Demonstration:** Coach demonstrates the skill with verbal cues
2. **Shadow Practice:** Player mimics the movement without a ball
3. **Isolated Practice:** Ball machine or coach feed with focus on the specific element
4. **Semi-Isolated Practice:** Practice with a partner, focusing on the element
5. **Application:** Use the skill in a game-like context

### Tactical Development

**Principles:**
- Present the tactical problem clearly
- Allow the player to discover solutions through guided practice
- Provide context for why the tactic is important
- Progress from predictable to unpredictable situations

**Structure:**
1. **Problem Presentation:** "How do you approach the net effectively?"
2. **Guided Discovery:** Practice approach shots in controlled conditions
3. **Pattern Practice:** Execute the pattern with increasing difficulty
4. **Game Application:** Apply the tactic in point play

## Skill Application Phase Design

### Drill Design Principles

1. **Specificity:** Drills should replicate match situations as closely as possible
2. **Repetition:** Enough repetitions to build skill, not so many as to cause boredom
3. **Challenge:** Difficulty should be appropriate to the player's level (success rate of 60-80%)
4. **Feedback:** Provide clear, actionable feedback after each repetition
5. **Variation:** Vary drills to maintain engagement and develop adaptability

### Drill Types

| Drill Type | Purpose | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Technical Drill | Isolate and develop a specific technique | Crosscourt forehand rally with target zones |
| Tactical Drill | Develop tactical awareness and decision-making | Approach and volley drill with feeder |
| Movement Drill | Develop court coverage and footwork | Star drill with directional cues |
| Pressure Drill | Simulate competitive pressure | Serve on break point with consequences |
| Conditioning Drill | Develop tennis-specific fitness | 10-ball rapid feed drill |

## Competitive Play Phase Design

### Point Play

- Play full points with standard scoring
- Emphasize tactical execution over technical perfection
- Encourage competitive intensity
- Use competitive play to assess skill transfer from drills

### Match Simulation

- Simulate specific match scenarios (serving at 4-5, playing a tiebreak)
- Introduce consequences for losing (push-ups, sprints)
- Use simulation to develop mental toughness
- Practice pre-match and between-set routines

### Practice Matches

- Full practice matches with a partner
- Treat matches like competition (full warm-up, post-match review)
- Use matches to test tactical plans and technical development
- Track statistics for performance analysis

## Session Intensity Management

### The Intensity Curve

A well-designed session follows an intensity curve:

1. **Warm-Up:** Low intensity, gradually increasing
2. **Skill Acquisition:** Moderate intensity, focused on quality
3. **Skill Application:** Moderate to high intensity, game-like
4. **Competitive Play:** High intensity, match-like
5. **Cool-Down:** Low intensity, recovery

### Managing Fatigue

- Alternate high-intensity and low-intensity activities
- Monitor player signs of fatigue (reduced movement quality, increased errors, heavy breathing)
- Adjust session duration and intensity based on the player's condition
- Build in recovery periods between high-intensity blocks

## Session Planning Template

### Pre-Session Planning

**Session Objective:** What is the primary goal of this session?
**Target Skills:** Which technical/tactical elements will be developed?
**Drill Selection:** Which drills will be used to develop these skills?
**Intensity Plan:** What is the intensity progression for the session?
**Equipment Needed:** What equipment is required?

### Post-Session Review

**Objective Achievement:** Was the session objective met?
**Player Engagement:** Was the player engaged and motivated?
**Technical Progress:** Did the player show improvement in target skills?
**Adjustments Needed:** What should be changed for the next session?
**Next Session Focus:** What should be the focus of the next session?

## Practical Application: "The 90-Minute Professional Session"

Structure a 90-minute professional-level session:

**Warm-Up (15 minutes):**
- General warm-up: 5 minutes (jogging, skipping)
- Dynamic mobility: 5 minutes (arm circles, leg swings, rotations)
- Neural preparation: 5 minutes (progressive hitting from baseline)

**Skill Acquisition (25 minutes):**
- Technical focus: Serve development (10 minutes)
  - Toss consistency: 3 minutes
  - Trophy position: 3 minutes
  - Full serve practice: 4 minutes
- Tactical focus: Serve +1 patterns (15 minutes)
  - Wide serve + aggressive +1: 5 minutes
  - Body serve + drive +1: 5 minutes
  - T serve + crosscourt +1: 5 minutes

**Skill Application (25 minutes):**
- Drill: Approach and volley with feeder (10 minutes)
- Drill: Crosscourt rally with target zones (10 minutes)
- Drill: Break point simulation (5 minutes)

**Competitive Play (20 minutes):**
- Practice set with tactical emphasis (20 minutes)

**Cool-Down (5 minutes):**
- Light rally (2 minutes)
- Stretching (2 minutes)
- Brief review and goal setting (1 minute)

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Practice quality declines after 60 minutes | Session too long, insufficient intensity management | Reduced learning, increased injury risk | Shorten sessions; build in recovery breaks; alternate intensity |
| Skills don't transfer to matches | Drills too isolated, insufficient game-like practice | Good practice, poor match performance | Increase game-based activities; reduce isolated drilling |
| Player bored and disengaged | Repetitive drills, insufficient variety | Reduced motivation, dropout risk | Introduce new drills; vary activities; increase game play |
| Fatigue in later sessions of the week | Insufficient recovery between sessions | Cumulative fatigue, reduced training quality | Adjust weekly schedule; build in rest days; monitor training load |
