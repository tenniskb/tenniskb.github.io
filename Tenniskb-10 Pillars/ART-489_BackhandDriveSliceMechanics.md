# ART-489: Backhand Drills for Intermediates

## Executive Summary
Intermediate players should focus on refining their backhand technique and developing shot variety through a series of progressive drills. These drills build on the fundamentals, adding spin variation, directional control, and tactical awareness. This article provides a comprehensive set of drills for intermediate players.

## The Topspin Target Drill
The topspin target drill involves placing a target on the court and practicing hitting topspin drives to it. The target should be placed deep in the court, requiring the player to hit with heavy topspin and depth. This drill develops the ability to control the direction and depth of the topspin drive.

Intermediate players should practice this drill with both crosscourt and down-the-line targets, developing the ability to hit to different areas of the court.

## The Slice Approach Drill
The slice approach drill involves hitting a slice approach shot and then moving forward to the net. This drill develops the ability to use the slice as a transitional shot and to move forward effectively.

The player should focus on producing a low, skidding slice that lands in the service box, forcing the opponent to hit up on the ball. The drill also develops the forward movement needed for the net approach.

## The Directional Control Drill
The directional control drill involves practicing hitting the backhand to different areas of the court. The player should alternate between crosscourt and down-the-line shots, developing the ability to control the direction of the shot.

This drill also develops the ability to change the direction of the rally, which is essential for constructing points and keeping the opponent guessing.

## Common Intermediate Errors
One common error is failing to vary the shot, making the backhand predictable. Another is failing to develop a reliable slice, limiting the player's tactical options. Some intermediates also struggle with directional control, resulting in shots that go where the opponent expects.

Intermediate players who struggle with these errors often benefit from drills that emphasize shot variety and directional control.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix
| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shot predictable | Always hitting same way | Opponent anticipates | Develop shot variety; drill variation |
| Cannot produce slice | Grip too closed or swing path wrong | Weak slice | Check grip; drill high-to-low path |
| Cannot control direction | Always hitting crosscourt | Predictable shots | Develop directional control; drill to targets |
| Cannot approach | Slice too weak or too high | Caught in no-man's land | Develop slice approach; drill transition |
| Cannot change direction | Always hitting same way | Predictable rally | Practice direction change; drill variation |

## Practical Application: "The Intermediate's Routine"
Intermediate players should practice the topspin target drill, slice approach drill, and directional control drill regularly, focusing on shot variety and directional control. Track progress over sessions to ensure the skills are developing correctly. As the player improves, they can add more advanced drills to their routine.
