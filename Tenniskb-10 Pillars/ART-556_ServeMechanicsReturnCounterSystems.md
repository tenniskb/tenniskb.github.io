# ART-556: Return Positioning: Standing Deep, at the Baseline, or Inside
## Executive Summary

Where you stand to return serve significantly affects your ability to handle different serve types and speeds. This article covers the three primary return positions — deep, at the baseline, and inside the baseline — and when each is optimal.

## The Three Return Positions

### Deep (6+ Feet Behind the Baseline)
**Advantages:**
- More time to react to the serve
- Better for handling fast flat servers
- Can handle high-bouncing kick servers
- More time to set up for the return

**Disadvantages:**
- The ball drops lower after the long bounce
- Must hit up more to clear the net
- Gives the server more court to target
- Difficult to attack short serves

**Best for:**
- Returning big servers (120+ mph serves)
- Returning kick servers on clay
- When you are struggling with timing
- When you want to play defensively

### At the Baseline (On or Just Behind)
**Advantages:**
- Optimal balance of time and positioning
- Can handle most serve types effectively
- Good angle into the service box
- Can attack or defend as needed

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires good timing and reflexes
- May struggle with very fast serves
- Must adjust for high-bouncing kick serves

**Best for:**
- All-purpose returning
- When you are confident in your timing
- Against servers with moderate pace (90-110 mph)
- When you want to mix aggression and defense

### Inside the Baseline (1-3 Feet In)
**Advantages:**
- Takes time away from the server
- Can attack short or weak serves
- Better angle into the service box
- Puts pressure on the server

**Disadvantages:**
- Less time to react to fast serves
- Vulnerable to big flat servers
- Requires excellent anticipation and reflexes
- Difficult to handle high-bouncing kick serves

**Best for:**
- Returning second serves (slower pace)
- Against servers with weaker serves
- When you want to be aggressive
- On fast surfaces where the ball stays low

## Positioning by Serve Type

### Against Fast Flat Servers (120+ mph)
- Stand deep (6-10 feet behind baseline)
- Use a block or short swing
- Focus on getting the ball back in play
- Do not try to attack big serves

### Against Moderate Servers (90-110 mph)
- Stand at the baseline
- Use a normal swing
- Can attack or defend as needed
- Mix up your return strategy

### Against Slow/Weak Servers (below 90 mph)
- Stand inside the baseline
- Attack every return
- Step into the return
- Drive the ball aggressively

### Against Kick Servers
- Stand deep on clay (8-12 feet behind)
- Stand at baseline on hard courts
- Be ready to handle high bounces
- Use topspin returns

### Against Slice Servers
- Stand at the baseline or slightly inside
- Adjust laterally for the curve
- Stay low for the low bounce
- Block or drive depending on serve speed

## Adjusting Positioning During a Match

### If You Are Struggling
- Move back to give yourself more time
- Focus on getting the ball back in play
- Do not worry about attacking — just neutralize the serve

### If You Are Comfortable
- Move forward to take time away from the server
- Attack weaker serves
- Put pressure on the server with aggressive returns

### If the Server Changes Strategy
- Pay attention to the server's patterns
- Adjust your positioning based on what is working
- Communicate with your coach (if applicable) about what you are seeing

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Late on fast serves | Standing too far forward | Mishits and errors | Move deeper; practice deep returning |
| Cannot attack weak serves | Standing too deep | Missed opportunities | Move inside baseline; practice attacking |
| Struggling with kick serves | Incorrect positioning | Difficult returns | Adjust depth based on surface and serve type |
| Consistently in the same spot | No positional adjustment | Predictable returning | Practice varying positioning; adapt to server |
| Returns lack depth | Poor positioning | Short returns returnable | Move closer to baseline; step into returns |

## Practical Application: "The Three-Position Drill"

Have a partner serve 10 balls while you stand deep, 10 while you stand at the baseline, and 10 while you stand inside. Note which position produces the best results against that particular server. Use this information to determine your default return position for that opponent. Repeat this drill with different servers to develop your ability to adjust positioning.
