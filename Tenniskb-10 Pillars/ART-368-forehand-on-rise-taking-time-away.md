# ART-368: The Forehand on the Rise — Taking Time Away

## Executive Summary

The forehand on the rise is a shot taken as the ball ascends after its bounce, before it reaches the apex of its trajectory. By contacting the ball early in its flight, the player takes time away from the opponent, compressing their decision window and forcing defensive responses. The forehand on the rise is one of the most aggressive shots in modern tennis, used by elite players to dictate play and keep opponents on the defensive. Understanding the mechanics and timing of the on-the-rise forehand allows players to incorporate this weapon into their game.

## The Mechanics of the On-the-Rise Forehand

The on-the-rise forehand requires:

1. Early positioning: The player must position themselves inside the baseline to meet the ball before it reaches the apex.

2. Modified swing path: The swing path is less steep than the standard forehand because the ball is still ascending, requiring less upward brush to redirect it over the net.

3. Earlier contact point: The contact point is further in front of the body (0.5 to 0.8m) to meet the ball early.

4. Reduced backswing: The backswing is abbreviated because there is less time to prepare.

5. Rapid forward swing: The forward swing is faster to ensure the racquet head peaks at the moment the ball is at the contact point.

## Tactical Advantages

The on-the-rise forehand offers three tactical advantages:

1. Takes time away from the opponent: By contacting the ball early, the player reduces the opponent's reaction time by 0.1 to 0.2 seconds.

2. Compresses the opponent's decision window: The opponent has less time to read the shot and select a response.

3. Forces defensive responses: The early contact often produces a flatter, faster shot that the opponent must handle defensively.

## When to Use the On-the-Rise Forehand

The on-the-rise forehand is most effective when:

1. The player is positioned inside the baseline, with time to set up.

2. The ball is at waist height or below at contact (the most common height for on-the-rise shots).

3. The player wants to take time away from the opponent and dictate play.

4. The opponent is positioned deep behind the baseline, giving the player space to hit into.

## Risks and Considerations

The on-the-rise forehand carries risks:

1. Timing difficulty: Contacting the ball on the rise requires precise timing, and errors are more frequent.

2. Reduced topspin: The less steep swing path produces less topspin, reducing the margin for error over the net.

3. Higher error rate: The combination of early timing and reduced topspin produces a higher error rate than the standard forehand.

To minimize these risks, players should:

1. Practice the timing extensively to develop consistency.

2. Use the on-the-rise forehand selectively, not on every ball.

3. Accept a higher error rate in exchange for the tactical advantage.

## Training the On-the-Rise Forehand

Training involves:

1. Timing drills: Practice hitting balls on the rise with a partner feeding at varying speeds.

2. Swing path drills: Practice the modified swing path for on-the-rise shots.

3. Tactical drills: Practice using the on-the-rise forehand in match situations.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Contacting ball too late (after apex) | Standard forehand; no time advantage | Reduced tactical impact | Move further inside baseline; take ball earlier |
| Swing path too steep | Excessive topspin; slower ball | Reduced pace; less time advantage | Flatten swing path; reduce low-to-high angle |
| Backswing not abbreviated | Late preparation; rushed shot | Mishits; inconsistent contact | Shorten backswing; prepare earlier |
| Poor timing on rise | Mishits; high error rate | Unforced errors | Practice timing drills; develop consistency |
| Using on-rise on every shot | Predictable; high error rate | Vulnerable to anticipation | Use selectively; vary shot selection |

## Practical Application: "The Rise and Rip"

Practice the on-the-rise forehand by having a partner feed balls that bounce at the service line. Position yourself inside the baseline and practice taking each ball as it rises, focusing on the early contact and the feeling of taking time away from an imaginary opponent. Start with slow feeds and progress to faster, more realistic speeds.
