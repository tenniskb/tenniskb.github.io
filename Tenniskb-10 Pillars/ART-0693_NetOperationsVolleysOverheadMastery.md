# ART-0693: The Volley Against the Return with Wide Angle — Handling Wide Returns

## Executive Summary

Returns hit at extreme angles pull the net player wide, reducing their ability to control the volley and recover to the center. When you are the net player facing a wide-angle return, you must use the cross-step for maximum reach, contact the ball in front of your body, and recover quickly to the center. Wide-angle returns are a standard tactic to stretch the net player and open the court.

## The Wide-Angle Return

The wide-angle return: (1) is hit at an extreme angle, (2) pulls the net player wide of the court, (3) is hit with slice (to stay low) or topspin (to kick wide). At the net, the wide-angle return forces lateral movement and a wide volley, reducing control and recovery time.

## Technique for Handling Wide-Angle Returns

Against wide-angle returns: (1) use the cross-step to move laterally, (2) contact the ball in front of your body, (3) use the block volley (absorb pace, redirect), (4) aim deep to the open court, (5) recover to the center with the crossover step. The key is quick lateral movement and a compact volley.

## Common Errors

Not using the cross-step (the shuffle step cannot cover enough ground). Reaching instead of moving the feet. Breaking the wrist on the wide volley. Not recovering to the center after the shot.

## Summary

Wide-angle returns demand the cross-step, block volley, and quick recovery. Move laterally, block the ball, and recover to the center.

## Diagnostic & Training Matrix

| Observation | Failure | Result | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cannot reach wide returns | Using shuffle instead of cross-step | Missed volleys | Drill cross-step for lateral movement |
| Wide volley lacks control | Reaching instead of moving feet | Erratic direction | Move feet first, then contact |
| No recovery after wide volley | Standing and watching | Court left open | Practice crossover recovery steps |
| Wrist breaks on wide volley | Grip too soft | Erratic direction | Firm wrist, increase grip pressure |

## Practical Application: "Cross-Step, Block, Recover"

Against wide-angle returns: (1) cross-step to reach the ball, (2) block the volley, (3) crossover step to recover. Quick lateral movement and recovery are the keys.
