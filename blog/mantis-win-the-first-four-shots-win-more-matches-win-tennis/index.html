Win the First Four Shots. Win More Matches. Win tennis!

By Mantis Sport Technical Team · Technical & Gear Guides · Original Article ↗

Think long rallies decide who can win a tennis match? Think again.

Around seven out of every 10 points in professional tennis are over within the first four shots. That means the serve, return and the very next shot are where most matches are actually won and lost.

It's a simple statistic that changes how you should think about the game.

Tennis is a game of short rallies

When rally length data was first analysed at Grand Slam level, one trend stood out immediately.

Most points don't become long exchanges.

They finish quickly.

Rally Length Percentage of Points
0–4 shots 70.4%
5–8 shots 20.1%
9+ shots 9.5%

What's remarkable is how little this has changed over the past decade. Despite faster rackets, stronger athletes and evolving tactics, around 70% of points are still decided within the first four shots.

That's a statistic every player should pay attention to.

Why this matters if you want to win at tennis

Many players spend the majority of practice sessions rallying cross-court, chasing consistency and trying to build longer exchanges.

Those skills absolutely matter.

But they're only part of the picture.

If nearly three-quarters of all points finish before either player has hit more than two balls, your serve and return deserve just as much attention as your groundstrokes.

For the server, those first four shots are simply:

  • Your serve
  • Your first shot after the serve (Serve +1)

For the returner:

  • Your return
  • Your first shot after the return (Return +1)

That's where matches are won.

Three lessons every player can use

1. Make your first two shots count

In short rallies, mistakes are costly.

Around 80% of 0–4 shot points end with an error, making consistency in your opening shots incredibly valuable.

You don't always need to hit bigger.

You need to hit smarter.

A well-placed serve followed by a controlled first groundstroke will win far more points than chasing outright winners.

Mantis Tip: Focus on making your first two shots reliable before trying to make them spectacular.

2. Practise what actually happens in matches

If 70% of points finish within four shots, your practice should reflect that.

Spend more time working on:

  • First serves and second serves
  • Return of serve
  • Serve +1 patterns
  • Return +1 patterns
  • Target serving under pressure

The return of serve is often one of the least-practised shots in club tennis, despite being one of the most important.

Training should mirror match play.

3. The most common rally lasts... one shot

Ask most players how long the average rally lasts and they'll probably guess four or five shots.

Even Novak Djokovic and Andy Murray estimated four when asked.

The real answer?

One shot.

Around 30% of all points finish immediately after the serve, usually because of a return error or an ace.

That single statistic highlights just how important the quality of your serve—and your return—really is.

What this means for your game

Winning more matches isn't always about producing longer rallies or hitting harder from the baseline.

It's about taking control earlier.

Develop a repeatable service action.

Learn to hit your targets consistently.

Return deep through the middle to reduce your opponent's attacking options.

Then build confidence in your first shot after the serve or return.

Master those opening moments of the point and you'll put yourself in a winning position far more often.

Key Takeaways

  • Around 70% of all points finish within the first four shots.
  • Your serve, return and first groundstroke are the most important shots in tennis.
  • Practise serve, return, Serve +1 and Return +1 as often as you practise baseline rallies.
  • Consistency in your opening shots wins more points than chasing outright winners.
  • The player who controls the first four shots usually controls the match.

The next time you watch professional tennis, don't just admire the long rallies. And for a deeper dive into the business of the sport try Racket Business

Watch who dominates the first four shots.

More often than not, they'll be the player walking off court with the win.
