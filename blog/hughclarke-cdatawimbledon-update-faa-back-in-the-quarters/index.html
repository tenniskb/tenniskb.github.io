Wimbledon Update: FAA Back in the Quarters

By Hugh Clarke · A Thread of Order · Original Article ↗

Felix Auger Aliassime came through a rollercoaster five-setter against Alejandro Davidovich Fokina 6/7(4) 7/6(6) 6/3 6/7(2) 6/1 to reach his first quarterfinal at Wimbledon since 2021.

The serve and forehand have been firing for the Canadian — the usual backbone of his game — and so far he has only been broken once the entire tournament: when serving for the match at 5-3 in the fourth-set yesterday.

It’s still the weaker wing for the Canadian, but at times it held up quite well against ADF’s flat and compact left-side. Recall the crucial set point in the second set tie-breaker:

On grass, however, players with a weaker topspin backhand often navigate Wimbledon by possessing an elite slice. Berrettini, Dimitrov, Musetti and even Federer all leaned heavily on it.

But in this department I think there is room for improvement with the Canadian.

FAA’s typical slice backswing is low and abbreviated. It works for low and short balls, but it lacks the racquet head speed and high-to-low swing path necessary to tame more difficult incoming balls with any real authority. Compare FAA with the slice backhand speed and spin leaders in Dimitrov and Evans:

Notice how Dimitrov and Evans get the hitting hand pulled higher. They create a much better stretch in the hitting arm/shoulder muscles, and have more length to accelerate the racquet head, as well as the option to still hit down on higher and rising balls. Getting the hitting hand above the ball and leaning over it creates something akin to “compression” (if you are a golfer), as all great slicers do. I think FAA is often lacking these elements, resulting in his slice floating. The best ones flirt with the net tape before knifing low into your opponent’s ankles.
