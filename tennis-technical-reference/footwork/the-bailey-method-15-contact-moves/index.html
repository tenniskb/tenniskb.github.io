The Bailey Method: The 15 Contact Moves of Modern Tennis

← Back to all chapters in Footwork & Movement

Author: David Bailey · Source: Bailey Tennis Footwork

Introduction to the David Bailey Tennis Footwork Method

The Bailey Tennis Footwork System is universally recognized as the definitive taxonomy of modern tennis movement. Developed by legendary footwork coach David Bailey after analyzing over 20,000 hours of professional tournament footage, the system classifies every movement on the tennis court into 15 Distinct Contact Moves across three tactical situations: 5 Rallying Contacts, 4 Attacking Contacts, and 6 Defensive Contacts.

1. The 5 Rallying Contact Moves

Rallying moves are executed from the baseline when establishing court position, maintaining depth, and controlling baseline exchanges:

  • 1. The 1-Foot Pivot (Neutral/Semi-Open): The fundamental anchor move where the player loads the outside leg and pivots cleanly around the hip axis during unhurried baseline rallies.
  • 2. The 2-Foot Pivot (Closed/Square Stance): Traditional linear transfer stepping into the court with the front foot before transferring weight across the hitting plane.
  • 3. The Hop Move: Used when striking aggressive baseline topspin, where vertical kinetic drive lifts the athlete off the loaded outside foot with synchronous scissoring recovery.
  • 4. The Transfer Step (Step-Down): Driving linearly through the contact point, common in aggressive crosscourt forehand rallies.
  • 5. The Balance Stance Rally: Wide stationary platform used for heavy topspin exchanges when retrieving balls at waist height.

2. The 4 Attacking / Approach Contact Moves

Attacking contact moves are triggered when taking short balls inside the baseline or transitioning toward the net:

  • 6. The Front Hop: Explosive airborne contact landing on the front foot inside the court to take time away from the defender.
  • 7. The Forward Pivot: Smooth directional transition around the front leg while flowing seamlessly into the net position.
  • 8. The Drive-Volley Step: Airborne swing volley takeoff designed to crush floating mid-court balls before they drop.
  • 9. The Step-In Cut: Aggressive inside-out angle cut off short balls, redirecting the ball behind the recovering opponent.

3. The 6 Defensive / Out-of-Position Contact Moves

Defensive moves allow elite athletes like Novak Djokovic, Carlos Alcaraz, and Rafael Nadal to retrieve balls from outside the doubles alley and recover back to the center of the court instantly:

  • 10. The 1-Foot Spin (Spin Recovery): High-speed dynamic spin around the outside foot following a deep wide recovery.
  • 11. The Open Stance Slide: Controlled hard court or clay court slide decelerating through the outside foot while maintaining an upright spine.
  • 12. The Mogul Move: Double-leg absorption jump used when pushed deep and wide behind the baseline, maintaining upper body posture.
  • 13. The Drop-Step (Crossover Step-Back): Rapid retreat move for deep high-bouncing balls driven into the corners.
  • 14. The Reverse Pivot: Loading the outside leg on extreme stretch and using hip internal rotation to brake and redirect body momentum back toward the baseline center.
  • 15. The Emergency Stretch Poke: Low-center-of-gravity lunge used for retrieving drop shots or passing balls on full extension.
